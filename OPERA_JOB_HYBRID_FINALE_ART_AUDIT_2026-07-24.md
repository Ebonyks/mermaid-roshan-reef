## Evaluation dimensions

Each key was reviewed for:

1. silhouette and phone-size readability;
2. consistency with the accepted Opera, Roshan, outfit, and boss families;
3. palette and line/shadow language;
4. mechanic legibility;
5. logical object placement and stage continuity;
6. modelability as rounded low-cost 3D;
7. exact count, order, mapping, species, and safety locks; and
8. nonpunitive child-appropriate boss presentation.

## Family consistency

The group uses one shared visual grammar:

- formal rounded proscenium architecture;
- broad uncluttered gameplay decks;
- coral, teal, cream, and plum color families;
- navy-purple outlines;
- aqua and lavender shadow planes;
- restrained gold, pearl, shell, and footlight accents;
- flat illustrated surfaces rather than realistic material noise;
- large props with simple modelable silhouettes; and
- one Roshan and one recurring boss per composition.

Floor identity remains visible through lighting and boss choice without
breaking the family:

- Floor 1 is warm coral and lagoon light with the Curtain Dragon.
- Floor 2 is moonlit aqua/lavender with the Shadow Phantom.
- Floor 3 is midnight jewel color with the Midnight Maestro.

## Rejected iterations

- Pastry Chef v1 scored 4.2 because the montage duplicated Roshan and the

- Painter v1 scored 4.3 because foreground overlap obscured the binding
  plum/coral/cream station order.

Only corrected passing images were added.

## 3D translation cautions

- Treat each image as composition guidance, not a single baked gameplay card.
- Rebuild proscenium, deck, scenic flats, props, and boss anchors separately.
- Keep the interaction plane open; decorative props must not create hidden
  collision.
- Simplify painted folds and ornament into broad shapes.
- Use opaque geometry and baked lighting wherever possible.
- Do not reproduce painterly sparkle clusters as dense particles.
- Do not introduce a boss health bar merely because the image is called a
  finale.
