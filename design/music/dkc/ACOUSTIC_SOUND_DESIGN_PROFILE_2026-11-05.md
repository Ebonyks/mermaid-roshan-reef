# DKC acoustic sound design profile

The DKC album is finding its identity in a small, tactile band: recognizable game melodies, sharply spoken wind phrases, warm struck metal, lively wooden mallets, a clear bass line, and interlocking hand percussion. Orchestration can change substantially while the melodic shape, rhythmic landmarks, harmonic tension and return points remain recognizable. This profile develops the owner’s acoustic direction from the current drafts and applies it to the remaining album. It is also a reference for the Mermaid Roshan sound palette; these DKC adaptations are a separate production study and are not replacement runtime music.

## Reference performances

Use the exact files in `acoustic_album_reference_v1.json`. Simian Segue V4, DK Island Swing V6, and Aquatic Ambiance V5r3 form the current reference set. The older V5 Island file has the rejected percussion-only opening; the V6 edit starts with the bass and groove. Positive owner feedback establishes a developing direction, with final approval still open.

**Simian Segue** demonstrates a conversational ensemble. A Djafe flute carries the principal line with selected recorded Hit articulations; a distinctly toned Latin pan flute answers on the separate source melody. Phrase accents matter more than constant maximum velocity. The source echo stays behind the two players. Guitar and acoustic piano give the tune body without occupying every gap.

**DK Island Swing** supplies the party end of the album. The bass makes the beat intelligible immediately; hand drums and small percussion sustain the dance. Independent wind players preserve the original overlaps without forcing several lines through a monophonic patch. Contrast comes from changes of register, texture and foreground ownership. This is the most extroverted reference; its density should not become the default for every location.

**Aquatic Ambiance** supplies the warm, patient end. Within the first source-led cycle, the selected guide’s 451 principal notes remain intact: 299 steel-pan notes and 152 softer handpan notes. Whole phrases pass between those colors. Quiet reflected notes sit behind the principal statement, while a brighter Balafon 2 part answers the melody in the later passage. The handpan is a locally prepared acoustic sampled instrument; the wood voice is a balafon, not an identified marimba. Upper-metal resonance and the balance at handoffs remain listening priorities.

Fresh advisory excerpts accompany this profile. They describe useful impressions of pulse and edge, but sometimes misidentify acoustic sounds as synths or name nonexistent instruments. Instrument identity comes from the native state and source record. Acoustic provenance does not prove a convincing performance: repeated articulations, unnatural gates or overly uniform dynamics can still sound synthetic, so those impressions remain useful listening questions. Their precise frequency suggestions are hypotheses for local checking, not measurements or acceptance grades.

## Preserve the musical identity

The direct game capture is the first reference for what the original actually does. The ROM trace and extracted MIDI explain voices, notes, timing, controllers and loops. Earlier native drafts provide a comparison for how an interpretation has evolved. Commercial MIDI files and General MIDI labels remain secondary aids.

Keep the hook’s ordered intervals, phrase rhythm, rests, harmonic arrivals, bass relationship and distinctive transitions. A source sample’s register may need translation to a physical acoustic instrument. Declare a consistent octave choice for a phrase or part and compare it by ear. Never fold individual notes into a convenient range: that can retain pitch classes while destroying the tune. If the chosen instrument cannot carry the line, change the instrument or divide coherent phrases between players.

Separate a true second melodic voice from a reflected copy. Give the former its own player and breathing plan; give the latter distance and lower level. Recover source note gates before adding expression. A preset keyboard can include ghost notes, prerecorded riffs and octave-transposing notation maps. Test isolated notes at range boundaries and compare actual sounding register with the game; RMS activity may be a previous release or an unintended gesture. The Mines low-string repair, Forest upper-motif reassignment and Funky octave correction are concrete examples. A note in one player’s line must never truncate another player’s gate. Do not send unrelated voices through one shared bend stream. Retain structural slides and deliberate bends, and identify any removed small source pitch fluctuation rather than silently substituting automatic vibrato.

The arrangement may add restrained swing, a pickup, an answering fill or a different accompaniment voicing. Keep the first clear statement close to the source. Put the most adventurous response on a later return, then bring the recognizable theme back. Preserve a readable beat at the entrance and a complete phrase at the ending. An editorial preview splice must never masquerade as a musical transition.

## Acoustic palette and performance

Electronic oscillators, synth pads, electronic keys and electronic drums are excluded from this album direction. Sampled acoustic instruments are usable when their actual loaded layers and articulations are verified. A SamplePlayer label alone does not establish an acoustic source. Chromatic handpan parts describe a studio realization from retuned acoustic recordings or multiple tuned pans; they are not a claim that one fixed-scale handpan can play every required note. The owner specifically requested UVI Soul Bass for Funky’s Fugue: its sampled electric-string sound is a named exception to a strictly unplugged ensemble; its synth layer stays off. This exception does not expand to electric keys or general synth bass.

Our chosen transfer principles are compact arrangements, audible playing gestures and direct melodic recognition. These are production interpretations, not claims that his recordings use our samples or techniques. His live recording method differs from our sampled performance construction.

## Attack and envelope guidance

The current Djafe sustain state stores attack 0, decay 0, sustain 1 and release approximately 0.08, with the global LFOs bypassed and their depths at zero. The call sheets retain exact saved envelope and LFO controls for all selected parts; those controls are distinct from measured sample-waveform timing.

A zero attack control cannot remove a slow onset already recorded in a sustain sample. For an exposed phrase head, use a real tongued or Hit articulation, align its sounding attack with the musical onset, and let the sustain body continue behind it. Begin with a short 1–3 ms edge fade after trimming unused sample lead-in. Compare the actual first 30–100 ms against the game’s dry voice and the previous successful draft. Do not add a click to make an otherwise wrong flute seem forceful.

Wind phrase heads may be more accented than passing notes; repeated figures need several levels of stress. Use a separate Hit layer only at intentional accents and keep its body subordinate to the sustained player. A provisional 60–100 ms release can prevent choking while leaving a clear next attack. Source gates and audible breathing override generic envelope numbers. Use mild natural pitch movement when appropriate; disable unintended automatic pitch LFO, tremolo and panning before deciding whether the sample itself is unstable.

Wooden mallets should have a firm strike and a resonant but controlled tail. Balafon 2 is the present brighter candidate. Start with approximately 50–280 ms note gates, then assess whether the recorded release needs a longer opening. Avoid heavy damping and low-pass processing that make the wood flat and dead. For metal, preserve the tactile impact while controlling the particular ringing band; different registers need different treatment.

## Groove and space

Anchor bass and the principal low drum near the center. Place answering players modestly to either side, typically within 10–25 percent of center; separation also needs rests, register and tone. Small early reflections suggest a shared room. Long tails belong to particular atmospheric moments and must recede before dense attacks. Do not make stereo width essential to hearing the melody.

Keep a few consistent rhythmic anchors. Human timing is selective: an accompaniment may lean slightly behind the bass or favor a swung subdivision, while the lead’s recognizable rhythm stays intact. Avoid independent random jitter on every layer. A provisional 52–56 percent subdivision is an audition range for suitable accompaniment, not a mandatory ratio and not a historical claim about DKC. Hear each choice against the source and straight control.

One main foreground line and one answering role are usually enough. When two themes genuinely overlap in the source, keep both readable. Otherwise the second player waits, holds a supporting note, or yields by several decibels. Fills should lead into an arrival rather than obscure it. A percussion-only opening is allowed only if it establishes the beat immediately and earns its duration; Island’s rejected opening is a standing counterexample.

## Mixing and mastering

Solve overlapping arrangement roles before EQ. Compare a warm lead with an unchanged control at matched listening level. For Aquatic, prior useful treatment included broad metal-resonance reduction around 2.45–3.65 kHz and a low-pass around 2.8–3.3 kHz. Those settings are evidence for that instrument in that cue, not an album-wide preset. Wind and mallet attacks must remain audible after any warmth treatment.

Bass and low drum share a stable foundation; acoustic guitar and piano should give that range room. Keep rhythm close enough to support the lead, especially in Aquatic. Use short, smooth gain moves for handoffs. Reduce a competing answer before adding presence to an already bright principal instrument. Check repeated high notes, not only average spectral balance.

Private album masters begin around -18 LUFS integrated for active pieces and -19 LUFS for atmospheric pieces, with at least 2 dB true-peak headroom. Preserve contrast when a loudness target would overwork the attacks. Verify lossless and listening encodes separately, complete beginnings and endings, finite samples, clipping, mono, and stem-sum reproduction. Game delivery has its own stricter OGG, loop, voice-ducking and device requirements under DL-SND-07 through DL-SND-17. These album masters do not supersede them.

## Applying the identity to Mermaid Roshan

Transfer the band’s phrasing, texture and arrangement habits into Roshan’s approved themes. The DKC adaptation album remains a separate study. A warm low end, a crisp acoustic lead, an answering player and a few purposeful percussion roles can unify new Roshan music without making every scene share the same instrumentation.

| Game role | Transfer from the album |
|---|---|
| Water and open space | Aquatic’s patient entrances and two metal colors, with restrained upper resonance and space for the existing melodic hook. |
| Travel and active play | Island’s immediately readable bass/pulse relationship and interlocking hand drums, scaled to the activity’s energy. |
| Intimate rooms | Simian’s two-player conversation with a smaller accompaniment, real breathing gaps and short acoustic chord responses. |
| Work and rhythmic activities | Fear’s repeating wood/percussion mechanism as an arranging technique; choose a welcoming emotional character appropriate to Roshan. |
| Rewards and transitions | Brief, recognizable acoustic cadences from the same band family, with clear entries and releases. |

Family dialogue remains the foreground when it plays. Source-palette choices do not change music ownership, cue restoration, voice ducking, touch response or the game’s no-fail character. Full album forms and runtime loops are different exports; the existing runtime rules, exact theme review and device listening still govern integration.

## Review sequence for every remaining track

1. Compare the original dry voices, full direct capture, extracted guide and previous draft. Write the hook, bass, rhythm and section landmarks before orchestration.
2. Record actual performer profile revisions, native program/layers, articulation, range, roles, rests, handoffs and declared overdubs. An installed preset name is not a playable-range test.
3. Print a close source-led control. Verify ordered pitches and intervals, onsets, source gates and meaningful bends per independent voice. Document coherent transpositions and all omissions.
4. Make the acoustic performance pass: articulate phrase starts, vary stress, clarify bass/drum placement, and preserve the source’s silences.
5. Study lead/answer overlaps and busy transitions at normal speed, soloed and in mono. Compare old and new versions at matched level.
6. Refine timbre and envelopes. Correct sample choice before aggressive processing; retain both viable alternatives when the difference is artistically valuable.
7. Develop the full song with complete cycles, purposeful returns and a resolved ending. Mark repeated printed performances honestly; a duplicated loop is not a second performed take.
8. Mix for depth, warmth and intelligibility; inspect exposed and dense sections separately. Check small-speaker translation and album neighbors.
9. Master and validate the actual delivered files. Reopen arrangement or mixing if the master reveals a musical problem.
10. Record the remaining owner comments and the exact version approved. Keep machine checks, advisory AI listening, owner acceptance and device/runtime acceptance separate.

Each pass must address a named musical or technical question and retain its result. Ten checks are not ten creative revisions. Do not count identical exports or routine validation as artistic iteration. The current private production batch begins with Mines, Funky and Fear Factory; the album board tracks all 30 original review entries and the source gaps that still require captures.
