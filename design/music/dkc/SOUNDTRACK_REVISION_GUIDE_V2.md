# DKC soundtrack revision guide

Revision 2 · Owner direction dated November 2, 2026 · Complete written outline for the original SNES soundtrack study

The revision should sound like a band discovering new life inside Donkey Kong Country: recognizable melodies, purposeful bass lines, vivid instrumental contrasts and players who listen and answer. Preserve the original in-game instrument identity first. The cast supplies phrasing, rhythmic conversation and arranging imagination; add a voice or expand a capability whenever the source requires it.

The project has two linked layers. The first identifies each cue’s theme, melodic gesture, instrumental roles, harmonic foundation, tone and defining contrasts. The second applies the master-audit collective to those materials, with specific entrances, handoffs, rests, articulation and groove choices. All 30 entries have a working direction below, including the atmospheric pieces and miniature event cues. A sparse soundscape does not need a new tune, and a two-second gesture does not need a verse and chorus.

The readings are adopted, revisable interpretations supported by the [original-audio and MIDI review](INTERPRETIVE_REVIEW.md). The A, B and return stages below are proposed arrangement plans, not verified bar-number transcriptions of the source. First establish the source phrase in the working session, then fit the proposed development to its actual length. The [structured call sheets](revision_call_sheets_v2.json) retain exact performer revisions/hashes, source links, program candidates, physical limits and review targets.

## Project outline

1. **Establish the source identity.** Use the selected original recording, selected MIDI where useful, and the event/control logs. Write the hook, its response, bass contour and harmonic arrivals before decorating them. Preserve meaningful sampled or synthetic character even when an acoustic substitute is attractive.
2. **Build a small, legible band around it.** Assign one foreground and one groove leader per section. Let support change function or rest. Most passages need three to six audible roles, not all fifteen capabilities at once.
3. **Make the performance lively.** Author touch, damping, breath, accent, anticipation and response. Allow a little swing or unevenness where it strengthens the phrase. Keep important ensemble arrivals coherent.
4. **Develop the return.** Make the first statement clear; thin or redistribute the contrasting passage; bring the tune back with a changed touch, register, response or dynamic destination. More activity is only one way to create lift.
5. **Compare the actual result.** Test the dry hook and low part before the full mix. Compare straight and expressive versions at equal loudness, and retain the version that improves movement while preserving identity. Document the chosen liberty and what would make us revise it.

This is a separate DKC reinterpretation study. It does not replace Roshan’s approved melodies or commission game-over/failure behavior. Written completion, native construction readiness, rendered musical acceptance and any later game delivery remain separate claims.

## What can change and what should remain recognizable

The melodic contour, characteristic pickups, bass/harmonic destinations, central instrumental identity and major sectional contrasts anchor each piece. Those anchors can be performed with different note weight, breath, release, voicing or response. A guitar can answer the flute; an offbeat bass pickup can prepare a cadence; a returning mallet phrase can be lighter at the start and firmer at its destination.

Mild liberties include a short response window, a passing bass connection consistent with the harmony, a changed accompaniment inversion, a few differentiated ghost strokes, and a newly shaped return. Keep the complete original hook audible before a substantial new response. A new solo, reharmonization, tempo/meter change, rewritten hook or wholesale replacement of a synth texture is a larger alternate arrangement and should be labelled as such rather than slipped into this revision.

If the cast and the source conflict, the source wins. A clarinet-like answer does not become flute because the wind member has a flute test. An electric-guitar chop does not become Sunbird because that program is convenient. An evolving synth is not a static pad, a synthetic bass is not automatically upright bass, and a marimba-like attack is not automatically steel pan. The roster is expandable, not a set of mandatory substitutions.

## Groove and expressive timing

Use five approaches, chosen cue by cue. The numeric settings are starting auditions for our version, not measurements of the originals or definitions of traditional styles.

| Approach | Starting performance plan | Best uses |
|---|---|---|
| G0 Floating phrase | Preserve a clear phrase destination while shaping swells, held-note motion, breath and tails. No compulsory drum grid or swing. | Aquatic, Temple, Misty, quiet ending cues and the opening of Northern. |
| G1 Interlocking pulse | A stable foundation with complementary bass, pluck, drum or shaker cells. Let rests, anticipations and different note lengths create motion. | Island, Cave, Bonus Room, Life in the Mines, forest, treetop and ice. |
| G2 Light lilt | Compare straight against roughly 53–57% first-half duration in the selected paired subdivision; 50% is even. Choose eighths or sixteenths explicitly and leave other parts independent. | Simian, Funky, Credits and selected character replies. |
| G3 Firm engine | Keep main riff, principal bass and major kit anchors coherent. Add elasticity to one secondary layer or a phrase-ending answer. | Mine Cart, Bad Boss, Fear Factory and the later Galleon passage. |
| G4 Compact event | Preserve pitch order, shared arrival and useful duration. Shape attack weight and release; avoid a long groove template. | Fanfares, outcomes, reveals and extra-life gestures. |

Start from authored accents, not randomization. For a quiet connecting stroke, try 15–25 velocity units below its main stroke, then adjust for the actual library response. A returning main phrase might vary by 4–8 velocity units because its destination changes, not because every note needs noise. Translate those numbers through the sample’s audible dynamic layers; equal velocity differences do not imply equal loudness differences.

For an expressive secondary part, test a phrase-consistent 5–12 ms placement difference before scattering note offsets. A reply can breathe at its ending; a shared cadence should still sound shared. Keep any offset below roughly a tenth of the shortest neighboring onset interval, preserve note order and avoid accidental flams. Do not move a triggered event cue away from its intended event. If a part needs a larger delay to feel right, rewrite the rhythmic relation deliberately.

Avoid one global humanize pass. Do not swing echoes twice, loosen both sides of a tight unison differently, randomize pad note lengths into gaps, or add pitch drift to everything. Keep an untouched timing version for comparison. When an expressive take loses the source’s bounce, reduce the offsets and work on touch, rests and note endings instead.

## Instrument readiness and focused stress tests

The [instrument candidate register](instrument_candidates.json) links exact prior UVI programs. Native recall evidence means a program made a sound in its tested state; it does not establish a faithful source timbre, a complete expressive range or every named articulation.

| Need | Starting resource or current choice | Focused test and action if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Dry acoustic keys | TDR Mellow Dark or Nordic Upright | Source-register melody, repeated notes, short/long releases and three useful dynamic levels. Keep piano identity; try a sampled-key hybrid before defaulting to EP. |
| Plucked bass | Cuba Bass or Jazzistic Double Bass; source synth bass stays synthetic | Source low notes, repeated pitch, short/held endings and any slide. Reject octave shifts made only to hide a weak low register. |
| Hand percussion | Congas menu, Udu Large and the exact other selected drum program | Identify the sounding instrument and open/muted/bass/slap mapping one note at a time. Test a feasible two-hand cell and the actual phrase. |
| Kit | Jazz Kit, Jazz Brushed Kit 1 or an exact BBA2 state | Main/ghost note separation, kick/hat relation, open/closed articulations and a real fill. Keep electronic source transients where they define the cue. |
| Wind or reed | Djafe Flute Sustain is only a flute candidate | Test source attack, register and breath. Select a bottle synth, reed/clarinet, piccolo/calliope or accordion-like voice where closer. Add a specialist capability if necessary. |
| Evolving synth and choir | Falcon voice design; Warmth is only a possible pad start | Compare attack, held evolution and release, retaining meaningful modulation/pitch controls. A static pad cannot pass by sharing a chord. |
| Mallet and bright pluck | A named wooden/glassy program still needs selection | Compare at the source pitch and loudness; inspect damping and dynamic response. Steel Pan Main and FM Harp are candidates for their own identities, not universal substitutes. |
| Electric guitar | Exact clean/muted/edged UVI program and capability extension | Test ringing versus stopped attack, dyads, repeated notes and the source riff. Keep the synthetic original when it matches better. |
| Kora and harp | Select the actual instrument, tuning and active state | The prior Sky Valiha state used Valiha D1, selector 3, inside a program named Kora 1. Do not count it as a kora performance. Test fixed-string pitch availability and damping. |
| Environmental and vocal-like events | Authored/synthesized source-compatible gestures | Match event envelope, placement and role. Family voices and protected recordings cannot fill these gaps. |

For every new choice, keep a short dry source-register phrase, an expressive version, a second-register/dynamic check and a cue-context excerpt. Record exact program, active layers/selectors, controller map and native state. Compare attack/body/tail, pitch and phrasing at matched loudness. Use the original excerpt as the authority; audition receipts and MIDI program names are supporting evidence.

## Thirty cue plans

### 01 Theme

[Original reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7XBJGVvYSBM) · [Selected MIDI scaffold](https://themushroomkingdom.net/sounds/mid/dkcountry_theme.mid)

**Source layer.** A compact identity statement that opens into a welcoming band. A deliberately small square-like opening establishes the hook; the larger sampled statement answers it with brighter attacks. Keep the recognizable pickup and phrase-ending accents when the sound opens out.

**Harmony and tone.** Plucked electric bass defines the harmonic floor; brass-like stabs mark arrivals rather than running under every lead note. The contrast in scale is more important than adding chord extensions. Cheeky, confident and celebratory, with a sharp change from tiny electronic gesture to a full groove.

**Arrangement form.** Opening: preserve the retro voice alone. A: full hook with bass and kit. B: shorten the backing to expose an answer. Return: restore the hook with one additional percussion response, then a clean cadence.

**Cast layer.**

- **producer r2 — Cue-specific original synthetic/sampled lead, accents or support.** Opening square voice; A lead and sampled accents. B short electronic answer with thinner kit. Return original lead; one new accent only.
- **bass r1 — Plucked electric bass.** A source bass figure, short releases. B fewer roots and a compact pickup. Return firm source contour.
- **afro_caribbean_percussion r1 — Conga-family hand percussion.** A muted/open stroke conversation. B reduce to the essential cell. Return one answering fill.
- **aux_percussion r1 — One selected shaker, woodblock, bell or related small percussion.** A light shaker, no constant bell. B rest during the lead reply. Return sparse bell or woodblock punctuation.

**Performance.** G1 interlocking pulse. Audition a 53–55% lilt only in the full-groove offbeats; keep the retro opening and main hook rhythm intact. Let conga and bass endings create lift before adding extra attacks.

**Artistic liberty.** Add a short percussion answer on the returning statement and one bass anticipation at a harmonic arrival. Preserve the original opening/full-band contrast.

**Review target.** Compare opening, first full statement and return separately. A louder brass patch must not erase the square opening or make every phrase sound like a fanfare.

### 02 Simian Segue

[Original reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b1xkXrzVQdU) · [Selected MIDI scaffold](https://www.vgmusic.com/music/console/nintendo/snes/DKC_Map-KM.mid)

**Source layer.** A friendly travelling refrain with a slightly old-fashioned wink. The piano’s short, bouncing sentence is the foreground identity; clipped string pulses and the reed-like reply are separate functions. Write the piano as a statement, gap and answer rather than a continuous keyboard fill.

**Harmony and tone.** An upright-like low part supplies bounce, with brief pizzicato chords above it. Keep the bass audible and avoid adding left-hand roots over it. Echo-named MIDI tracks are effects candidates, not extra musicians. Dry, playful and companionable; worn acoustic attack has priority over soft electric-piano gloss.

**Arrangement form.** A: piano melody over bass and compact kit. B: piano thins to leave the original reed answer exposed. Return: piano comes back with a changed final release and one comping inversion, then clears space for the next cue.

**Cast layer.**

- **keys r1 — Acoustic piano; dry upright attack.** A foreground piano, detached melody above quiet support. B reduce left hand and leave reply gap. Return hook with a lighter pickup and slightly fuller arrival.
- **bass r1 — Upright bass pizzicato.** A clear plucked source figure. B hold the anchor while piano rests. Return same contour, one shaped pickup.
- **producer r2 — Clarinet/reed-like sampled or synthesized answer.** A short pizzicato support and restrained echo. B preserve clarinet-like synthetic/reed answer. Return trim effects before the piano cadence.
- **acoustic_drums r1 — Compact brushed acoustic kit.** A small rim/brush pulse. B rest on the exposed reply. Return a quiet lead-in rather than a crash.

**Performance.** G2 light lilt. Compare 54%, 56% and straight eighth-note versions at equal loudness. Place melody and accompaniment independently; do not swing duplicated echo notes a second time. Keep the low part’s short endings intelligible.

**Artistic liberty.** A small piano turnaround and bass pickup may enliven the return. A swing feel may be stronger in accompaniment than in the iconic melody.

**Review target.** Use the King Meteor scaffold, remove echo doubling, and compare the piano/reed handoff. Its 390 low-register notes and MIDI 28–43 bass window require actual low-note audition, not octave transposition to suit a patch.

### 03 DK Island Swing

[Original reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HG8YrDF6cDk) · [Selected MIDI scaffold](https://www.vgmusic.com/music/console/nintendo/snes/DKC_-_DK_Island_Swing-1.mid)

**Source layer.** The island reveals itself through rhythm before the tune fully arrives. Treat the breathy pipe or bottle-like hook as a compact rhythmic call. Its identity comes from the attack, register and gaps as much as pitch. The percussion-led introduction and fuller melodic passage need contrasting density.

**Harmony and tone.** Low bass anchors the pitched passage while sustained strings or pad provide depth. Bright replies should point to phrase ends. Keep the original bass/harmony relationship before testing a single passing anticipation. Earthy, alert and exuberant, opening into a more expansive jungle space.

**Arrangement form.** Opening: percussion alone establishes a conversational cycle. A: add the breathy hook and low anchor. B: open the held backing and reduce small percussion. Return: restore the hook with a different hand-drum answer rather than a louder unison layer.

**Cast layer.**

- **afro_caribbean_percussion r1 — Conga-family hand percussion.** Opening and A groove leader, differentiated strokes. B fewer strokes under the sustained passage. Return an authored answer ending before hook re-entry.
- **aux_percussion r1 — One selected shaker, woodblock, bell or related small percussion.** Opening wood clicks or shaker countercell. B withdraw the bright layer. Return one contrasting register.
- **winds r1 — Breathy pipe/flute; source-matched bottle synth allowed.** A hook with short breath-shaped groups. B longer but separated answer. Return original attack and register.
- **bass r1 — Plucked electric bass.** A low source anchor with controlled endings. B fewer attacks supporting sustained color. Return source contour.
- **producer r2 — Cue-specific original synthetic/sampled lead, accents or support.** Opening ambience kept behind drums. B strings/pad and bottle-like alternative if needed. Return retain original synthetic accents.

**Performance.** G1 stable timeline plus conversation. Start mostly straight; audition a restrained lilt in a secondary shaker only. If testing 2–3 son orientation, fit it to the phrase and preserve orientation through the return; reject it if it fights the original accents.

**Artistic liberty.** More open/muted drum contrast, a two-part percussion answer and selective bass anticipation can make the performance livelier without replacing the hook.

**Review target.** The selected MIDI’s later-section fit is weak. Align and compare each source section separately; never apply its global 1.225 timing fit as a performance instruction. Compare natural flute with a bottle-like synth and keep the closer voice.

### 04 Cranky's Theme

[Original reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_KycvSI-lkg) · [Selected MIDI scaffold](https://themushroomkingdom.net/sounds/mid/dkcountry_cranky_cabin.mid)

**Source layer.** A comic character portrait built from wooden attacks and quick replies. The working foreground is a marimba-like wooden line, punctuated by a high piccolo-like answer and occasional piano light. Keep its compact, teasing phrasing and the response above it instead of recasting everything as honky-tonk piano.

**Harmony and tone.** Clipped low strings or plucked bass define short support, with thin sustained backing only where the melody has room. Use the original harmonic movement as the first chart. Spry, mischievous and dry, with brief high-register sparkle.

**Arrangement form.** A: wooden theme and clipped low support. B: expose the high answer while the mallet rests. Return: mallet repeats with a changed accent group and one piano punctuation; end the joke cleanly.

**Cast layer.**

- **pitched_percussion r1 — Wooden marimba/xylophone/kalimba-like source voice.** A two-mallet wooden foreground. B leave a complete answering gap. Return the hook with one altered accent, no continuous roll.
- **bass r1 — Upright bass pizzicato.** A short pizzicato anchor. B reduce during high reply. Return clean releases.
- **winds r1 — High pipe/piccolo/calliope-like source voice.** A sparse high punctuation. B short featured answer with breath gap. Return rest under mallet arrival.
- **keys r1 — Acoustic piano; dry upright attack.** A isolated highlights. B rest when wind speaks. Return one upper chord tone.

**Performance.** G2 optional 53–55% lilt. Short notes and unequal phrase weight supply the humor; avoid delaying every high reply or making all mallet velocities alternate mechanically.

**Artistic liberty.** Vary the return’s final two-note articulation and allow a brief piano reply. Keep the mallet-first casting unless the source comparison favors a sampled-key hybrid.

**Review target.** Audition wooden mallet attack and decay against the source before choosing pan, FM harp or piano. None is a default substitute merely because it is installed.

## Construction order and review

Start with seven diagnostic pilots: Simian Segue for dry piano, low bass and light lilt; DK Island Swing for hand percussion, section alignment and pipe/bottle identity; Aquatic Ambience for held sound evolution; Life in the Mines for breathy lead against strings; Forest Frenzy for environmental interlock; Funky’s Fugue for electric articulation and pocket; and Fear Factory for synth bass and a firm engine. These cover the most consequential uncertainties without rendering thirty complete arrangements first.

For each pilot, make a faithful plain statement, an expressive performance of the same parts, and a restrained cast expansion. Keep the pitch content and nominal tempo the same while evaluating groove; compare a larger liberty separately. Choose on recognizable hook, instrumental identity, movement, phrase direction and mix clarity. Record why a version wins and the exact condition that would make us change our mind. This prevents louder or denser versions from winning by default.

After the pilot choices, build the remaining full cues by family and finish the short events as compact gestures. Use their actual source phrase boundaries rather than imposing eight-bar forms. Keep editable MIDI, articulation/control lanes, dry stems, selected performance, processed stems and a readable session map. Record every added response and overdub. A useful provisional decision can proceed while its revision trigger remains explicit.
