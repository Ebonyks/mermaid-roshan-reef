# Mermaid Roshan soundtrack identity proposal

Status: **Owner-endorsed overall mission statement with proposed implementation details**. The owner regards the overall sonic design as the mission statement; detailed palettes, programs and cue casts remain for review. The owner has commissioned a game-wide, multi-genre design phase and added the original 1994 SNES *Donkey Kong Country* as a concrete influence. Opera House, Sky Lagoon and Castle are improving studies; the owner has explicitly not accepted them for release. This proposal develops the musical language before further cue construction.

The [ensemble design guide](ENSEMBLE_DESIGN_GUIDE_2026-11-01.md) organizes this direction as a performing band, including a core DJ/producer and historical references for uncertain technique and style decisions.

## Sound identity

**A warm, melodic adventure score joining Afro-Caribbean groove, intimate acoustic playing and spacious electronic atmosphere.** Locations have distinct musical personalities. A small family of melodic gestures, rounded tones, responsive percussion and recurring approaches to phrasing makes those personalities feel related.

The score should move comfortably between aquatic ambience, acoustic soul, chamber dance, funk, folk and calypso. Afro-Caribbean playing supplies a strong rhythmic and instrumental influence across the project. Its presence can be a bass figure, guitar response or percussion conversation as readily as a steel-pan lead.

The constants are expressive foreground playing, a tune the listener can follow, purposeful rhythmic movement and space for another voice to answer. Lead instruments, meter, harmonic mode, ensemble size and genre change with the scene. A family connection should survive a change from guitar to flute, fiddle, piano or synth.

He describes the jungle theme growing from three demos and his wave-sequencing experiment in *Aquatic Ambience*. His stated influences include synthesized film music, rock and dance music. This supports a broad score vocabulary, including electronic atmosphere alongside acoustic and rhythmic colors. [Composer interview](https://vgmonline.net/davidwiseinterview/)

## Palette families

Each cue selects a primary family and an individual substyle. These families describe musical roles rather than cultural labels for characters.

| Family | Genre range | Featured voices | Rhythm and depth | Main uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water and reverie | Ambient soul, downtempo electronica | Warm guitar, electric piano or a breathing flute | Udu, rounded bass, sparse plucks and a slowly changing instrumental texture | Reef depth, night, pool, quiet discovery |
| Sunlight and living places | Afro-Caribbean groove, highlife, calypso | Nylon guitar, kora, balafon; pan for selected identities | Hand-percussion conversations and offbeat chord voicings; bass carries a recognizable figure | Daylight, garden, craft and gathering |
| Home and companionship | Acoustic soul, bossa, chamber waltz | Sunbird, piano or Celtic harp | Upright bass, brushes and warm answering strings or plucks | Castle rooms, care, familiar company, bedtime |
| Theatre and character play | Revue, chamber jazz, light dance forms | Opera's single pan, keys, gypsy jazz guitar, harp or fiddle | A written refrain with contrasting character verses and instrumental spotlights | Opera and career identities |
| Air and discovery | Modal chamber music, ambient electronica | Flute, duduk, santoor or a soft synth voice | Valiha, selected gamelan or harp, small pulse and changing registers | Sky Lagoon, magic, galaxy and discovery |
| Movement and invention | Warm funk, light breakbeat, playful mechanical groove | Melodic bass, keys or clean guitar | Selected BeatBox kit, wooden percussion, breaks and written fills | Racing, bouncing, engineering and active play |
| Weather and distant places | Modal folk, Celtic chamber music, cold ambient color | Low whistle, fiddle, harp or duduk | Santoor, bodhran or a measured ostinato, with warmth in the bass and cadence | Northern world, ice and exploration |
| Challenge and coming together | Call-and-response soul, calypso funk, gentle celebratory rock | Guitar, keys, bass or a returning thematic lead | Confident calls, readable pauses and reassuring ensemble answers | Friendly challenges, Grand Puff care and celebration |

Working pace lanes range from 68-96 quarter-note beats per minute for reflective water music, 96-116 for sunlit groove, 72-104 for home or folk, and 108-126 for movement. Theatre follows its character's dance form; air/discovery can retain Sky Lagoon's effective 112 BPM. These are starting lanes rather than fixed limits. Three-beat, compound and straight meters should create meaningful differences between cue families.

A Castle room can be a chamber waltz while another is bossa or acoustic soul. A Ballerina cue can use a three-beat harp-and-fiddle dance; a Racer can use bass-led funk. Both can belong to Roshan through phrasing, motif fragments, tonal warmth and the way the ensemble responds.

## Shared themes and musical grammar

The existing score declares a six-note rising-and-returning Roshan contour. It is a useful starting point for review alongside the already approved melody studies. The proposed replacement score draws on its short interval fragments, rhythmic answers and cadence shape. Full statements belong at meaningful arrivals, homecoming and celebration; each room develops its own memorable tune.

Four thematic roles give the score an emotional organization:

- **Roshan and curiosity:** an opening gesture that reaches outward and receives an answer. It can become a guitar pickup, flute phrase, bass response or brief plucked figure.
- **Home and care:** a settling answer and reassuring harmonic arrival, heard most fully in home and care scenes.
- **Discovery and wonder:** an open-ended version of the gesture, developed through register, spacing and modal color.
- **Play and togetherness:** a rhythmic version that supports movement, then resolves toward the home/care answer.

These are proposed roles, not four newly composed melodies. We should compare the approved hooks and choose their shared DNA before fixing new notes. Similarities in percussion, phrase lengths, answering gestures and voicing can connect cues even where a melody quote would be distracting.

Use sixths, added ninths, suspended colors and selected modal turns as part of the vocabulary. Key and mode follow the emotional role. Rhythmic links can include an offbeat guitar answer, a small three-plus-three-plus-two accent or a hand-drum reply, translated to suit the cue's genre.

### Tone targets for preset auditions

| Voice | Tone and performance target |
|---|---|
| Guitar | Rounded attack with audible wood and string body; soft upper-mid bite. Chord voicings and staggered strokes support the lead's phrasing. |
| Piano and electric piano | A warm core with enough attack to make a clear rhythmic figure. Bell-like color appears selectively above the body. |
| Flute, whistle and duduk | A human breath shape, stable melodic center and natural phrase ends. Their register and intensity match the instrument's useful range. |
| Harp, kora, valiha and small plucks | Resonance and decay remain audible; pulse comes from the written pattern and touch, with contrasting registers given space. |
| Fiddle and bowed colors | Authored bow-length phrases, useful dynamic changes and soft releases. Select a program that supports the intended articulation. |
| Pan and struck instruments | A clear first strike followed by natural ringing or a shaped roll. Brightness retains body; the part has room for other foreground voices. |
| Bass and percussion | Rounded low end, audible note identity and believable skin, brush or wood attacks. Choose kit character separately for each genre family. |
| Electronic depth | Slow harmonic movement, a controlled stereo image and a clear place behind the foreground voice. Deliberate color stays consistent with the family. |

Performance design is part of preset selection. Guitar chords need playable voicings and believable strokes; winds need breathing phrases; bowed parts need suitable attacks and releases. Pan rolls use shaped alternating strikes, with the current bank's lack of a separate sampled roll articulation kept explicit. The pan references already selected remain useful performance studies; pan is one featured voice in the larger score.

## How full pieces develop

Main songs need an opening identity, development, contrast, a recognizable return and an ending. A song-led cue can use verses and refrains; an atmospheric environment can use a lyrical A section, a contrasting B landscape, a changed return and coda. Both have a written arc.

Changes in orchestration must have a musical purpose. A lead can pass its phrase to another instrument; a dense section can resolve into a duo; a bass or percussion figure can carry a transition. Harmony, rhythm and counterlines receive featured passages as well as the principal melody. The first two bars still establish a recognizable place, even in a spacious cue.

Main-theme duration follows the complete song, with the current studies around three to four minutes. Minigames receive distinct, complete miniatures of no more than 60 seconds: an immediate hook, contrasting response, changed return and concise ending or separately planned loop edit. Short preset tests are labeled auditions and never presented as finished soundtrack songs.

## Applying the language to the current studies

- **Opera House:** the theatre/calypso identity has the strongest existing foundation. Keep its single pan part as the principal signature, while expanding written roles for flute, guitar, bass, keys and percussion. Pan articulation, melodic focus and source roughness remain listening priorities.
- **Sky Lagoon:** retain the stronger flute identity and its relationship with valiha and udu. Develop the air/discovery family through harmonic depth, breathing and clear changes in instrumental space.
- **Castle:** warm guitar and chord playing are a useful home-family study. Explore a stronger homecoming refrain and complementary piano, harp or bowed colors after palette auditions; the present flute chorus is one orchestration experiment.

These versions inform the language. The owner's latest assessment is improvement with further polish required, rather than release acceptance.

## Whole score coverage and the next construction step

The companion map assigns **44 current declarative cues and 14 retained score roles** to proposed palette families and substyles. It excludes the magic-beans toot, which is SFX. The map covers the current catalogue without changing runtime ownership or declaring every future scene commissioned.

Before another cue is built, its short composition brief should name:

1. Scene purpose, palette family, genre and two concrete references at most.
2. Lead, supporting voices and exact auditioned UVI programs or the explicitly authorized RetroPlug/LSDJ Game Boy setup.
3. Main hook, thematic relationship and contrasting material.
4. Form with section lengths, entrances, withdrawals, handoffs and return.
5. Rhythm, bass, harmony, performance articulations and spaces for speech.
6. Full-song ending and any separately needed runtime loop edit.

Review the identity, family map and thematic roles first. Then audition the contrasting palettes, select their strongest programs and approve cue briefs before full arrangement construction.

## Production and acceptance

Keep the established REAPER/Falcon/Lua workflow, editable individual parts and printed mixes. EQ and dynamics begin at the instrument source, followed by ensemble balance and restrained mastering. Preserve earlier versions and clear authorship, program and render records.

The existing audio mix, voice protection, routing, mobile and human-listening requirements remain binding. The current demo freeze keeps this work on a source/design branch; this proposal does not insert new music into the demo. Musical identity and cue acceptance remain owner decisions, separately from machine checks.
