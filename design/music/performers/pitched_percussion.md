## Musical identity
This collaborator composes through resonance, rhythm and melodic shape. They can bring a foreground tune, a repeating harmonic figure, an answering phrase, a percussion-led hook or a well-placed silence. Instrument choice follows the scene: pan, balafon, marimba, vibraphone and small bells offer different attacks, sustain mechanisms and physical possibilities.

Pan is this performer’s deepest proposed specialization. Andy Narell is the principal reference for combining pan performance, composition and ensemble thinking. The other instruments require their own technical and contextual study; this profile makes no claim that Narell performs every instrument listed here. All named artists are references, not project participants.

Opera has **exactly one principal pan part**, carrying its melodic identity. Guitar, winds, keys, bass and percussion must still have substantial written roles. Sky Lagoon and Castle retain their non-pan identities; this performer may contribute another instrument or rest for the entire cue.

The wider score follows the owner’s collective concept: related multi-instrumentalists exchange foreground, harmony, groove, answer and texture roles. Original DKC1 informs place, atmosphere, contrasting episodes and recognizable returns. Afro-Caribbean interaction and warm lo-fi foundations inform the ensemble without requiring pan in every location.

## Proposed musical knowledge and working habits
The fictional performer should be able to:

- Read melody, chord symbols, rhythmic notation, dynamics, rests and section maps; learn an approved phrase by ear and preserve its identity.
- Understand chord tones, inversions, guide-tone movement, sixths, ninths, suspensions and modal color. Choose extensions with the harmonic players instead of filling every available register.
- Distinguish straight, swung and compound subdivisions; place syncopation against an established pulse; recognize when a pan figure competes with the percussion’s main rhythmic idea.
- Develop a short motive through changed register, rhythm, spacing or answering contour while keeping its recognizable core.
- Hear the difference between a useful harmonic clash, an unintended ringing overlap and a sample artifact.
- Suggest an arranging change when a part cannot be played convincingly, rather than hiding the problem in extra tracks.

This is an intended professional capability specification, not evidence that the present MIDI or samples already demonstrate it.

For writing sessions, the performer can propose a hook, harmonic route or rhythmic cell. Once selected, the arrangement records its statements, development, contrast and return. Improvisation supplies bounded alternatives during development; accepted takes become deliberate written performances. Repeated passages should have purposeful variation and recognizable anchors.

## Pan technique: the principal specialization
**Clean attack and useful ring.** Each important melodic arrival begins with a deliberate, readable strike. The player releases the stick from the surface so the note can speak. Subsequent resonance belongs to the phrase: it may continue into a compatible harmony, yield to another voice or require a controlled ending. Bright attack should retain a resonant body.

**Alternating-hand rolls.** A sustained pan roll consists of reiterated strikes, normally alternating hands on the sustained pitch. Start from balanced hands and controlled rebound; shape the whole note with an entrance, dynamic trajectory and release. The coordination must be even enough to avoid an accidental dominant-hand accent. Pan education sources describe this as a single-stroke roll. [Steelpan technique guide](https://www.steelpan-steeldrums-information.com/steel-pan-technique.html)

For this performer, a successful roll must have:

- A clear initial note, followed by continuation that supports its sustain.
- A chosen dynamic contour: growing, settling or holding with a stated purpose.
- A rate appropriate to the register, resonance and musical effect.
- A planned transition into the next note and a controlled release.
- Enough remaining physical capacity for the next phrase.

A roll is **not pitch vibrato**. Repeated attacks renew energy at the note’s pitch; pitch vibrato changes pitch around a center. Pitch-bend or an LFO cannot stand in for missing pan strikes. Tremolo terminology should be clarified in the call sheet: same-pitch reiteration, alternation between pitches and electronic modulation are different requests.

**Double-time flourishes.** Define “double time” against the established subdivision. A brief melodic flourish can double the note density while the underlying tempo remains stable. Its first note, destination and accent shape must be audible. Speed and loudness are separate choices: a faster passage need not become a louder one.

The performer should be able to shape a flourish toward a destination, then leave space. They should reject an ornamental run that obscures the approved hook or forces an implausible crossing. A fast moving-pitch flourish and a same-pitch sustaining roll need different programming and review.

**Sticking and register.** Select the intended physical pan configuration before treating the part as fully playable. “Steel Pan Main” does not establish tenor, double-second or another layout. Pitches adjacent on a piano keyboard may require substantial travel on pan.

Alternation is a starting point, not an absolute law for moving passages. Tom Miller’s double-second exercises explicitly address repeated same-hand strokes and crossovers arising from the instrument’s layout. [Tom Miller, “Double Seconds in Practice”](https://weteachpan.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/The-Steel-Times-2-9.pdf)

Every exposed fast passage needs a sticking proposal, reachable movements and a credible recovery into the following phrase. A two-hand roll already occupies both hands; it cannot simultaneously sustain an independent busy accompaniment.

**Damping and phrase endings.** Mark where ringing should continue and where a clean stop matters. Acoustic muting must allow for hand movement and instrument geometry. In software, verify what note-off actually does: a short MIDI note may still trigger a long one-shot sample. Conversely, an envelope that chops the sound instantly may imply an impossible acoustic stop.

**Melodic economy and dynamics.** Prefer a complete, memorable statement with breathing room to continuous decoration. Use a restrained pickup, shaped peak and settling answer. Reserve the busiest flourish or strongest roll for a meaningful arrival. A return can gain weight through accompaniment or register while preserving the melody.

## Collaboration and physical limits
The performer listens first to the section’s foreground and groove leaders. With bass, agree where low movement and pan arrivals reinforce each other. With guitar and keys, distribute chord tones and register. With winds, leave real phrase space for a reply. With percussion, decide whether the pitched figure leads the groove, answers it or sits outside it.

One coherent pan part may contain melody and occasional playable dyads. It should not secretly contain several simultaneous players. Opera’s single principal pan may use ordinary production routing, but a second layered pan melody or independent pan accompaniment would change the agreed cast.

Switching from pan to marimba, vibes or bells needs time to move and change implements. Simultaneous incompatible instruments require a declared overdub or another performer. Four mallets do not remove limits on reach, independence, speed or damping. A ringing tail may overlap a change of instrument only where the physical and recorded behavior is credible.

## Future programming checks
- Author an intentional attack, continuation and release for each roll. Avoid both identical hard retriggers and arbitrary velocity jitter.
- Choose sustaining rates by register and desired effect; do not mechanically tie every roll to the song’s smallest subdivision.
- Verify overlap, release behavior and maximum active voices at the densest passage.
- Check the transition into and out of the roll, not only its middle.
- Preserve natural body before applying further EQ. Compare at matched loudness, including dry and ensemble contexts.
- Keep the editable MIDI, exact program/state, source processing, render settings and file hashes together.

## Failure modes and acceptance questions
| Failure mode | Review question |
|---|---|
| Constant ornament replaces the tune | Can the hook be recognized after one statement, and does its return remain clear? |
| Repeated samples become a mechanical buzz | Does the roll sustain a musical note while retaining believable articulation? |
| Pitch modulation substitutes for rolls | Are the requested physical strikes actually present? |
| Roll density creates an unintended accent | Do both hands contribute evenly, with intentional phrase-level dynamics? |
| Keyboard logic creates an unplayable pan part | Is the selected setup, sticking, travel and recovery credible at tempo? |
| Ringing obscures harmony or handoffs | Are resonance and damping intentional at each change? |
| Opera becomes pan over decorative backing | Can bass, percussion and another melodic/harmonic voice be described as meaningful written parts? |
| Mallet colors homogenize locations | Do Sky and Castle retain their non-pan identities and their own foregrounds? |
| Tone polishing removes body | Does the instrument retain identity at low volume and in mono? |
| Technically clean audio is called accepted | Are named human, voice, device, child and owner reviews actually recorded? |
