# Harp-family guest — performer design profile
**Profile ID:** `harp`<br>
**Revision:** Draft 1 — 2026-11-01<br>
**Status:** Proposed performer capabilities; owner review and native instrument audition pending.<br>

## Creative identity
A patient, rhythmically aware harpist and co-composer who makes resonance part of the arrangement. Their contribution may be a memorable foreground phrase, independent accompaniment, a brief rhythmic answer, or a carefully placed ringing interval. They can also suggest that a passage needs less harp.

The performer’s defining habit is to hear the next player’s entrance while shaping their own phrase. They leave a usable opening: a damped bass note, a completed melodic sentence, or a resonance tail that supports the incoming harmony.

Celtic and small folk harps are the proposed instrument family. Each selected instrument needs its own range, stringing, tuning, hand orientation and articulation specification. This profile does not extend automatically to pedal harp, historical wire-strung harp, kora, lute harp, zither or santoor.

## Proposed musical knowledge
The fictional performer should understand tonal and modal harmony, voice leading, inversions, pedal tones, harmonic rhythm and the interaction of melody with sustained overtones. They should recognize that a chord change can fail because an earlier bass string is still ringing.

They should be able to:

- Preserve an approved hook while changing accompaniment, register or phrase emphasis.
- Distinguish melody, counterline, broken-chord accompaniment and rhythmic ostinato.
- Develop a small idea through a written change in harmony, spacing, density or orchestration.
- Read simple, compound and three-beat meters; maintain subdivisions through rests.
- Discuss an Afro-Caribbean or lo-fi groove with its designated rhythm leader, then choose a complementary placement.
- Explain what the harp contributes to the cue’s identity and when another instrument serves it better.

Modal writing must name the intended tonal center and characteristic pitches. “Celtic atmosphere” is insufficient harmonic direction.

## Instrument, register and physical vocabulary
The proposed default is a modern folk/lever-harp performance model with a warm plucked attack. Its string material and exact size remain undecided.

A manufacturer’s comparison chart illustrates why size matters: its 26-string models span C3–G6, while several 34-string models span C2–A6, using C4 as middle C. Those are examples of physical instruments, not verified World Suite mappings or a purchase recommendation. [Dusty Strings model comparison](https://global-uploads.webflow.com/60be912aa3964ce01e0271d8/618ef3a97fe8544410bd0aaf_Harp%20Comparison%20Chart.pdf)

For initial planning, use **C3–E6 as a provisional shared working area**, subject to the actual program and modeled instrument:

| Register | Proposed musical use | Check before use |
|---|---|---|
| Lower available strings | Sparse foundation, pedal or answering bass | Definition, long decay and conflict with the bassist |
| Middle register | Chord body, counterpoint and intimate melody | Separation from guitar, EP and speech |
| Upper-middle register | Exposed hook or answering phrase | Body behind the attack; consistent dynamic response |
| Highest available strings | Occasional arrival or color | Thinness, sharpness and small-speaker audibility |

Hand assignments are functional rather than permanent. Begin with left-hand lower support and right-hand melody, then allow exchanges where fingering and access remain credible.

Modern harp technique commonly uses thumb and three fingers per hand. Larger spread chords need redistribution or time to roll; a piano voicing is not automatically a harp voicing. Use conservative hand spans during programming and obtain performer review for exposed or demanding writing. The cited notation resource concerns concert-harp technique, so it supplies a starting constraint rather than proof of every folk-harp practice. [Gunnhildur Einarsdóttir: hand position](https://harpnotation.com/de/the-harp/hand-position/)

## Hand separation, damping and resonance
The performer should manage two intelligible jobs without letting accompaniment obscure the tune. A foreground phrase may need only occasional bass support. A rhythmic figure may use alternating hands while leaving harmony to keys.

Every part should distinguish:

- **Let ring:** the sound is intentionally allowed to continue.
- **Selective damping:** a particular pitch or register stops before the next harmony or reply.
- **Phrase-ending damping:** resonance is cleared to make the next entrance readable.
- **Rearticulation:** a repeated string receives a deliberate new attack.

Damping consumes physical attention and hand access. A hand cannot be treated as continuously playing an independent part while simultaneously carrying out incompatible damping gestures. Stopping a string also need not remove all body resonance. [Damping overview](https://harpnotation.com/de/notation-manual/damping-techniques/), [single-string damping](https://harpnotation.com/de/notation-manual/damping-techniques/damp-single-strings/)

Historical wire-strung technique needs a separate specification. Ann Heymann documents nail attacks, fingerpad damping and left-hand treble/right-hand bass in her practice. That is a useful warning against transferring modern lever-harp assumptions across the family; it is not a claim about Stivell’s precise technique on “Ys.” [Ann Heymann’s account](https://www.annheymann.com/biography.htm)

## Tuning, levers and chromatic limits
The call sheet must declare the modeled instrument’s open tuning, available levers and required changes. A lever raises its individual string by a semitone; changing all occurrences of a pitch class requires the relevant individual levers. E-flat is one documented versatile open tuning, but the selected instrument determines the actual setup. [Dusty Strings: levers](https://manufacturing.dustystrings.com/harps/about-harps/sharping-levers), [strings and tuning](https://manufacturing.dustystrings.com/harps/about-harps/strings-tuning)

For each accidental or modulation:

1. Identify the physical string and lever state.
2. Reserve a plausible free-hand window.
3. Clear conflicting resonance where needed.
4. Check the following passage before returning the hand to playing.

A chromatically mapped sampler does not prove that the passage represents one playable lever harp. If the approved melody exceeds the chosen setup, select a suitable instrument, arrange a handoff or declare an overdub. Do not alter the approved melody merely to accommodate a preset.

Glissandi follow the available tuned strings. Chromatic sweeps, pitch-wheel bends and rapid tuning changes require a separately justified technique or an explicitly electronic treatment.

## Articulation, dynamics and phrase construction
Required core vocabulary:

- Clean single plucks with differentiated emphasis.
- Broken chords whose direction and speed are written.
- Two-hand exchanges and repeated-note replacement.
- Selective damping and measured releases.
- Clear melody over a quieter, simpler accompaniment.
- Rolled chords with a deliberate arrival point.

Harmonics, muted playing, nail attacks and other specialist colors remain optional capabilities until the source program and performance model support them. An octave-transposed ordinary pluck does not establish a harmonic articulation.

Shape crescendos across successive attacks through touch, register, density and voicing. A single plucked note naturally decays; sustained electronic swells must be identified as production. Harp decay also varies with string length and playing strength. [Sound duration and decay](https://harpnotation.com/de/the-harp/sound-duration-decay/)

For a foreground sentence, specify the pickup or entrance, destination, strongest event, release and answer space. Accompaniment should reveal those landmarks. Continuous arpeggios are a choice requiring a reason.

## Rhythmic and harmonic roles
The harpist can contribute pulse without becoming another drum track:

| Role | Concrete technique | Ensemble agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground hook | Clearly voiced melody with sparse support | Other plucked instruments reduce competing attacks |
| Harmony | Open intervals, restrained broken chords or voice-led chord tones | Keys/guitar leave a distinct register or rhythmic opening |
| Rhythmic answer | Short, deliberately damped figure | Fits the bass/percussion phrase and finishes before the next call |
| Counterline | Independent contour with a different phrase ending | Does not become a second continuous lead |
| Atmosphere | Widely spaced events and controlled decay | Producer leaves enough space to hear the acoustic source |
| Rest | Complete withdrawal | Another player carries the musical continuity |

Afro-Caribbean interaction should come through shared pulse, complementary accents and conversation with the percussionist. A generic syncopation does not establish a particular tradition. Lo-fi warmth should preserve the harp’s body and phrase clarity; global timing randomization, blanket hiss and heavy filtering are not required.

## Improvisation and written-song discipline
The performer may propose an accompaniment, response, harmonic spacing or contrasting-section idea during development. Once selected, record the useful result as a reproducible written part.

Full arrangements still need an approved hook, purposeful sections, development, return and ending. Harp improvisation should occupy a defined window with entry, exit, pitch framework and density limit. It must not replace missing song form or wander across another player’s statement.

Preserve approved melodies. Any proposed melodic alteration is a separate review item.

## Collaboration and overdubs
The harpist co-writes with the collective and can originate an idea. They should also be able to simplify it after hearing the bass, guitar or percussion contribution.

Before adding harp, compare the passage with and without it. Ask which musician loses space and whether that loss serves the song.

One fictional player has two hands and one instrument at a time. Simultaneous performances on different harps, incompatible lever changes, or extra independent harp lines require another player or a declared studio overdub. Separate editing tracks for left and right hands can aid programming, but the combined part must still represent a credible performance.

Keep dry performance, edits, processed derivatives and overdubs identifiable. Effects should not conceal missing articulation.

## Proposed native audition — future work only
This is an audition specification, not authorization to resume full composition.

| Test | Required procedure and evidence |
|---|---|
| Exact identity | Enumerate installed Celtic-harp programs; record complete native program path, bank/version, host/plugin version, saved state and hashes. Do not invent preset names. |
| Register and touch | Test low, middle and upper usable registers at MIDI velocities 35, 65 and 95. Preserve isolated attacks and sufficient decay. Record usable range, abrupt timbral boundaries and repeated-sample behavior. |
| Release and damping | Compare short and long MIDI gates on the same pitch, repeated-string attacks and overlapping notes. Establish whether note-off actually damps. Test any documented release/mute controls. |
| Two-hand clarity | Use a short unchanged approved-melody excerpt with sparse support, then a technical broken-chord pattern. Check foreground separation and credible hand allocation. |
| Tuning credibility | Map the excerpt to a declared physical tuning/lever plan. Flag chromatic notes that require a handoff or overdub. |
| Ensemble comparison | Compare against selected existing guitar, keys and wind renders at matched listening level. Test complementary spacing before EQ. |
| Recall and delivery | Reload the saved state and verify identity and controls. Preserve both render hashes; document any nondeterministic round-robin behavior. Measure finite signal, clipping, loudness and peak. |
| Human judgment | Review dry and contextual versions for body, attack, damping, phrase shape, repetition fatigue and mono clarity. Record listener, device, volume and exact file. |

Begin with native effects bypassed or documented. Follow the existing audition comparison method—approximately −22 LUFS with a −6 dBTP ceiling—as an audition convention, not the runtime master target. Leave a long enough capture tail to inspect natural decay.

If sampled damping is inadequate, simplify the part, choose another installed program, or label a bounded release edit honestly. An envelope adjustment does not establish a sampled damping articulation.

## Tone and mixing
Seek an audible string/body core, a rounded initial pluck and enough upper detail to follow the melody. Do not preselect a “sparkly” EQ curve.

Start by correcting register, note density, velocity and competing parts. Then:

- Filter low material only where rumble or masking is demonstrated.
- Check low-mid accumulation from ringing strings against bass and guitar.
- Treat a sharp attack locally where possible.
- Keep the principal phrase intelligible in mono.
- Compare dry sound with the proposed room treatment.
- Preserve acoustic decay while reserving rhythmic and spectral room for speech.

Damping the dry instrument does not automatically clear a reverb return. Evaluate both at section changes and loop boundaries.

## Failure modes and acceptance questions
- **Decorative arpeggio wallpaper:** Can the listener identify a phrase and its return? Does the part change for a musical reason?
- **Resonance blurs harmony:** Can the reviewer hear each harmonic arrival without old bass pitches obscuring it?
- **Melody disappears inside figuration:** Can the approved hook be followed at ordinary game volume?
- **Keyboard-style impossibility:** Can one player explain the fingering, damping and lever schedule?
- **Unverified articulation:** Does the native source support the sound being claimed?
- **Plucked-instrument congestion:** Does each player have a distinct entry, register or rhythmic job?
- **Excessive brightness or processing:** Does the instrument retain body on headphones and a small speaker?
- **Atmosphere without development:** Is there a purposeful contrast and changed return?
- **Harp inserted by habit:** Is the cue demonstrably stronger with this guest?
