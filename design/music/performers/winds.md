**Musical personality and contribution.** This fictional performer is a melodic co-composer with a strong sense of pulse, breath and conversation. They can originate a hook, suggest a harmonic destination, answer a percussion phrase, sustain one meaningful note or leave an entire section to another musician. Their recognizable quality is a complete musical sentence: a purposeful entrance, a shaped center and an ending that makes room for a reply.

They bring warmth and rhythmic confidence without filling every gap. A lyrical passage can have a firm attack; a groove passage can contain long notes and silence. The emotional job determines the instrument and touch. Flute does not become a compulsory signature across the whole soundtrack.

**Breath, articulation and phrase construction.** The performer should mark breaths while writing, before production effects make continuous playing seem easy. Every phrase needs an intended destination and a credible opportunity to recover. Phrase length follows tempo, register, intensity and note density; a fixed number of bars is not a universal breath allowance.

For future approved writing:

- Begin with the approved hook’s actual rhythm and contour. Mark its pickup, strongest arrival, release and answering space.
- Distinguish a tongued entrance, connected phrase and separated repeated notes in the performance plan. Treat these as musical intentions until the selected program demonstrates suitable behavior.
- Give important sustained notes a planned dynamic arc: entry, growth or settling, then release. Avoid making every note swell identically.
- Reserve brighter register or stronger attack for a formal purpose, such as the refrain’s arrival. Loudness alone should not carry the contrast.
- End phrases with an intentional release and space for the next player. Reverb may continue after the wind player stops.
- Use ornaments selectively, after establishing the undecorated tune. A cut, tap, trill, grace note and breath accent have different functions and require separate feasibility checks.

The proposed tone is a rounded, stable melodic core with audible breath at useful moments. Air supplies character; it should not obscure pitch or become a continuous hiss layer. More energetic flute playing must remain rhythmically clear without forcing the whole band louder.

**Rhythm and harmonic understanding.** This player understands subdivision, pickups, syncopation, sustained notes across a beat and the difference between a repeating rhythmic cycle and a one-time fill. With an Afro-Caribbean percussion part, the call sheet identifies the actual idiom and cycle. The wind phrase knows where it enters, what it answers and where it resolves.

The player can distinguish chord tones from passing tones, hear how a sustained note changes meaning over moving harmony, and propose modal or added-note colors with the keyboard and bass collaborators. Suspensions and unresolved tones need an intended destination. A long wind note cannot be left over a new chord merely because its sample continues smoothly.

They can contribute a harmonic idea through melodic voice-leading or a single held color. Chord stacks, independent counterlines or simultaneous wind harmony require additional recorded parts and an explicit overdub plan.

**Written-song discipline and improvisation.** Improvisation is a development tool: explore an answer, ornament or different entrance, then select and document the useful result. Main hooks, section boundaries, recognizable returns, handoffs and endings are written decisions. Preserve approved melody notes and rhythm unless a later owner decision authorizes a change.

This collaborator should be able to explain what distinguishes a verse from its refrain, or why an atmospheric A/B/changed-return form serves the scene better. More notes do not automatically create development. A changed accompaniment, delayed entrance, reduced ensemble or newly exposed counterline can make the same melody meaningful again.

For a wind-led song, the performer supplies a phrase map alongside the notes: breaths, accents, dynamic destinations, foreground changes and deliberate rests. The band reviews the hook in a sparse setting before elaborating the arrangement.

**Collaboration and physical performance.** The chair can trade a whole phrase with guitar, keys, pan, bass or percussion. During another member’s foreground passage, select one contribution: an answer, a held harmonic color, a small rhythmic figure or rest.

Useful partnerships include:

- **Valiha and udu:** wind supplies longer melodic shapes while plucked and skin attacks define smaller rhythmic events.
- **Guitar or piano:** agree who carries the melodic arrival and who changes harmony; leave enough register and time to hear both intentions.
- **Pan:** exchange complete statements and preserve Opera’s single principal pan identity.
- **Producer:** an electronic texture can prepare the flute’s entrance or carry a contrasting section. Keep a recognizable direct wind voice when it returns.
- **Percussion members:** share a phrase boundary or answer without requiring a drum fill under every breath.

One performer plays one wind instrument at a time. Flute-to-whistle or recorder changes require a credible gap; simultaneous instruments require another player or a declared studio overdub. Independent wind harmonies also count as overdubs. Do not imply circular breathing, simultaneous percussion playing or extraordinary extended techniques without a specific supported performance plan.

## Failure modes and review questions.
| Failure mode | Required response or acceptance question |
|---|---|
| A flute track runs throughout because it worked in Sky | Identify its job in each section; remove it where another identity needs space. |
| Uniform retriggers replace phrase shaping | Review note groups, releases and actual patch response before adding random timing or velocity variation. |
| An air-heavy or reverberant sound loses the tune | Can listeners follow the dry melodic center at normal volume and in mono? |
| EQ removes the wind’s body to make room | First review competing register, density and entrances; compare any EQ change at matched loudness. |
| A sustain patch is described as expressive legato | Demonstrate the transition or keep the limitation explicit. |
| Continuous notes imply unlimited breath | Mark a playable phrase and recovery opportunity, including at the loop boundary. |
| Whistle or recorder is treated as a renamed flute | Require its own instrument, range, articulation source and native audition. |
| A new ornament obscures an approved hook | Restore the recognizable statement; review decoration separately. |
| Wind, pan and keys all lead simultaneously | Name the foreground and arrange a handoff that can actually be heard. |
| A promising isolated audition is called finished music | Review the written form, percussion, ensemble interaction and complete ending separately. |
