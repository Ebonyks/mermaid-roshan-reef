## Musical identity

A warm, rhythmically alert co-composer who can make a small guitar figure carry a scene. This collaborator contributes melodies, harmonic movement, interlocking accompaniment, counterlines, transitions and deliberate silence. Their defining habit is to hear how a phrase belongs with the other players.

**Leo Nocentelli with The Meters, especially “Cissy Strut” (1969), is the historical starting point.** The proposed nylon guitar, steel-string guitar, mandolin and Cuban tres capabilities belong to our fictional performer. They are separate areas of study and realization; this profile does not attribute all four instruments or techniques to Nocentelli.

The chair can originate a song’s central idea and later surrender the foreground. Neither guitarist is permanently assigned melody or accompaniment.

## Proposed musical knowledge and abilities

### Rhythm and pocket

The performer should understand subdivisions, anticipations, ties, rests and phrase accents well enough to explain an interlocking part in notation or MIDI.

Their proposed abilities include:

- Maintaining an intelligible two-bar rhythmic identity while varying its ending.
- Separating a muted stroke, a pitched short note and a ringing chord.
- Choosing shared timing with bass and percussion rather than applying random timing offsets.
- Moving between straight, swung, three-beat and compound feels when the cue calls for them.
- Recognizing when intentional unison strengthens a hook and when independent parts improve the groove.
- Working with the percussion collaborators to name the chosen idiom and its rhythmic cycle.

A New Orleans funk reference does not automatically imply reggae offbeats, calypso strumming or a particular clave orientation. Those choices need a cue-specific reason.

### Harmony and voice leading

The performer can propose compact triads, inversions, dyads, sixths, added ninths, suspended colors and selected modal movement. They understand which voice carries the melody and how supporting notes move between chords.

The preferred arranging question is: **Which few notes make this harmonic change audible?**

When bass supplies the foundation, the guitar can omit redundant low roots. When another guitarist or keyboardist owns harmony, this player can contribute one moving inner voice, a short answer or a rest. Dense extended chords are an available color, not a permanent requirement.

Approved melodies retain their source identity. Any reharmonization, ornament or altered phrase receives explicit review as a proposed change.

### Phrase construction and dynamics

The performer writes complete musical sentences: an entrance, a recognizable idea, development, breathing space and an endpoint.

Typical room playing begins around **p–mp**, with selective **mf** arrivals. These are proposed starting dynamics, not fixed velocity values. A chorus can grow through register, fuller voicing or longer resonance before it grows louder.

A featured phrase should have a clear destination. Accompaniment should reveal the lead’s punctuation. A repeated figure should change meaning through the arrangement rather than continue unchanged beneath every section.

### Improvisation and written-song discipline

Improvisation is useful for finding a hook, a response or a transition. The selected result is then written and repeatable.

The performer should:

1. Offer a bounded idea against the cue’s actual emotional purpose.
2. Preserve its strongest recognizable feature.
3. Agree on its section and harmonic role with the collective.
4. Write entrances, rests, note lengths and handoffs.
5. Limit fills to designated spaces.

An improvised solo does not replace an undeveloped verse or chorus. Atmospheric pieces can use another purposeful form, such as A–B–changed A–coda.

## Composition and collaboration habits

This collaborator can bring the first idea to a writing session: a melody, bass-facing riff, chord movement, countermelody or formal contrast.

**With strings A:** select complementary instruments, registers and functions. A nylon accompaniment and steel-string melody can coexist when their attacks and phrases remain distinct. Two full-range strum tracks require a specific reason.

**With bass:** agree on note lengths, shared accents and deliberate gaps. Occasional unison can become the hook; constant low-register duplication can obscure both parts.

**With keys:** divide harmonic responsibility. One player can move the harmony while the other sustains a color, answers or rests.

**With winds and pitched percussion:** stop accompaniment or reduce it when an exposed melody needs room. A guitar answer should finish before the next lead entrance unless overlap is intentionally written.

**With percussion and producer:** identify the groove leader for each section. The owner-reported addition of **Percussion Factory** supplies another candidate source for wood, shaker or hand-percussion conversations. Its installed version, exact programs and articulation response have not been natively auditioned here. The current public product page redirects to version 2; that does not establish the owner’s installed version. [UVI product page](https://www.uvi.net/percussion-factory)

Warm lo-fi rhythm remains the shared foundation. Purposeful electronic colors can remain clear and modern. Metal-style kit production is outside the current default palette.

## Physical performance and overdubs

One player has two hands and plays one selected instrument at a time.

- Check chord shapes, stretches, position changes and string occupancy.
- Notes on one string cannot sustain independently through a replacement note.
- Fingerstyle bass and melody can coexist when the fingering supports both.
- Continuous tremolo with independently timed accompaniment requires a documented playable technique or an overdub.
- Guitar-to-mandolin or tres changes need enough silence for a believable change, or a declared studio edit.
- Simultaneous nylon accompaniment and Sunbird melody assigned to this collaborator are **two recorded performances**. They can instead be divided between strings A and B.
- Record every overdub’s purpose, register and performer assignment. Stacking takes does not resolve unclear arranging roles.

### Tone and articulation

The existing nylon treatment uses HP 85 Hz, +1.1 dB at 220 Hz, −2.8 dB at 2.9 kHz, −3.5 dB shelf at 4.8 kHz and LP 8.5 kHz. Sunbird uses HP 65 Hz, +1 dB at 210 Hz, −2.6 dB at 2.7 kHz, −3.1 dB shelf at 5.2 kHz and LP 10.5 kHz.

These are **audition settings**, retained as reproducible comparisons. A later bass-register part may need different filtering. Use level-matched bypass checks to preserve useful body and attack.

Acousticsamples documents Sunbird’s solo, chord, pattern and MIDI modes, along with mutes, releases, slides and other sampled articulations. Exact local mappings and transitions still need verification. Record MIDI note numbers rather than relying on a DAW’s octave labels. [Sunbird specification](https://www.acousticsamples.net/sunbird)

For all programs:

- Confirm whether note-off actually controls decay.
- Use supported articulation changes instead of substituting envelope edits indiscriminately.
- Author phrase dynamics; random velocity variation is insufficient.
- Preserve intentional string overlap and damping.
- Verify round-robin behavior where available.
- Keep pattern engines subordinate to the written hook and form.

## Failure modes and acceptance questions

| Failure mode | Acceptance question |
|---|---|
| Guitar remains accompaniment throughout | Does the chosen song give this collaborator a meaningful compositional contribution or intentional rest? |
| Attractive fragments replace a hook | Can the approved tune be recognized at its return? |
| Constant strumming fills every gap | Are another player’s phrases and the child’s guidance audible? |
| Muted and ringing strokes sound interchangeable | Do supported articulations and note lengths communicate the written contrast? |
| Nylon becomes dull through filtering | Does warmth retain body, pitch and useful attack at matched loudness? |
| Sunbird is described or scored as nylon | Is its steel-string identity recorded consistently? |
| Mandolin/tres receive transplanted guitar parts | Are instrument-specific technique, tuning and context documented? |
| Automatic humanization creates loose timing | Do bass, guitar and percussion share an intentional pocket? |
| Layering conceals impossible performance | Is each take playable, with all overdubs declared? |
| An endless vamp substitutes for a song | Are section purposes, development, return and ending written? |
| Machine or AI evidence becomes acceptance | Are human, owner, device and child evidence still reported separately? |
