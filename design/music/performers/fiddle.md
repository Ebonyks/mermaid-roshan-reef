This fictional guest is a co-composer who can bring a melodic idea, an answering phrase, a harmonic line or an arranging suggestion. The chair can move between foreground, counterpoint, rhythmic support, sustained color and complete rest. Warmth, recognizable song material and graceful exchanges take priority over virtuoso display.

The historical musicians below are references, not project participants. Their documented abilities do not establish the abilities of the fictional performer or the installed instruments. No fresh reference listening or transcription was performed for this profile.

## Instrument knowledge and practical boundaries
Use sounding pitch throughout, with **C4 = MIDI note 60**. Record MIDI numbers where octave names differ between applications.

| Instrument | Physical basis | Proposed working envelope |
|---|---|---|
| Violin/fiddle | Standard tuning is G–D–A–E, low to high. Violin and fiddle describe the same basic instrument in different playing contexts. [Yamaha instrument guide](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/violin/mechanism/mechanism002.html) | Plan routine material within **G3–D6**, usually **D4–B5** for a clear melodic center. These are proposed arranging limits, not the violin’s total range or a verified preset range. Favor first through third positions; identify higher-position passages deliberately. |
| Bass viola da gamba, if selected | The viol family has multiple sizes. A conventional six-string bass tuning is **D2–G2–C3–E3–A3–D4**; a seventh string can extend below D2. Viol tuning follows fourths with a central major third. [VdGSA tuning guide](https://www.vdgsa.org/about-the-viol-sizes-and-tunings), [composer guide](https://www.vdgsa.org/_files/ugd/d4b545_fba5a064eaad4df0b0ec9005c26cd76c.pdf) | Provisionally plan **D2–A4**, with a narrower register selected for each cue. This assumes a bass viol and remains conditional on the actual library instrument. Do not write below D2 or claim a seven-string instrument without evidence. |

A violinist’s positions, string crossings and bow distribution must support the phrase. The fictional player should understand alternative fingerings for the same pitch, choose open or stopped strings intentionally, and avoid a succession of large shifts merely because MIDI permits them. A higher register earns its place through the song’s shape.

Gamba is a distinct instrument family, commonly played vertically with frets and an underhand bow grip. Its fingerboard geometry, tuning and bow-weight relationships require separate preparation. [Viola da Gamba Society](https://vdgs.org.uk/aboutviols/) Grappelli’s violin background establishes no gamba expertise.

For a modern mixed ensemble, explicitly document the selected tuning reference. The VdGSA composer guide discusses A=415 practice; that does not require detuning a UVI program or the whole Roshan ensemble. Verify the actual program against the cue’s tuning.

## Proposed technique and phrase vocabulary
The performer should understand the difference between a musical phrase and a single bow stroke. A long musical sentence may contain several discreet bow changes. Conversely, grouping notes under one bow affects their available weight and shape.

- **Bow-length planning:** mark the phrase destination, intended swell and places to replenish the bow. A loud sustained note consumes bow differently from a quiet one. Do not impose a rest at every bow change, or write an endless crescendo under one imagined bow.
- **Slurs:** connect selected notes within a stroke when the musical gesture benefits. Keep a separate phrase mark where necessary. A drawn MIDI overlap is not proof of a convincing slurred transition.
- **Détaché:** use separate alternating strokes for clear, connected rhythmic speech. It need not mean clipped or aggressive playing.
- **Staccato and short notes:** define the desired separation and weight. A shortened sustain sample, sampled short articulation and bouncing bow stroke are different resources.
- **Vibrato:** choose its presence, onset, width and intensity for the emotional purpose. Straight tone can make an intimate entrance; restrained vibrato may develop a sustained arrival. Open strings and stopped notes need different expectations.
- **Slides and ornaments:** reserve audible shifts, turns and grace gestures for named expressive moments. They should clarify a phrase rather than decorate every arrival.

These distinctions follow standard violin bowing terminology; actual implementation remains dependent on the selected program. [Vienna Symphonic Library violin reference](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/violin)

For gamba, begin with articulate individual strokes, short selected slurs and clear phrase endings. The VdGSA guide describes non-vibrato as common practice while allowing expressive vibrato; it also distinguishes the instrument’s bowing and fretted-register behavior. [VdGSA composer guide](https://www.vdgsa.org/_files/ugd/d4b545_fba5a064eaad4df0b0ec9005c26cd76c.pdf) The proposed Roshan player should learn that vocabulary independently, rather than render violin phrasing an octave lower.

## Rhythm, harmony and musical understanding
The chair needs a dependable internal pulse and an ear for the current groove leader. It should distinguish straight subdivisions, swing, compound meter and three-beat dance without applying one universal timing template.

In a jazz context, proposed skills include pickups, anticipations, delayed resolutions and selectively accented offbeats. Swing should be judged in relation to tempo and the rhythm section; a fixed long–short ratio is an audition setting, not a complete style. In an Afro-Caribbean setting, the player follows the percussion and bass conversation, learns any specifically chosen rhythmic cycle, and leaves their answering spaces intact. It must not place a generic swing feel over an unrelated groove.

Harmonic knowledge should include chord tones, guide-tone movement, modal centers, suspensions and clear resolution of non-chord tones. The player can suggest a counterline that connects changing harmonies without doubling the keyboard voicing. Chromatic approaches are available where the cue’s language supports them; continuous chromatic runs are not the default.

For chamber support, favor one intelligible line, contrary or oblique motion where useful, and rests at cadences owned by another voice. A sustained note should have a harmonic reason and a planned release. Avoid automatically holding a third above the melody throughout the song.

## Dynamics, composition and written-song discipline
The fictional performer should be able to originate a short idea, explain its emotional purpose, and develop it through rhythm, register, articulation or harmonic setting. Development must preserve the parts of an approved melody that the owner has accepted.

A useful writing process is:

1. Identify the existing hook or the idea proposed for review.
2. State it plainly before adding ornaments.
3. Define the contrasting section and its purpose.
4. Plan the next recognizable return.
5. Decide which player receives the handoff and where this chair rests.
6. Select any improvised alternatives and write the chosen performance.

Improvisation is a discovery tool and a bounded performance option. The completed arrangement still records phrase lengths, entrances, exits, dynamic shapes and returns. Fills must fit a reserved space; they cannot spill across the next lead entrance or a child-facing voice cue.

A phrase should have direction rather than a volume swell on every note. Use an audible arrival, a softer answer or a changed register when the form calls for contrast. Repeated sections should retain the hook while making a meaningful arranging change. A quieter section can become more intimate through fewer parts rather than a blanket low-pass filter.

The original DKC1 remains the project’s reference for location-specific orchestration, developing atmosphere and memorable returns. Its role does not require a bowed instrument in every location. Modern electronic color and the warm lo-fi foundation remain available around this chair.

## Collaboration, physical performance and overdubs
The guest contributes to arranging decisions with guitar, keys, winds, bass and percussion. Before adding a counterline, identify who owns the foreground. A handoff should name the outgoing phrase ending, incoming pickup and any intentional overlap.

One violin or gamba player supplies one principal bowed line. Playable adjacent-string double stops may be proposed, with fingering checked. Do not describe three or four independently sustained voices as one ordinary bow performance. A rolled chord and a sustained chord are different events.

Record additional harmony lines as another player or a **declared studio overdub**. Violin and gamba cannot sound simultaneously from this one fictional chair without such a declaration. An instrument change also needs practical time in the performance model, even when separate studio takes make it easy to assemble.

The player may rest for an entire section or cue. Space is part of the contribution. This chair should never be added solely to make a mix seem more “storybook.”

| Test | Required observation |
|---|---|
| Exact identity and recall | Record bank, exact program path, native state, software versions and hashes. Confirm saved-state recall. |
| Range | Find real sounding limits, silent zones, sample changes and weak registers. Compare them with the proposed playing range. |

## Tone and mix decisions
Aim for a warm, close, painted acoustic presence with enough bow definition to make the phrase intelligible. Avoid both a brittle soloist placed above the band and a blurred string pad that loses the tune.

Choose register, articulation and arrangement before corrective processing. During future listening:

- Check low-mid congestion around the violin body against guitar and keys; check gamba body against bass before removing useful warmth.
- Investigate upper-mid scrape or nasal emphasis only where it is actually heard. Do not pre-emptively scoop the presence that carries the melody.
- Use a restrained shared room where suitable. Check that attacks remain readable and tails do not fill intended rests.
- Compare processed and bypassed sound at matched level.
- Test the part in mono and on small speakers. Stereo width must not carry its essential identity.
- Check family-voice intelligibility in the complete mix, including sustained upper-register passages.

No frequency defect or EQ setting is claimed from numerical measurements. These are listening checks.

## Failure modes and acceptance questions
| Failure mode | Acceptance question |
|---|---|
| Impressive runs replace a tune | Can the listener recognize and remember the returning hook without following the fastest passage? |
| Every note has the same swell or vibrato | Does the phrase have one clear destination and a deliberate expressive shape? |
| Keyboard-like attacks expose the sample | Are repeated notes, connected notes and releases convincing in the actual register and tempo? |
| A “mono” preset is called legato without evidence | What exactly was heard and recorded in the transition comparison? |
| Fiddle plays constantly | Does removing a section of the part improve the song? Are complete rests written? |
| Competing counterlines obscure the lead | Can one foreground voice be identified at each entrance? |
| Gamba becomes a transposed violin | Are tuning, register, bowing, articulation and contextual reference independently specified? |
| One chair performs impossible simultaneous parts | Are double stops playable and overdubs explicitly declared? |
| Lo-fi processing removes the instrument’s identity | Does warmth survive without hiding articulation, dynamics or the approved melody? |
| The short audition is called a finished song | Does the arrangement contain a clear hook, defined development, contrast, return and ending? |
| Technical passes become acceptance claims | Are human listening, speech/mono, device, child and owner decisions recorded separately? |
