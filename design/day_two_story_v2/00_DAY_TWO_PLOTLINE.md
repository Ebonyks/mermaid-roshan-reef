## 1. The story in brief

### 1.1 In one sentence

On her birthday, Mermaid Roshan makes a party one little job at a time: she
sends every friend an invitation by rocket, finds a shy lamb to be the star of
the stuffies' show, and plays her drums in a battle of the bands, and when the
Ember King cheats and takes her rainbow candle for his own party, what we make
together can't be taken.

### 1.2 The story on one page

The morning after Roshan cleaned Pearl Castle, she wakes in the attic, in the
same pile of bubbles where Book One ended. Baby Eagle chirps, Grand Puff
bounces on her tail, and Daddy smiles: "Wake up, birthday girl!"

In the Main Hall Daddy unrolls a party plan: eight empty pictures. A rocket, a
tree, a basket of strawberries, a cake, a banner, a little stage with an empty
star in the middle, a drum and a candle. Along the bottom is a row of empty
frames, one for every friend who will come. "Let's make it together," he says.
"One little job at a time." Every job starts at the Opera House, where Roshan
practises. Then she does it for real, in the castle and on the Sky Lagoon, and
every job has a friend from Book One in it.

First, the invitations, because a party is for friends and friends have to be
told. At the Mermaid Pool, the seahorse she saved yesterday helps fill a little
bubble rocket with water from the rainbow waterfall. Roshan puts in one picture
invitation for each friend, naming them as they go ("For Evie!", "For Wacky
and Chuck!"), and holds to launch. The rocket rises through the pool and
bursts into invitation bubbles that float away to every friend, and on the
plan each friend's picture pops into its frame.

Baby Eagle's favourite tree in the Sky Lagoon meadow has orange spots on its
leaves, so Tree Doctor Roshan opens her Tree Book: which tree, which sick leaf,
which medicine. She carries the leaf spray to the tree and sprays, and the
same tree bursts into pink blossom: the party tree. In the strawberry grove she
counts four strawberries, and a dust bunny rolls the fifth away: "We were just
playing!" Baby Eagle spots it. In the Royal Kitchen Daddy ties his big apron on
her, and she bakes a six-layer rainbow cake with the five strawberries on top.
A little floury nose sniffs the cake and bounces away, dropping a lavender egg.
In the Craft Room, on the table she scrubbed yesterday, she paints her birthday
banner in the colour she chose yesterday.

In the Stuffie Playroom, Kitty and Bunny want to put on a ballet show at the
party, but a show needs a star, and there is no one to be the star. Roshan
follows the clues (a tuft of wool, floury bounce marks, a tiny "Baa!") to
Lamma, the shy lamb who has been hiding since Day One. She gives back the
lavender egg and asks: "Will you be our star? There's room for everyone."
Lamma nods, her picture fills the empty star on the plan, and the Ballerina job
can begin: Roshan learns the dance on the Opera stage and rehearses it with the
stuffies, Lamma in the middle. On the Opera Hall stage, Roshan picks up her
Pop Star drumsticks. Daddy tunes his ukulele, Baby Eagle holds his bass, and
the family band practises their song, Iko Iko. In the Royal Library, the magic
storybook glows, and Detective Roshan finds the rainbow candle in its pages.
Every picture on the plan is full.

In the middle meadow of the Sky Lagoon, where the playground stood, the party
is ready: a stage, the blooming tree, the cake on its pedestal, and every
friend who got an invitation. Lamma dances the star part in the stuffies'
show. Daddy lights the candle, and rainbow light spills over every face. Then
the Ember King climbs onto the stage with his son, carrying a big guitar. It
is nearly the King's birthday too, and he wants the brightest party in the
world: "That light belongs at a KING'S party!" "Father," says the Prince, "it's
HER birthday." "Then let's see whose band is best!" Roshan feels small, but she
has her band. She drums, Daddy strums and Baby Eagle plucks, through the King's
loud guitar and the Prince's enormous drum kit, all the way to the end of their
song. She did it. Then the King cheats: his royal magic lifts the candle off
the cake and into his hand. "You said the best band wins!" says the Prince,
but he follows his father. "I'm sorry," he whispers, and they are gone.

"He took our light." Lamma, the star of the show, brings Roshan the party hat
that the King's last loud chord blew away. Daddy holds her. The tree, the
cake, the banner, the show, her band and all her friends are still here. "You
can still make a wish," says Daddy. Roshan closes her eyes and wishes. At
home, a secret door in the castle wakes with a soft rainbow glow, as if the
castle heard her. After birthday supper (cake!), a movie of the day and a
sleepover with every friend, Roshan whispers, "We'll find our light together."

### 1.5 The rules this story keeps

- **Book One is canon.** Its quotes are quoted exactly ("There was room for
  everyone."), Grand Puff is restored "to his own rainbow self", the dust
  bunnies are endearing pests who say sorry, and Baby Eagle was trapped under
  two dust bunnies, never a blanket.
- **A four-year-old non-reader plays this.** One finger, no fail states, no
  timers against her, every objective spoken and pointed at, waiting never
  wins, nothing scary.
- **Each job does what the records say it does.** The Astronaut sends the
  invitations, the Pop Star plays the family band's Iko Iko, the Ballerina's
  show has no star until Lamma joins, and the Detective finds the candle (the
  owner's words of 2026-09-30, and the records they point to:
  [01, section 12](01_STORY_BIBLE.md#12-the-job-canon-on-record)). A job earns
  its place by what the party would miss without it (the August party-function
  rule), never by a need invented to fill a table.
- **The Ember King** is loud, not cruel. He wants the candle for his own
  birthday party, because he wants the brightest party and everyone looking
  at him. He takes only the candle, by royal magic, after Roshan's band has
  played its song to the end. Nothing suggests his taking it is fair.
- **The Prince** is kind and conflicted. He plays drums in his father's band,
  speaks up for Roshan once, objects to the cheat, apologises and leaves with
  his father. No romance in Chapter 2.
- **Rumi** has no voice and gets no new art (IP hold).
- **Grand Puff, the rainbow friend,** has no wings, so he hops, and no voice.
- **Daddy's real recordings** (`daddy1.ogg` to `daddy3.ogg`) are never
  edited; his new lines use the Daddy-only synthetic filler voice.
- **No imps on Day Two.** The Opera career imps stay in the Opera shows.
  Chapter 2 story and tutorial runs get no imp and no contest (rule C13 of the
  imp-contest handoff on `dev`,
  `docs/handoffs/codex_opera_imp_contest_2026-09-30/CONTEST_DESIGN.md`; and
  the design language's new Opera-contest rule on `dev`: "Chapter 2 story and tutorial runs opt out"). The Ember
  King is Day Two's only antagonist.
- **The band's song is never edited.** The band plays the published Iko Iko
  v89 master as it is: no song edits, retiming, invented lyrics or replacement
  voices (the battle-of-the-bands commission). Whether the composition may ship
  in the game is still open ([decision 15](README.md#owner-decisions)).
- **Day One's story clips are never edited** (`DL-CIN-16`); Lamma's Day One
  moments happen in the rooms, between clips.
- **Protected art** (book art, family voices, friend portraits) is never
  altered.
- **The build's causal chain is kept, with the invitations first:** the
  invitations go out before anything else; the berries, then the one cake; the
  candle found last, lit only at the party and taken by the King. The cake and
  everything else stay.

### 2.2 How each scene is written

Each scene follows the same pattern, so any one can be lifted out and built:

- **Story:** why it happens, in plain words.
- **Where and who.**
- **Beats:** a numbered table of what the child sees, what she does, what is
  said, and the picture cue that tells a non-reader what to do.
- **Challenges:** which challenge slots the scene has (section 5).
- **After:** what changes in the world, what is saved, what the child sees
  next.
- **Book:** the Book Two pages it anchors.

Voice lines are exact and shown in quotes. A line marked *(new)* needs
recording; a line without that mark already exists as a recording, or (for
the Tree Book) is specified word for word in the Arborist handoff.

### 2.3 The routine: practise, then do it for real

The owner's formula: the Opera House introduces each job's mechanics, and the
second level happens in the game world, reskinned to fit the plot. Daddy
makes it part of the story in Act I: *"First we practise on the Opera stage.
Then we do it for real!"* After that, every job runs the same four steps, so
the child learns the rhythm of the day:

1. **The plan.** Daddy's Party Plan shows the next empty picture, glowing.
   Daddy names it: *"Next, the cake!"* Grand Puff hops to the right door, and
   that door lights up.
2. **The practice (L1).** Roshan touches the job's card in that room, and the
   Opera show opens: a short cut of the existing career show, with its
   stations, its gestures and its exact stage recordings. It teaches only
   the moves the real level will use. It is short (two to four steps), it
   has no rival race, and it ends with a quick bow. If she already has that
   career's star from freeplay, the practice shrinks to one warm-up step. It
   is never skipped silently.
3. **For real (L2).** The show's curtain opens back onto the real room (or
   its door leads outside), and Roshan does the job there, in costume, with
   the same moves, now meaning something in the story. A friend is there with
   her.
4. **The plan again (R-beat).** The finished piece appears where it belongs
   in the world. Daddy arrives with the plan, and the empty picture fills with
   what she made. Roshan says what the party has now (the job's function
   line). Then Daddy says his line from Book One: *"One little job at a
   time!"* The next picture glows.

**No imp at the practices.** In freeplay the Opera career shows have imp
rivals. The Day Two practices run through the `chapter2_tutorial` path, which
already skips the rival and the curtain call, and they stay imp-free: the
imp-contest handoff on `dev` gives Chapter 2 story and tutorial runs no imp and
no contest (its rule C13), and the design language's new Opera-contest rule on
`dev` records the same opt-out.
Chapter 2's antagonist is the Ember King.

job, a change you can see. Day Two keeps that promise. Every place the child
visits has a story of its own, tied to what happened there in Book One, and
every place changes as the day goes on.

## 4. People: who they are on Day Two

Each character has something they want today, a small arc, a way they appear
in play, and a voice rule.

**Mermaid Roshan.**
- **Wants:** the best party ever, with everyone she loves part of it.
- **Arc:** the birthday girl who makes her own party; the friend who invites
  every friend; the drummer who plays her song all the way to the end; the sad
  one who is held; the hopeful one who wishes.
- **In play:** she does every job herself, in the room, visibly. In the real
  levels she wears the approved Roshan look with a small costume piece per
  job (decision 10): a helmet, a sun hat and satchel, a straw hat, Daddy's
  apron, a painter's smock, a tutu, her Pop Star outfit (she drums in it, with
  two sticks and no microphone, as in the bands commission) and a detective
  cap.
- **Voice:** the existing synthetic Roshan voice. Short, bright lines.
- **Never:** hurts anyone, gives up, or is told off.

**Daddy Mermaid.**
- **Wants:** to give her a day she makes herself.
- **Arc:** planner (the Party Plan), coach (one demonstration when she needs
  it), helper (holds the basket, the banner, the heavy book), her bandmate on
  the ukulele, and the one who holds her when the candle is taken.
- **In play:** the Party Plan beat after every job; the Coach card in the
  challenge mix (section 5); the ukulele in the family band (`J7`, `F5`);
  lighting the candle (`F3`, decision 14); the hug in `F7`. His portrait moves
  only as a whole (slides, bobs, leans); pointing, the apron and the hug are
  art gaps. His ukulele-playing poses exist only as the bands commission's
  scene candidates (`BAND-03`, `BAND-07`), awaiting approval.

## 5. The challenge mix

The owner asked that Daddy, the dust bunnies (including the rainbow one) and
Baby Eagle be mixed into the challenges at random. Each real level (`Jn-L2`)
has a fixed story spine and two or three **challenge slots**, and each slot
draws one card from that job's deck.

### 5.1 The four roles

| Role | Who | What they do | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mischief** | A dust bunny; sometimes Grand Puff (a lucky accident) | Makes one small, funny mess the child fixes with the level's own gesture | A bunny rolls a strawberry toward the pond |
| **Helper** | Baby Eagle | Spots or carries something | He points at the hidden berry from the sky |
| **Coach** | Daddy | Shows the gesture once, holds something, counts along | "Watch me: round and round!" |
| **Hint** | Grand Puff | Appears only if the child hesitates; makes the right thing shimmer with rainbow light | He bounces beside the right card |

### 5.2 The rules

1. **First play is the book.** The first time through, each job uses its
   "book" card, the one Book Two prints. So the story the child hears at
   bedtime is the story she played.
2. **Replays are random.** Later plays draw from the deck with a seed saved
   per save file (`chapter2_challenge_seed`, new and additive). The same card
   never appears twice in a row for a job.
3. **Everyone gets a turn.** Across one full Day Two, Daddy, Baby Eagle, the
   dust bunnies and Grand Puff each appear in at least three jobs, and no job
   has two Mischief cards.
4. **Every card is gentle and short.** A visible cause (a wiggle, a chirp, a
   sneeze), one child action using the level's own gesture, a kind line, a
   visible result. At most about ten seconds.
5. **Nothing is ever lost.** A rolled-away strawberry is only moved; paint
   paw prints become part of the art. No card can undo progress or fail.
6. **Nothing stalls.** If the child does nothing for 8 seconds, the Helper
   or Coach fixes it for her (Baby Eagle brings the berry back).
7. **Never in Lamma's moments, never during the battle of the bands.** The
   finale is authored, not random.
8. **Save:** each card seen is recorded in `chapter2_challenge_seen` (new,
   additive), so replays and probes can prove variety.

### 5.3 Card format

Each job's deck (in section 8) lists its cards with the same fields: the card
ID, the role, when it can appear, what happens, what the child does, the
lines, and how it ends. Deck IDs are `Jn-DECK-<letter><number>`: D for a dust
bunny, P for Grand Puff, E for Baby Eagle, M for Daddy Mermaid. The card
marked **book** is the one used on the first play.

## 9. Act III: The party

The party is the day's payoff and its one sad moment. It has to do four
things, in this order:

1. **Show her everything she made.** The child's own work, and the friends
   who came because she invited them, all in one place.
2. **Light the candle, and play.** The brightest, warmest moment of the day,
   then her band's song.
3. **Let a loud guest take one thing.** The Ember King is a big, loud, silly,
   unfair guest, not a monster. Roshan's band plays its song to the end, and
   the loss is his choice afterwards, never her failure.
4. **Hold her.** Everything else and everyone stays; the shyest friend is
   brave for her; she makes a wish; the castle answers.

The contest is the owner's battle of the bands (2026-09-20): Roshan on drums,
Daddy on ukulele and Baby Eagle on bass, against the Ember King on lead guitar
and the Prince on an enormous drum kit, to the recorded Iko Iko, in the middle
meadow where the playground stood (the same middle screen the lawn alpha
already uses). It replaces the stomp-and-dodge protection
rounds of the lawn alpha; the bands commission says so itself ("This
supersedes the earlier stomp/dodge and birthday-lawn staging direction"). Its
prototype and fourteen scene candidates are on branch
`codex/battle-of-bands-20260920` (`design/BATTLE_OF_BANDS_2026-09-20.md`), not
yet on `dev`, where the open question `OQ-BANDS-VS-LAWN` still keeps the lawn
finale by default ([decision 15](README.md#owner-decisions)).

[04](04_FINALE_PARTY_CHAPTER.md) has the production detail (animation, voices,
staging numbers). This section is the story, beat by beat.

## 11. The Ember King and the Prince

### 11.1 Who they are

Both identities are locked: the owner's King reference and the V4 concept for
the King; the recovered identity card for the Prince, who stands exactly
four-fifths of his father's height
(`assets_src/cinematics/chapter2_lawn_scale_v2_2026-09-06/characters/REFERENCE_LOCK.json`).

**The Ember King** is a stocky red turtle-dragon with a cream muzzle, amber
eyes, an obsidian crown and a shell with glowing orange seams, a black spiky
mane, charcoal armour with a chain belt, and an enormous aubergine cape. In
the battle of the bands he plays lead electric guitar.

**The Prince** is lanky and coral-red, with an asymmetrical black fringe,
short obsidian horns, a long split coat, and an ember-heart clasp that
glows. In the battle of the bands he plays a comically elaborate metal drum
kit: many drums and cymbals, and exactly one kick drum. The crowned-flame
family emblem on his kit is a trial, not established canon.

### 11.2 What the King wants

- **The brightest party in the world, for his own birthday.** This is canon
  (the production spine's story promise): he takes the candle because he
  wants it for his own birthday party. His deepest want is recognition: the
  biggest entrance, the brightest light, everyone looking at him. He mistakes
  being in charge for owning things.
- **He wants the light, not the friends it is for.** Roshan's party is for
  the friends she invited, and every piece of it is made for them
  ([what each job gives the party](#what-each-job-gives-the-party)). He wants
  the candle for himself. The difference is shown, never spoken, and it does
  not make his taking fair.
- **He expects easy admiration.** He starts a battle of the bands because he
  assumes his band will win. When Roshan's band plays its song all the way to
  the end, he changes the terms. That is the cheat, and it is plainly unfair.
- **Why his parties need a stolen light** is a story for a later chapter.
  Chapter 2 plants only the Prince's look back.

### 11.3 How the King behaves

- **Speech:** short, loud declarations. "Make way!" "Then let's see whose band
  is best!" "Come, son." He never threatens to hurt anyone, never mocks
  Roshan, never says who she is or isn't.
- **Acting:** theatrical. A heavy, slow entrance; a big guitar flourish with an
  open-mouthed growl on his breaks (`BAND-05`, `BAND-11`); one sweep of royal
  magic for the theft. His indignation can be funny.
- **Distance:** his place is the far side of the stage, behind his guitar. He
  never looms over the guests or crosses into them.
- **Menace level:** his design has sharp cues (horns, tusks, claws). Keep
  them from frightening by acting, distance and a theatrical voice, never by
  redesign.

### 11.4 What the Prince wants

- **To be included.** He came because his father brought him, out of family
  loyalty, not because he wants the candle. He never watches, reaches for or
  admires the candle; he looks at the people first.
- **He admires Roshan's courage and her music**, not her looks.
- **His arc in one afternoon:** curious ("You made that?"), then quietly on
  her side ("Father, it's HER birthday"), then playing his drums for his
  father's band, then admiring despite himself (the one clap, "She did it,
  Father"), then objecting ("You said the best band wins!"), then ashamed
  ("I'm sorry"), and looking back once as he leaves with his father.
- **Never:** romance, hearts or blushing in Chapter 2. Later affection must
  come from his own repeated choices to make amends.
