## 1. The story in brief

### 1.1 In one sentence

On her birthday, Mermaid Roshan makes a party one little job at a time with
the friends she helped yesterday, finds a shy lamb who joins the stuffie team,
and keeps everyone safe when the Ember King takes her rainbow candle for his
own party, because what we make together can't be taken.

### 1.2 The story on one page

The morning after Roshan cleaned Pearl Castle, she wakes in the attic, in the
same pile of bubbles where Book One ended. Baby Eagle chirps, Grand Puff
bounces on her tail, and Daddy smiles: "Wake up, birthday girl!"

In the Main Hall Daddy unrolls a party plan: eight empty pictures. A tree, a
basket of strawberries, a cake, a banner, dancing stuffies, a microphone, a
rocket and a candle. Along the bottom are the friends he invited at dawn, and
one empty frame: "There's always room for one more." "Let's make it
together," he says. "One little job at a time." Every job starts at the
Opera House, where Roshan practises on stage. A little ember imp keeps
sneaking into the shows to take notes ("I was sent to learn the CAKE!"), but
nobody knows who sent him. Then she does each job for real, in the castle and
on the Sky Lagoon. Every job helps a friend from Book One, and every piece is
for someone at the party: shade for Faron's baby, cake for Evie, a dance for
Harper and Fiona, a song that reaches Kareem at the back.

Baby Eagle's favourite tree on the Sky Lagoon lawn is sick, so Tree Doctor
Roshan opens her Tree Book, waters its roots and bandages its broken branch
until it bursts into pink blossom: the party tree. In the strawberry grove she
counts four strawberries, and a dust bunny rolls the fifth away: "We were just
playing!" Baby Eagle spots it. In the Royal Kitchen Daddy ties his big apron
on her, and she bakes a six-layer rainbow cake with the five strawberries on
top. A little floury nose sniffs the cake and bounces away, dropping a
lavender egg. In the Craft Room, on the table she scrubbed yesterday, she
paints her birthday banner in the colour she chose yesterday.

In the Stuffie Playroom, Kitty and Bunny want to dance at the party, but a
stuffie dance needs one more dancer. Roshan follows the clues (a tuft of
wool, floury bounce marks, a tiny "Baa!") to Lamma, the shy lamb who has been
hiding since Day One. She gives back the lavender egg and asks her to dance:
"There's room for everyone." Lamma fills the empty frame: she is the one
more. On the Opera stage, Rumi teaches Roshan the castle's birthday song, then
remembers something from hundreds of years ago: a rainbow candle. At the
Mermaid Pool, water from the rainbow waterfall Roshan freed fills a little
rocket that can make a spark. In the Royal Library, the magic storybook gives
up the candle. Every picture on the plan is full.

On the lawn, under the blossoms, everyone is waiting. The rocket's spark
lights the candle, rainbow light spills over every face, and the birthday
song begins. In the hedge an ember imp gasps and runs. Stomp. Stomp. STOMP.
The Ember King marches up the hill with his son. It is nearly the King's
birthday too, and he wants the brightest party in the world. "That light
belongs at a KING'S party!" "Father," says the Prince, "it's HER birthday."
Roshan feels small, but she can keep her friends safe. The dust bunnies make
a soft wall, Baby Eagle brings the banner as a roof, and Grand Puff puffs his
rainbow over everyone. She wins. Then the King cheats: he reaches over the
rainbow and takes only the candle. "I'm sorry," whispers the Prince, and they
are gone.

"He took our light." Lamma, the shyest friend of all, brings Roshan the party
hat she lost. Daddy holds her. The tree, the cake, the banner, the song and
all her friends are still here. "You can still make a wish," says Daddy.
Roshan closes her eyes and wishes. As they walk home at sunset, a secret door
in the castle wakes with a soft rainbow glow, as if the castle heard her. After
birthday supper (cake!), a movie of the day and a sleepover with every friend,
Roshan whispers, "We'll find our light together."

### 1.5 The rules this story keeps

- **Book One is canon.** Its quotes are quoted exactly ("There was room for
  everyone."), Grand Puff is restored "to his own rainbow self", the dust
  bunnies are endearing pests who say sorry, and Baby Eagle was trapped under
  two dust bunnies, never a blanket.
- **A four-year-old non-reader plays this.** One finger, no fail states, no
  timers against her, every objective spoken and pointed at, waiting never
  wins, nothing scary.
- **Every job meets a guest's need.** A job earns its place by what the party
  would miss without it, not by the object it makes (the owner-reviewed
  August party-function rule; see
  [what each job gives the party](#what-each-job-gives-the-party)).
- **The Ember King** is loud, not cruel. He wants the candle for his own
  birthday party, because he wants the brightest party and everyone looking
  at him. He takes only the candle. Nothing suggests his taking it is fair.
- **The Prince** is kind and conflicted. He helps once, objects once,
  apologises and leaves with his father. No romance in Chapter 2.
- **Rumi** has no voice and gets no new art (IP hold).
- **Grand Puff, the rainbow friend,** has no wings, so he hops, and no voice.
- **Daddy's real recordings** (`daddy1.ogg` to `daddy3.ogg`) are never
  edited; his new lines use the Daddy-only synthetic filler voice.
- **Day One's story clips are never edited** (`DL-CIN-16`); Lamma's Day One
  moments happen in the rooms, between clips.
- **Protected art** (book art, family voices, friend portraits) is never
  altered.
- **The build's causal chain is kept:** berries, then the one cake, then the
  banner and dance and song, then the rocket, then the candle found last, lit
  only at the party, taken by the King. The cake and everything else stay.

### 2.2 How each scene is written

Each scene follows the same pattern, so any one can be lifted out and built:

- **Story:** why it happens, in plain words.
- **Where and who.**
- **Beats:** a numbered table of what the child sees, what she does, what is
  said, and the picture cue that tells a non-reader what to do.
- **Challenges:** which challenge slots the scene has (section 5).
- **After:** what changes in the world, what is saved, what the child sees
  next.
- **Book:** the Book Two pages it anchors.

Voice lines are exact and shown in quotes. A line marked *(new)* needs
recording; a line without that mark already exists as a recording, or (for
the Tree Book) is specified word for word in the Arborist handoff.

### 2.3 The routine: practise, then do it for real

The owner's formula: the Opera House introduces each job's mechanics, and the
second level happens in the game world, reskinned to fit the plot. Daddy
makes it part of the story in Act I: *"First we practise on the Opera stage.
Then we do it for real!"* After that, every job runs the same four steps, so
the child learns the rhythm of the day:

1. **The plan.** Daddy's Party Plan shows the next empty picture, glowing.
   Daddy names it: *"Next, the cake!"* Grand Puff hops to the right door, and
   that door lights up.
2. **The practice (L1).** Roshan touches the job's card in that room, and the
   Opera show opens: a short cut of the existing career show, with its
   stations, its gestures and its exact stage recordings. It teaches only
   the moves the real level will use. It is short (two to four steps), it
   has no rival race, and it ends with a quick bow. If she already has that
   career's star from freeplay, the practice shrinks to one warm-up step. It
   is never skipped silently.
3. **For real (L2).** The show's curtain opens back onto the real room (or
   its door leads outside), and Roshan does the job there, in costume, with
   the same moves, now meaning something in the story. A friend is there who
   needs it.
4. **The plan again (R-beat).** The finished piece appears where it belongs
   in the world. Daddy arrives with the plan, and the empty picture fills with
   what she made. The friend it is for hops into the picture, and Roshan says
   what the party has now (the job's function line). Then Daddy says his line
   from Book One: *"One little job at a time!"* The next picture glows.

**The imp apprentice.** At each practice a little ember imp is already on
stage, pretending not to be there. He is one of the Ember King's helpers,
sent to learn how to make a birthday party, although nobody says so until the
party. His lines already exist as recordings (`imp_op_<career>_arrive`,
`_copy`, `_steal`, `_bop`):

1. When the show opens: *"I was sent to learn the CAKE. Don't mind me, I'm
   learning."*
2. Once, halfway: a copy line, a comic mistake. *"Flour goes in the bowl... or
   on my head. Either way!"*
3. Just before the bow: he grabs the job's prop with his steal line, the
   child taps him ("Boop!"), he drops it with his bop line and scurries off
   stage, clutching a scribbled note. It never costs anything and cannot
   fail; if the child does nothing for 5 seconds, he trips over his own tail
   and drops it.

Eight practices plant the same small mystery: someone, somewhere, is
learning how to make a party. It pays off in F3.

job, a change you can see. Day Two keeps that promise. Every place the child
visits has a story of its own, tied to what happened there in Book One, and
every place changes as the day goes on.

## 4. People: who they are on Day Two

Each character has something they want today, a small arc, a way they appear
in play, and a voice rule.

**Mermaid Roshan.**
- **Wants:** the best party ever, with everyone she loves part of it.
- **Arc:** the birthday girl who makes her own party; the friend who helps
  each helper; the brave one who keeps her friends safe; the sad one who is
  held; the hopeful one who wishes.
- **In play:** she does every job herself, in the room, visibly. In the real
  levels she wears the approved Roshan look with a small costume piece per
  job (decision 10): a sun hat and satchel, a straw hat, Daddy's apron, a
  painter's smock, a tutu, a headset, a helmet, a detective cap.
- **Voice:** the existing synthetic Roshan voice. Short, bright lines.
- **Never:** hurts anyone, gives up, or is told off.

**Daddy Mermaid.**
- **Wants:** to give her a day she makes herself.
- **Arc:** planner (the Party Plan), coach (one demonstration when she needs
  it), helper (holds the basket, the banner, the heavy book), and the one who
  holds her when the candle is taken.
- **In play:** the Party Plan beat after every job; the Coach card in the
  challenge mix (section 5); the hug in `F7`. His portrait moves only as a
  whole (slides, bobs, leans); pointing, the apron and the hug are art gaps.
- **Voice:** new lines use the manifest's Daddy-only synthetic filler voice.
  His real recordings are never edited. His signature line, from Book One:
  *"One little job at a time."*

**Baby Eagle.**
- **Wants:** his favourite tree to be well, and to help Roshan the way she
  helped him.
- **Arc:** worried ("Chirp, chirp!") to happy on his healed branch; then the
  day's spotter and carrier: he finds the lost strawberry, carries the
  banner to the tree, and in `F5` carries it again as a roof over the guests.
- **In play:** follows Roshan everywhere now, the Sky Lagoon and the lawn
  included. The Helper card in the challenge mix.
- **Voice:** his existing chirp ("Cheep cheep! Cheep!"). No words.

**Grand Puff, the rainbow friend.**
- **Wants:** to show everyone he is a friend now.
- **Arc:** the day's guide (he hops ahead to the next door); the rainbow hint
  when she hesitates; the maker of happy rainbow accidents; in `F5`, the
  rainbow dome over everyone. This pays off Book One's title line: *"Your
  rainbow was there all along!"*
- **In play:** follows Roshan. The Hint (always available) and sometimes the
  Mischief card, but his mischief is always a lucky accident. He hops; he has
  no wings.
- **Voice:** none. A soft happy "boing" sound when he hops (new sound
  effect). Roshan calls him Grand Puff.

**The playful dust bunnies.**
- **Want:** to play. Today they learn to play gently, and to help.
- **Arc:** they start with tricks (rolling away the fifth strawberry), say
  sorry ("We were just playing!", Book One's own line), and become helpers
  (carrying berries, sweeping flour). In `F5` they make the first shelter:
  a soft wall in front of the guests. At night they come to supper.
- **In play:** the Mischief card in most jobs. Three recur. In this document
  they have working names so designers can tell them apart: **Splash** (the
  bath bunny from Book One p5) and **the Tumble twins** (the two who tumbled
  onto Baby Eagle, p17). The game and the book call them "the dust bunnies"
  unless the owner approves the names.
- **Voice:** new small voices for three lines at most (*"We were just
  playing!"*, *"Sorry!"*, *"We can help!"*), or giggle sounds. Book Two uses
  speech balloons, as Book One does.
- **Never:** dirty, scary or popped. On Day Two nobody pops a dust bunny.

**Rumi.**
- **Wants:** to share the castle's old birthday song and what she remembers
  of it.
- **Arc:** teaches the song (`J6`), remembers the rainbow candle, sings at
  the party, and brings the song back softly at the end.
- **In play:** existing frames only (idle, wave, swim); IP hold. She
  "speaks" through music and a thought-bubble picture; Roshan says what
  Rumi shows.
- **Voice:** none, by rule.

**The seahorse.**
- **Wants:** to help the girl who pulled the trash from its mouth yesterday.
- **In play:** in `J7` it sprays rainbow water into the rocket's tank. The

## 5. The challenge mix

The owner asked that Daddy, the dust bunnies (including the rainbow one) and
Baby Eagle be mixed into the challenges at random. Each real level (`Jn-L2`)
has a fixed story spine and two or three **challenge slots**, and each slot
draws one card from that job's deck.

### 5.1 The four roles

| Role | Who | What they do | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mischief** | A dust bunny; sometimes Grand Puff (a lucky accident) | Makes one small, funny mess the child fixes with the level's own gesture | A bunny rolls a strawberry toward the pond |
| **Helper** | Baby Eagle | Spots or carries something | He points at the hidden berry from the sky |
| **Coach** | Daddy | Shows the gesture once, holds something, counts along | "Watch me: round and round!" |
| **Hint** | Grand Puff | Appears only if the child hesitates; makes the right thing shimmer with rainbow light | He bounces beside the right card |

### 5.2 The rules

1. **First play is the book.** The first time through, each job uses its
   "book" card, the one Book Two prints. So the story the child hears at
   bedtime is the story she played.
2. **Replays are random.** Later plays draw from the deck with a seed saved
   per save file (`chapter2_challenge_seed`, new and additive). The same card
   never appears twice in a row for a job.
3. **Everyone gets a turn.** Across one full Day Two, Daddy, Baby Eagle, the
   dust bunnies and Grand Puff each appear in at least three jobs, and no job
   has two Mischief cards.
4. **Every card is gentle and short.** A visible cause (a wiggle, a chirp, a
   sneeze), one child action using the level's own gesture, a kind line, a
   visible result. At most about ten seconds.
5. **Nothing is ever lost.** A rolled-away strawberry is only moved; paint
   paw prints become part of the art. No card can undo progress or fail.
6. **Nothing stalls.** If the child does nothing for 8 seconds, the Helper
   or Coach fixes it for her (Baby Eagle brings the berry back).
7. **Never in Lamma's moments, never during the finale's rounds.** The finale's
   friend layers are authored, not random.
8. **Save:** each card seen is recorded in `chapter2_challenge_seen` (new,
   additive), so replays and probes can prove variety.

### 5.3 Card format

Each job's deck (in section 8) lists its cards with the same fields: the card
ID, the role, when it can appear, what happens, what the child does, the
lines, and how it ends. Deck IDs are `Jn-DECK-<letter><number>`: D for a dust
bunny, P for Grand Puff, E for Baby Eagle, M for Daddy Mermaid. The card
marked **book** is the one used on the first play.

**The rule.** A job does not earn its place by making an object. It earns it

## 9. Act III: The party

The party is the day's payoff and its one sad moment. It has to do four
things, in this order:

1. **Show her everything she made.** The child's own work, and the friends
   who helped, all in one place.
2. **Light the candle.** The biggest, brightest, warmest moment of the day.
3. **Let a loud guest take one thing.** The Ember King is a big, loud, silly,
   unfair guest, not a monster. The child wins every challenge, and the loss
   is his choice afterwards, never her failure.
4. **Hold her.** Everything else and everyone stays; the shyest friend is
   brave for her; she makes a wish; the castle answers.

## 11. The Ember King and the Prince

### 11.1 Who they are

Both identities are locked: the owner's King reference and the V4 concept for
the King; the recovered identity card for the Prince, who stands exactly
four-fifths of his father's height
(`assets_src/cinematics/chapter2_lawn_scale_v2_2026-09-06/characters/REFERENCE_LOCK.json`).

**The Ember King** is a stocky red turtle-dragon with a cream muzzle, amber
eyes, an obsidian crown and a shell with glowing orange seams, a black spiky
mane, charcoal armour with a chain belt, and an enormous aubergine cape.

**The Prince** is lanky and coral-red, with an asymmetrical black fringe,
short obsidian horns, a long split coat, and an ember-heart clasp that
glows.

### 11.2 What the King wants

- **The brightest party in the world, for his own birthday.** This is canon
  (the production spine's story promise): he takes the candle because he
  wants it for his own birthday party. His deepest want is recognition: the
  biggest entrance, the brightest light, everyone looking at him. He mistakes
  being in charge for owning things.
- **He sends helpers instead of making things.** His imps were "sent to
  learn" the cake, the decorating, the dancing and the singing, because his
  own parties are "very grey." Nobody in his kingdom makes a party together;
  he orders one. This is a seed for later chapters, about what his own party
  really needs. It is never spoken as an excuse, and nothing in Chapter 2
  suggests his taking the candle is fair.
- **He wants things, not the friends they are for.** Every piece Roshan
  makes is for someone ([what each job gives the party](#what-each-job-gives-the-party)).
  His imps learn what each piece looks like and never who it is for, and he
  wants the candle for himself: the brightest light, with everyone looking at
  him. The difference is shown, never spoken, and it does not make his
  taking fair.
- **He expects easy admiration.** He offers a challenge because he assumes
  he will win. When Roshan earns the real victory, he changes the terms. That
  is the cheat, and it is plainly unfair.

### 11.3 How the King behaves

- **Speech:** short, loud declarations. "Make way!" "Mine!" "Come, son." He
  never threatens to hurt anyone, never mocks Roshan, never says who she is
  or isn't.
- **Acting:** theatrical. Big stomps that puff dust, big wobbles when he loses
  his balance, a big dizzy spin under dizzy stars. His indignation can be
  funny.
- **Distance:** he never looms over the guests or crosses into their
  semicircle. His arena is the open lawn to the right.
- **Menace level:** his design has sharp cues (horns, tusks, claws). Keep
  them from frightening by acting, distance and a theatrical voice, never by
  redesign.

### 11.4 What the Prince wants

- **To be included.** He came because his father brought him, out of family
  loyalty, not because he wants the candle. He never watches, reaches for or
  admires the candle; he looks at the people first.
- **He admires Roshan's courage and resourcefulness**, not her looks.
- **His arc in one afternoon:** curious ("You made that?"), then quietly on
  her side ("Father, it's HER birthday"), then helping once for real ("Over
  here!"), then admiring despite himself (the one clap, "She did it,
  Father"), then objecting ("You promised a fair challenge!"), then ashamed
  ("I'm sorry"), and looking back once as he leaves with his father.
- **Never:** romance, hearts or blushing in Chapter 2. Later affection must
  come from his own repeated choices to make amends.
