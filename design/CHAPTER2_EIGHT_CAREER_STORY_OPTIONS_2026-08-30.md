# Chapter 2: Eight-Career Story Options

> **Sequence supersession (owner direction, 2026-08-30):** this is the ranked
> option record, not current production authority. See
> `CHAPTER2_EIGHT_CAREER_PRODUCTION_SPINE_2026-08-30.md` for Farmer-first,
> Candy Maker as final cake decoration, Astronaut-before-Detective, and the
> Detective candle reveal as the last required career beat.

Status: design selection for the next Chapter 2 implementation pass. The
owner's eight-career ceiling supersedes the current thirteen-contribution
template; runtime masks and probes must not be changed until the selected
story sequence is implemented together.

## Recommended roster

The eight careers with the strongest birthday causality, one-finger play,
existing asset reuse, and verb variety are:

1. Detective
2. Farmer
3. Pastry Chef
4. Candy Maker
5. Painter
6. Ballerina
7. Pop Star
8. Astronaut Engineer

This keeps the four already-authored opening identities (Chef, Detective,
Ballerina, and Candy Maker), adds the owner's Farmer and Pop Star examples,
and retains Painter and Astronaut for the two most visible final payoffs. The
five omitted Chapter 2 careers remain available elsewhere in the game:
Stuffie Surgeon overlaps Ballerina's stuffie problem, Magician overlaps party
entertainment, Boxer adds no required party object, Racer expands guest travel,
and Nursery Nurse adds a separate baby-care branch.

## Evaluation criteria

Ideas are ranked within each career using four questions:

- Does the game create or solve something the party visibly needs?
- Can a non-reader understand it through one large object and one-finger input?
- Can it reuse approved career mechanics, rooms, characters, art, and voices?
- Does it add a distinct verb instead of repeating another career's job?

Rank 1 is recommended. Rank 2 is a workable substitute. Rank 3 is viable but
adds overlap, weaker causality, or disproportionate production work.

## Detective Roshan

### 1. Find the unlit rainbow candle in the Library storybook — recommended

Roshan sweeps the magnifying glass over three oversized clues, matches the
rainbow wax bands on a tiny case board, then taps the glowing magic storybook.
The book opens and reveals the candle unlit.

Evaluation: strongest MacGuffin setup in the chapter. It gives the later rocket
and Ember King crash a reason to exist, reuses the approved Library hiding
place and Detective lens/match interactions, and needs no additional room.

### 2. Follow the missing strawberry-basket trail

Roshan follows red leaf-and-berry clues from the Castle gate to the Sky Lagoon,
revealing where Farmer Roshan should harvest.

Evaluation: a clean bridge to the outdoor sequence and easy to read, but it
uses the Detective before the chapter's central candle has been established.
It also makes Farmer's objective feel discovered rather than chosen.

### 3. Find Rumi's missing microphone

Roshan searches the Opera House wings and returns Rumi's microphone before the
sound check.

Evaluation: charming character connection and strong prop readability, but it
duplicates the Pop Star setup and makes the candle discovery require another
career or an unearned cutscene.

## Farmer Roshan

### 1. Gather Sky Lagoon strawberries for the cake — recommended

Ripe strawberries glow among low Sky Lagoon plants. Roshan taps five ripe
berries, drags the full basket onto the courtyard train, and hands it to Chef
Roshan at the Castle kitchen door.

Evaluation: excellent causal handoff into the cake, a welcome outdoor change of
scene, and simple tap/drag play. The Sky Lagoon and train already exist. The
only clear art gap is a small strawberry plant, berry pickup, and basket state.

### 2. Pick flowers for party crowns

Roshan selects three flower colours and places a crown on each stuffie guest.

Evaluation: gentle and readable, but it overlaps Painter's decorations and
Ballerina's stuffie staging. It also weakens the valuable Farmer-to-Chef chain.

### 3. Guide piggies carrying a picnic basket

Roshan swipes three piggies through a meadow path so they can deliver snacks to
the Castle.

Evaluation: maximizes reuse of existing Farmer assets and herding mechanics,
but moving several characters through the Sky Lagoon is broader than a small
party-prep game and makes the food less specific to Roshan's cake.

## Pastry Chef Roshan

### 1. Bake the gigantic strawberry birthday cake — recommended

The Farmer's berries arrive as the explicit ingredient. Roshan circles to mix,
taps the oven when it turns golden, then swipes frosting across three enormous
tiers and places the strawberries on top.

Evaluation: the clearest centerpiece payoff. It reuses existing mix, oven,
frost, and topping mechanics plus the new code-native giant cake. The ingredient
handoff makes the sequence feel like a story rather than eight unrelated jobs.

### 2. Bake one tiny cupcake for every stuffie

Roshan matches three frosting colours and places cupcakes at the stuffie table.

Evaluation: visually cute and age-appropriate, but it shrinks the party's main
spectacle and overlaps Candy Maker's individually wrapped treats.

### 3. Build a frozen rainbow ice-cream cake

Roshan stacks coloured frozen layers and keeps them cool until the party.

Evaluation: visually strong, but it requires new freezing art and logic, has no
existing Farmer ingredient dependency, and competes awkwardly with the candle
flame as the party's magical centerpiece.

## Candy Maker Roshan

### 1. Sort and wrap rainbow party-favour bags — recommended

Roshan pours one syrup batch, taps candies into three colour bins, then swipes
wrappers around eight little favour bags for the guests to take home.

Evaluation: distinct from the cake, strongly reuses the existing pour/sort/wrap
career modes, and leaves a visible row of completed bags on the Main Hall table.
It adds almost no new mechanic risk.

### 2. Make a rainbow candy crown for the cake

Roshan sorts jewel candies and snaps them into a crown-shaped cake topper.

Evaluation: a strong visual reward with simple snapping, but it divides
ownership of the cake centerpiece between Chef and Candy Maker.

### 3. Make edible microphones for the band

Roshan rolls candy sticks and adds sparkling microphone heads for Rumi and the
stuffies.

Evaluation: funny and memorable, but it overlaps Pop Star's real microphones,
requires new prop art, and is less believable as something the party needs.

## Painter Roshan

### 1. Paint and hang the Main Hall birthday banner — recommended

Roshan swipes colour across a cloudy banner, taps five large sea-star stamps,
then drags the finished banner onto two glowing wall hooks. The Main Hall
background visibly changes for every later preparation step.

Evaluation: strongest room-transformation payoff. It reuses Painter reveal and
stamp mechanics plus the existing Castle banner, and the result stays visible
without adding reading-dependent invitation text.

### 2. Paint picture invitations for the guests

Roshan stamps one character picture and one room picture onto each invitation.

Evaluation: picture-first and useful, but the invitations are small on a phone,
need delivery logic, and do not transform the party room.

### 3. Paint the candle-lighting rocket

Roshan adds rainbow stripes and stars to Astronaut Roshan's finished rocket.

Evaluation: a fun cross-career collaboration, but Painter cannot finish until
Astronaut has built the rocket, which weakens Astronaut's role as the final
pre-party payoff and adds a second dependency cycle.

## Ballerina Roshan

### 1. Teach the stuffies to dance and play together — recommended

In the Stuffie Room, Roshan demonstrates one pose, guides a glowing ribbon path,
and makes one grand circle. The stuffies mirror her, pair up, and bow together.

Evaluation: perfect character-scale storytelling with an immediate emotional
result. It reuses the approved stuffies and Ballerina pose/ribbon/twirl verbs,
and teaches cooperation rather than merely decorating the room.

### 2. Lead a stuffie welcome parade

Roshan taps a marching beat while the stuffies carry the favour bags into the
Main Hall.

Evaluation: nicely connects Candy Maker and the party room, but rhythm belongs
more clearly to Pop Star and the parade requires more path staging.

### 3. Balance plates in a cake-delivery dance

Roshan guides stuffies carrying plates around the giant cake without dropping
them.

Evaluation: visually comic, but it implies failure and danger around the cake,
adds collision/path complexity, and distracts from Ballerina's social role.

## Pop Star Roshan

### 1. Do a birthday-song sound check with Rumi — recommended

Rumi waits at the microphone in the Opera House. Roshan holds the microphone
until the rainbow note reaches Rumi, echoes Rumi's three-note musical phrase,
then taps two glowing dance cues. Rumi waves and the finished song becomes a
party-table music symbol.

Evaluation: the strongest relationship beat in the roster and a direct use of
the owner's example. It reuses Rumi's approved pose/swim atlases, Pop Star's
sound-check/rhythm mechanics, existing Roshan Pop Star recordings, and Opera
music. Rumi has no dedicated recorded dialogue, so she should answer with
animation and musical notes rather than substituted speech.

### 2. Rehearse a stuffie choir

Roshan taps three pitch pearls and the stuffies echo each phrase.

Evaluation: highly readable and asset-efficient, but it repeats Ballerina's
stuffie leadership and loses Rumi's established friendship payoff.

### 3. Compose the song by collecting notes around the Castle

Roshan visits three rooms, taps a room sound, and combines the notes onstage.

Evaluation: gives the Castle musical identity, but it adds navigation and
several audio authoring dependencies to what should be a small sequential game.

## Astronaut Engineer Roshan

### 1. Build the little rocket that lights the candle — recommended

Roshan connects three oversized pipes, taps three sparkling patches, turns one
large valve, and holds through a short countdown. The finished rocket waits
beside the cake; it launches only when the party starts and lights the large
rainbow flame.

Evaluation: the best final preparation because it reuses the existing
pipe/patch/valve/launch career, gives the candle a memorable ignition, and
creates the glowing object the Ember King wants for his own birthday party.

### 2. Launch picture invitations across the kingdom

Roshan loads guest portraits into a mail rocket and taps launch destinations.

Evaluation: visually clear and appropriately astronaut-like, but it duplicates
Painter's invitation option and creates off-screen delivery questions.

### 3. Inflate zero-gravity party balloons

Roshan connects an air hose and guides floating balloons onto ceiling hooks.

Evaluation: easy one-finger play and pretty room motion, but it overlaps the
Painter's decoration role and has much less story payoff than lighting the
candle.

## Recommended sequential chapter spine

The career tutorials may remain four-at-first for continuity, but tutorial
availability should not dictate story-use order. The eight plot games should
form one dependency chain:

1. Detective finds the unlit candle in the Library.
2. Farmer gathers strawberries in the Sky Lagoon.
3. Chef uses those strawberries to bake the gigantic cake.
4. Candy Maker prepares the guest favour bags.
5. Painter transforms the Main Hall with the birthday banner.
6. Ballerina teaches the stuffies their dance and cooperative play.
7. Pop Star completes the birthday-song sound check with Rumi.
8. Astronaut builds the candle-lighting rocket.
9. The party starts; the rocket lights the candle's large rainbow flame.
10. The Ember King takes the glowing candle for his own birthday party.

Each career game should target roughly 20–45 seconds, use two or three causal
beats, allow unlimited hints and retries, and produce one persistent picture or
room change. Do not require all thirteen Opera careers, normal Opera stars, text
reading, timers, adversarial races, or fail states for Chapter 2 completion.
