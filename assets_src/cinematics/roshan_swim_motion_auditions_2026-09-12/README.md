## Review and choose

1. Keep each original download unchanged. Record sample ID, take, prompt/input
   hashes, service/model/settings if disclosed, native FPS/dimensions/duration,
   creation time and SHA-256. Unknown seeds/settings stay null, never invented.
2. Watch each complete clip at normal speed, muted, in randomized order with
   neutral labels. Use an enlarged view and a 256px-high character comparison.
   Describe what she appears to want before reading the prompt. Reviewers do
   not see a desired score or the producer's score before judging.
3. Check identity, tail/fin topology, stable proportions, camera/scale, cropping,
   actual arm articulation and uninterrupted motion. Severe defects reject the
   take for direct use regardless of an attractive isolated moment.
4. Rate intention clarity, warmth/character specificity, buoyancy/propulsion,
   rhythm/spacing, arm-body integration and overall normal-speed appeal. Use
   concrete timestamps. 3 means readable but generic; 4 strong with visible
   limitations; 4.5 an appealing adaptation candidate; 5 is not a machine claim.
   No average can hide stiffness or an unreadable intention. Owner selection
   remains separate from an agent recommendation and final runtime acceptance.
5. Shortlist up to three whole performances. Then nominate specific useful
   fragments, with exact source hashes and in/out times, even when an otherwise
   rejected take offers an isolated timing idea. Mark such a fragment
   `inspiration_only`, never silently promote its defective pixels.

## Combining candidates without manufacturing a false performance

Prefer one coherent whole performance as the timing spine. Compatible fragments
may supply an anticipation, stroke, glide, arrival or expression reference.
An assembly is a **new reference candidate**, not automatically better than its
parts and not accepted sprite/cinematic pixels.

For every proposed join record matching view/facing, scale, pelvis position,
line of action, elbow/wrist pose, tail bend/fin phase, eye target, motion direction
and incoming/outgoing speed. Favor the same camera and naturally compatible
recovery/glide phases. Keep a written source-hash/timecode edit decision list.
Use straight cuts for the comparison; no crossfade, morphing, optical flow,
mirroring or hidden duplicate frames to make an incompatible splice seem smooth.
If the join needs a new connecting performance, request it as one of the two
replacement takes or leave the join unresolved. Never paste unrelated moving
arms onto another clip's torso/tail and call the composite a reference success.

For 07/08, examine both generated cycles independently and the 4s boundary.
Select matching phase **and velocity**, not just similar first/last silhouettes.
Keep the full eight-second native video. Export the proposed three-second
interior loop separately with exact in/out time and frame indices; do not reverse
or duplicate footage to claim that Grok generated two continuous cycles.
