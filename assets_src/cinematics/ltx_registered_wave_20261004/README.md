## What the pilot established

Take 1 executed in 95.211 seconds including model/loading/export. Its hand rises early (frames 2–8) and sweeps sideways while lowering (19–26). Take 2 uses twice the guide strength: timing improves, but hand/forearm smear remains. Its server execution was 116.18 seconds; 1700 seconds were spent waiting behind an unrelated shared-server job. The 1820.47-second client wall time is not render time. Resource samples taken during that wait belong to the shared queue and cannot establish this take's GPU peak.

Border-connected gray removal leaves a halo around some generated moving edges. Stronger controls and whole-image sharpening do not solve missing hand detail. After two takes, the authored source-arm trial restored crisp painted contours but did not establish a coherent whole-figure performance. The [diagnostic review](environment/sample_review.json) records failures and evidence boundaries. The owner rejected this limb-only method despite its sharper pixels. Current production guidance now requires [crisp contours and figure-wide continuity](../../../design/animation/ANIMATION_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md#preserve-crisp-contours-and-figure-wide-continuity).

## Figure-wide revision

The owner rejected isolated-limb rendering on continuity grounds. Full original pose drawings now receive only a whole-canvas integer translation to align their waist/bodice-tip landmark `(461,280)`. Native shoulders, torso, dress/bodice, hair and tail may all move. The initial attempted offsets came from arm registration; that submission was interrupted after 55.014 seconds at text encoding, before denoising or output frames. Correct root-registration inputs replace them; originals and failed inputs/receipt remain archived. The four source-root landmarks and shifts are declared in `environment/figure_registration.json`.

The new prompt describes sharply defined painted poses, short-exposure motion, distinct fingers, gentle coordinated whole-figure acting and no motion blur/trails. Prompt words are an experiment, not a guarantee; the installed distilled workflow uses CFG 1, so a separate negative prompt is not an independent strong control. Sharpness, anatomy and actual body response must be inspected in native frames. The full-frame Aseprite bridge retains one visible native-frame layer and hidden source references, with no body plate, limb mask, source endpoint substitution or sharpen. All 41 complete native RGBA frames reopen identically. The new take executed in 129.872 server seconds (132.148 client wall seconds); 41 unique frames. Shoulders/bodice/hair/tail respond, but hand rise remains early and lowering frames 19–26 remain badly smeared. This is **not a successful motion-blur repair**; it remains reference-only/rejected for production. Region raster-change metrics are diagnostic, not proof of coherent anatomy.

## Next production decision

The installed 2B first-pass workflow has not met this brief's sharpness/timing bar. The final81-frame native trial reduces the long smeared lowering sweep and preserves whole-figure movement, but transition hands remain blurred. It executed in119.893 server seconds (122.843 client wall seconds), with81 unique full frames round-tripped identically through Aseprite. Do not select it for production solely because it renders quickly. Keep figure-wide source registration and temporal review; retire the fixed-body/arm-only salvage for this performance. Native duration was tested in the final take; repeating that change alone is not an established production fix. A future pilot should test a verified stronger model or whole-frame temporal refinement recipe, preserving the full figure and recording equivalent content/costs before another run. Prompt-only repetition, per-frame ImageGen or sharper resting frames do not resolve the recorded failure. This packet does not evaluate newer LTX-2 hardware viability or certify another model.
