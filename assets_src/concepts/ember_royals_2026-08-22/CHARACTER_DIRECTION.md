# Ember Royals character direction

Status: owner-directed concept canon for the current review candidates,
`ember_king_concept_v6.png` and `ember_prince_concept_v4.png`. These are
review/source concepts, not runtime assets or finished gameplay specifications.

## Story premise

The Ember King does not need an ancient prophecy, elemental mandate, or mythic
history of commanding fire. He comes to Mermaid Roshan's kingdom for a small,
specific, child-readable reason: Roshan has an unusually fancy birthday cake
candle, he thinks it is the coolest thing he has ever seen, and he decides he
wants to take it.

The candle is still recognizably a birthday candle. Its pearly aqua, lavender,
coral, and rainbow spiral and cool blue-white flame make it distinctive without
turning it into a weapon, wand, relic, or world-saving artifact.

The King is an antagonist because he feels entitled to someone else's special
birthday object and refuses to accept “no,” not because he is cruel or
frightening. Returning, sharing, trading for, or helping make another candle
can resolve the conflict without punishment or humiliation.

The Prince does not share the fascination and does not need a separate reason
to want the candle. He comes because the King is his father, this is a family
journey, and accompanying the family is normal to him. At the beginning he is
neither secretly pursuing the same prize nor already planning to betray his
father. He is simply present, observant, and accustomed to quietly managing the
consequences of the King's enthusiasms.

## Personality contrast

### Ember King

- Adult ruler with the emotional presentation of a melodramatic teenage emo
  kid: slouched, defensive, easily embarrassed, possessive, and determined to
  look bored when he is actually delighted.
- Treats the large cape like an oversized favorite hoodie or security blanket.
- Performs indifference through eye rolls, half-lidded looks, exaggerated
  sighs, and stubborn pouts.
- Becomes abruptly energetic whenever the fancy candle is visible.
- Remains visually imposing and self-assured enough to read as the antagonist,
  but his motive and reactions stay funny and child-scale.

### Ember Prince

- More observant and self-controlled than his father, but still awkward and
  guarded.
- Comes on the journey from family habit and loyalty, not candle fascination,
  elemental destiny, political duty, coercion, or a hidden personal mission.
- Moves quickly to prevent situations from escalating, then pretends he was not
  helping.
- His relationship with Roshan grows through shared problem-solving rather than
  a mythic destiny or elemental prophecy.
- His smaller shell, open-back coat, ember-heart clasp, and precise movement
  separate him from the King's broad cape and planted silhouette.

## Family journey dynamic

- The King supplies the goal and emotional momentum. He sees the candle,
  abandons his performed boredom, and physically pulls the journey forward.
- The Prince supplies continuity. He follows a step behind, notices hazards,
  lifts a dragging cape edge, redirects a collision, or closes the distance
  after the King lurches ahead.
- The Prince's eyeline usually tracks the King or the surrounding people. It
  should not mirror the King's fixed stare toward the candle.
- Their comedy comes from familiarity rather than contempt: the Prince has
  clearly done this kind of practical caretaking before, and the King quietly
  relies on it while pretending he does not.
- The Prince should not begin as the King's prisoner, henchman, conscience, or
  secret opponent. He is a son participating in a family routine.
- His later bond with Roshan grows because he begins to see the situation from
  outside that routine. It is a new relationship and perspective, not the
  revelation of a plan he had before arriving.
- His first deviations are small and deniable—returning a displaced decoration,
  slowing the King long enough for someone to speak, or helping both sides avoid
  embarrassment. A major choice comes only after those quiet acts accumulate.

## Fire versus ice theme

Fire and ice are gameplay and visual verbs, not moral alignments. Fire is not
evil and ice is not good.

| Fire language | Ice language |
|---|---|
| warm coral, crimson, ember orange, aubergine | aqua, lavender, pearl white, blue-grey |
| expands, melts, pushes, speeds up | holds, shapes, cools, slows down |
| broad rounded sparks and short heat trails | clean crescents, snowflakes, and crystal paths |
| impulsive acting and quick payoff | patient acting and readable setup |

Roshan's water/rainbow identity bridges the two sides. Safe combinations create
steam puffs, rainbow mist, cooled obsidian stepping stones, thawed routes, and
warm shelters. Elemental mismatches never hurt the child or create a fail
state; they produce a kind bounce, harmless steam cloud, or clearer retry cue.

## Simple ability kits

These abilities need no origin myth. They are ordinary physical/elemental
things these characters can do in the storybook world.

### Ember King

1. **Cape fan** — a large, slow cape sweep fans nearby sparks into a broad warm
   gust. The cape lift is the anticipation cue and the gust pushes or reveals;
   it does not burn.
2. **Hot stomp** — two sulky stomps make a short floor strip glow, melt a small
   ice obstruction, or bounce a loose prop. The orange line appears before the
   effect.
3. **Shell brace** — he plants his feet and turns the obsidian shell toward an
   incoming snowball, water splash, or moving object. This is defensive and
   gives Roshan time to choose another route.
4. **Candle swipe** — his story-defining action is an overly dramatic reach,
   grab, or cape-assisted attempt to take the candle. Encounters should focus on
   recovering, protecting, sharing, or substituting the candle rather than
   defeating him.

### Ember Prince

1. **Cinderstep** — a short diagonal dash with a fading ember footprint. It is
   quick locomotion, not teleportation.
2. **Warm touch** — a small, precise heat pulse from his palm warms a frozen
   switch, loosens ice, or keeps a friend comfortable. He does not command
   distant flames.
3. **Shell guard** — he pivots the compact natural shell into a protective
   angle, shielding Roshan or reflecting a harmless icy projectile toward a
   puzzle target.
4. **Steam pairing** — when his warmth meets Roshan's water or ice action, the
   combined payoff is a readable rainbow-steam bridge, mist reveal, or cooled
   stepping stone.

## Movement and acting

### Ember King

- Baseline stance is broad but slightly slouched, with one shoulder higher and
  the cape pulled close.
- Walk uses a sulky four-beat stomp: heel, plant, cape drag, annoyed settle.
- When the candle appears, he breaks character with one sudden quick shuffle or
  eager lean before remembering to look unimpressed.
- Cape anticipation is large and readable; fabric settles after the body.
- Turns begin with an eye movement and reluctant head turn, then shoulders,
  torso, shell, and cape follow.
- Avoid shell spinning, predatory charging, roaring, or sustained aggression.

### Ember Prince

- Narrow asymmetrical stance, tucked chin, long limbs, and tail used as a clear
  counterbalance.
- Walk is heel-to-toe and guarded; coat tails lag one beat behind the hips.
- Cinderstep reads as crouch/anticipation, one diagonal burst, foot contact,
  then coat-and-hair settle.
- Emotional actions often begin with a glance at the King or a hand near the
  ember-heart clasp, followed by a small decision and decisive movement.
- Shell guard uses a half-turn rather than curling into or spinning the shell.

## Prince shell and jacket construction

- The shell is natural anatomy, not a backpack, detachable item, armor plate,
  or object worn over a finished coat.
- The coat has a genuinely absent center-back panel, not a panel hidden behind
  the shell. A continuous, phone-readable crimson skin halo separates the full
  shell perimeter from every clothing edge.
- The shoulder yoke stops at the upper shell rim and divides into two narrow
  side bands that follow the shell edges without crossing its center.
- No jacket fabric exists beneath, behind, across, or over the shell.
- Two long coat tails begin below the shell and split around the tail root.
- Two simple oxblood stitched seams connect the front collar/clasp construction
  to the sides of the shell opening. They are tailoring, not power cables.
- A narrow glimpse of muted red-plaid lining may appear inside the opening.
- The shell stays vertically elongated and close to the ribcage, with five or
  six short rounded rim nubs so it cannot intersect the collar or coat tails.

## Child-facing constraints

- No punitive failure, injury, burning, freezing, humiliation, or permanent
  loss of the birthday candle.
- Every elemental action uses a large anticipation cue and immediate visible
  response.
- Faces, hands, candle, and objective effects remain high-key and readable at
  phone size.
- The conflict resolves through recovery, cooperation, sharing, or making a
  satisfying substitute—not through defeating or banishing either royal.
