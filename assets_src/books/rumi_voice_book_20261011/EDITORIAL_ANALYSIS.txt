MERMAID ROSHAN: HERE I AM!
Critical plotting analysis and rewrite rationale - owner review candidate

BASELINE
Reviewed the complete 32-page action-story script at commit
056d890ef38f2496ab52669c955ada654f902511. Earlier illustrated editions remain
preserved; they are not illustrations of the rewritten action.

WHAT WAS NOT WORKING

1. Roshan had a worry, but no sufficiently concrete ambition. Being offered a
   place in a chorus is different from asking for a turn of her own. The early
   rehearsal resolved the personal conflict, leaving a long mechanical plot.

2. The strongest recurring actions were listening, waiting and moving crates.
   Those are worthwhile, but they did not consistently dramatise this owner's
   chosen lesson: letting your own voice be heard. The finale's ensemble
   wording also obscured whether Roshan actually got her chosen first line.

3. Two crate clearances, five separate crates and a magical song-current made
   the physical bookkeeping carry the book. More panels and more technical
   directions did not make the emotional stakes larger or clearer.

4. The imps refused a request, then stopped opposing the team before the last
   crate was cleared. Their change of heart was asserted after the action;
   the action did not give them an experience that could explain the change.

5. The bathhouse and food were attractive pauses but needed to change the
   relationships. Without an honest conversation and a new agreement, the
   interruption was just another event rather than the theft of a real turn.

6. The rescued courier mainly supported later engineering. He needed an
   opinion before the rescue, a say in how he was helped, and his own turn.

7. Crops could make a repeated instant look like more action. The latest owner
   rejects that approach. A unique filename or camera zoom is not a unique
   story moment. The new delivery requires newly drawn complete page boards
   and distinct authored drawings within them.

THE NEW CAUSAL SPINE

Roshan asks to sing the first line at neighbourhood Song Night. Rumi promises,
then the enthusiastic trio accidentally talks over her. The imps similarly
dismiss Duri's warning about their oversized prop. That prop slips and traps
him. Roshan persists with a request to investigate, calls Stop when Duri asks
them to wait, follows Duri's advice and asks Rumi for help in the renewed current.

At the bathhouse, Roshan says plainly that her friends talked over her. Rumi
owns the broken promise and apologises. Ordinary comfort and towel choices
are heard without Roshan needing a performance or another rescue. Roshan
chooses her first line and starts it at the extravagant shared supper.

The imps interrupt that precise chosen turn. Their fear of being forgotten
makes them demand every turn. Roshan states a boundary; her friends back her.
The foam-and-bubble show forces changed swimming routes and teamwork. Its
oversized final bubble catches the magician, whose own small help call goes
unheard. Roshan stops the show, listens and coordinates his rescue. His change
is now motivated by something he has experienced. The performer chooses to
help during the rescue, before the apology.

The imps clear the stage and agree to wait. Roshan explicitly asks for the
first line, sings 'Here I am!' alone, and receives 'Here you are!' from the
trio before the neighbourhood joins. Other singers get actual later turns.
Back at the familiar pool, Roshan asks to share an ordinary idea. Rumi listens.

PACING AND ACTION

Pages 2-7: wish, social obstacle, neglected warning and inciting help call.
Pages 8-12: first sustained action - diagnose the trapped strap, stop an
unhelpful pull, loosen it, withstand a changed current and back Duri out.
Page 13: clear result and Duri's chosen rest request.
Pages 14-17: relationship repair, ordinary choices, original song practice,
lavish couch feast and a first line just beginning.
Pages 18-21: personal interruption, boundary, imp motive, stage geography and
team roles. Duri completes his last invitation delivery in the safe aisle.
Pages 22-27: second sustained action - timed low swim, moving curtain/new
puff, changed route, caught stars, failed grand trick, Stop, hold then pop.
Pages 28-29: specific apology and practical amends.
Pages 30-32: unmistakable solo payoff, other turns and transfer to home life.

The rescue is careful cooperation against a current. The second sequence is
a contested stage with evasive swimming, singing and a sudden change from
opposition to helping. The repeated Stop is an intentional emotional payoff;
the mechanics and pictures are different. The story does not teach that every
person who ignores you will immediately become kind. Here the imps change
through a specific experience and observable choices.

VOICE LESSONS SHOWN THROUGH EVENTS

- Say what you want, including small preferences.
- Finish a request and try another way when you are missed.
- Stop is meaningful; friends have a responsibility to hear it.
- Ask for help. Needing another pair of hands is ordinary.
- Tell trusted friends when they have talked over you.
- Support another person's voice without taking their turn.
- A shaky voice counts. Singing skill and applause are not entry requirements.
- An apology includes doing something differently.

PROOFREADING AND BOUNDARIES

Short read-aloud sentences, explicit speakers and concrete verbs replace
mechanical exposition. Original lyrics only. No copied film dialogue, shot,
character design or plot mechanism. The Korean-inspired world remains below
Rumi's pool inside Pearl Castle. No Kareem, new voice magic, candle recovery,
weapon, injury, fail-state or game/runtime change. Four mermaid identities,
costumed imps and Duri's four-flipper anatomy remain stable references.

Editorial and native-image/PDF checks cannot establish owner approval or the
child's comprehension. Read-aloud acceptance, every illustration's artistic
acceptance and print production remain separate pending reviews.
