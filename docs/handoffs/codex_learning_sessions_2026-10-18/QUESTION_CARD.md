# Question card for the chapter-one picture book (package QC)

Part of the [learning-sessions work order](README.md). Owner choice:
`ODR-LEARNING-SCOPE-20261018`. Written by Claude; Codex typesets and
publishes. No game change, no book change and no image.

## 1. What this is

A printed card the grown-up keeps beside *Mermaid Roshan and the Hidden
Rainbow*. It lists one short question for every opening of the book, plus a
few "real life" ideas, so bedtime reading becomes a conversation. The book
itself does not change: the card lives outside the 34 pages, as the V35 story
review already proposes ("Separate grown-up card outside the 34-page story").

Why a card: in the learning-value review, talk-based reading has the
strongest evidence of anything considered, needs no code, and keeps bedtime
screen-free. Question-rich shared reading improves oral language; the review
notes the gains are smaller at ages 4 to 5, which is why the card asks for
talk, retelling and feelings rather than drills. Sources are in the
[README evidence section](README.md#3-evidence-copied-from-the-review).

## 3. Card text (typeset exactly; one US Letter sheet, both sides)

Typesetting notes are in section 4. Text in **bold** is a label; the quoted

### 3.1 Side 1

**Talking together: Mermaid Roshan and the Hidden Rainbow**

**How to use this card.** First time: just read and enjoy. Later readings:
pick one or two questions, not every page. Wait a few seconds. Whatever she
says, say it back and add a little ("Gunk! Yes, sticky green gunk."). Pointing
and acting count as answers. If she wants to keep reading, keep reading.
There are no wrong answers.

**A · Page 1** "Look, Daddy! Pearl Castle!"
Ask: What do you think is inside Pearl Castle?

**B · Pages 2–3** "The plane landed at Sky Lagoon…" / "Let's look inside, Daddy…"
Ask: How did Roshan and Daddy get to Pearl Castle?
Or: If you could fly anywhere, where would you go?

**C · Pages 4–5** "Inside, dust covered the floor…" / "Sniff, sniff!…"
Ask: Sniff, sniff! Who do you think is behind the big shell door?
Or: Roshan said, "Let's clean it together!" What would you clean first?

**D · Pages 6–7** "Daddy gave her a brush and some sponges…" / "A dust bunny splashed in the dirty bath…"
Ask: What little job could Roshan start with?
Or: The dust bunny loved to play. Splash, … ? (Let her finish: "splash!")
Real life: Next tidy-up, choose one little job together.

**E · Pages 8–9** "Round and round went the sponge…" / "Out went the dirty bath water…"
Ask: What did Roshan clean first, the sink or the tub?
Or: Show me how the sponge went round and round.
Real life: At bath time, what do we do first?

**F · Pages 10–11** "Clean water filled the bath…" / "Rumi was stuck under the gunk…"
Ask: How do you think Rumi feels, stuck under the gunk?
Or: What is gunk? What would it feel like?

**G · Pages 12–13** "I'll help!" said Roshan… / "Rainbow colors peeked through the gunk."
Ask, before you turn: Rainbow colors are peeking through. What do you think will happen?
Or: Scoop, … ? (Let her finish: "scoop!")

**H · Pages 14–15** "Whoosh! The waterfall turned rainbow!…" / "A cup was stuck in the fountain…"
Ask: What changed when the cup came out?
Or: Could real water turn rainbow, or is it pool magic?
Real life: In the bath, put the plug in, then pull it out. What does the water do?

**I · Pages 16–17** "The gunk was gone. Up came Rumi!…" / "You helped me!"…
Ask: How does Rumi feel now? How can you tell?
Or: What would you like to say to Rumi?

### 3.2 Side 2

**J · Pages 18–19** "Chirp, chirp!…" / "Two dust bunnies sat on Baby Eagle…"
Ask: How can you tell Baby Eagle does not like this game?
Or: What did the bunnies want? What did Baby Eagle want?
Real life: In pretend play, practise saying "I need space," and stopping when a friend asks.

**K · Pages 20–21** "She gently brushed one bunny away…" / "Baby Eagle was free!…"
Ask: The bunnies said sorry. What could they do now?
Or: Did Roshan push the bunnies hard, or brush them gently?

**L · Pages 22–23** "In the art room, Roshan put the paints in their places…" / "Only the big shell door was still shut…"
Ask, before you turn: AH-CHOO! Who do you think sneezed behind the door?
Real life: Where do your toys go when we tidy up?

**M · Pages 24–25** "Grand Puff! He was covered in dust…" / "Hold on, we'll make you feel…"
Ask: How does Grand Puff feel? How can you tell?
Or: What would help Grand Puff feel better?
Real life: When you have a cold, what helps you feel better?

**N · Pages 26–27** "Daddy, Rumi, and Baby Eagle helped Roshan…" / "Soft bubbles covered Grand Puff…"
Ask: Who came to help Roshan? How did they help?
Or, before you turn: What do you think will happen when the bubbles pop?
Real life: Today, ask her to help you with one thing. Then let her ask you.

**O · Pages 28–29** "POP! Grand Puff sprang out…" / "Down came Grand Puff, soft and small…"
Ask: How does Grand Puff feel now?
Or: Can you pop up like Grand Puff, then come down soft and small?

**P · Pages 30–31** "The art room was tidy…" / "We helped your rainbow shine!…"
Ask: What made Grand Puff feel welcome?
Or: One little job at a … ? (Let her finish: "time!")
Real life: Talk about one small way to include someone. There is no right answer.

**Q · Page 32** "There was room for everyone…"
Ask: Can you tell me the story? What happened first, then next, then at the end?
At bedtime: How do you feel at the end of today?

**After the story**
If she gets stuck retelling, turn back to three pictures (the bath, Rumi in
the pool, Grand Puff) and let her tell each one.
Words she may start using: gunk, scrub, scoop, gently, tidy, sniff, sneeze,
help, "one little job at a time".
If she played Grand Puff in the game today, ask: "How did Grand Puff feel?
How do you feel today?"

## 5. How the questions were chosen

- **Four come from Codex's V35 review, word for word:** D ("What little job
  could Roshan start with?"), H ("What changed when the cup came out?"), J
  ("How can you tell Baby Eagle does not like this game?") and P ("What made
  Grand Puff feel welcome?"). V35's fifth, "Who asked for help? Who helped?",
  fitted V34's "Can you help me?" page, which V36 removes, so N asks "Who
  came to help Roshan? How did they help?" instead. The "real life" lines
  adapt V35's suggested everyday connections.
- **Question types** follow dialogic reading (see the WWC and Mol et al.
  links in the README): open "what" and "who" questions, recall ("what did
  Roshan clean first"), finishing a refrain ("Splash, …"), guessing before a
  page turn, feelings ("How can you tell?"), and links to her own life.
- **Feelings words match the game.** Openings F, I, J, M and O ask how a
  character feels. The same feelings (sad because of a cold, happy after
  help) are the ones "Feelings with Puff" names in the game, so the book and
  the game teach one vocabulary.
- **Not every page, every night.** "Pick one or two questions" follows V35's
  limit ("no counting hunt, rainbow quiz or lesson box on every page") and
  the review's rule that play comes first.
- **Retelling.** Opening Q and "After the story" practise retelling three
  events in order, one of the review's success signs.
- **Book words.** The review hoped she would pick up "gunk" and "rumble".
  "Rumble" left the book in V34, and V36 replaces it with a sniffle and a
  sneeze, so the card listens for gunk, scrub, scoop, gently, tidy, sniff,
  sneeze and help instead.

## 6. Acceptance

- **Machine (Codex, QC2/QC3):** the verification file passes; ledger, impact
  and remote-verification gates pass.
- **Owner:** reads the card and approves its wording (`DL-QA-06`). Until then
  it is a candidate.
- **Family, two weeks** (log in [PARENT_LOG.md](PARENT_LOG.md)):
  - Off-screen use: over rereads she answers more, uses book words such as
    "gunk", "scrub" or "gently" in her own talk, and can retell three events
    in order.
  - Chooses it again: she asks for the book, or brings it at bedtime.
  - Frustration: she pushes questions away or asks to "just read". If that
    happens twice, use fewer questions or stop for a week; it is a signal,
    not a failure.
- No professional speech-language review, physical print test or measured
  learning outcome is claimed.
