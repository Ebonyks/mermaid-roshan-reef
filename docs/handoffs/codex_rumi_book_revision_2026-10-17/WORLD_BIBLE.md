# Pearl Hollow — world bible for the under-pool book and chapter

**Status:** `PROPOSED / CANDIDATE`, agent-authored by Claude on 2026-10-17
within the owner-approved premise (a Korean-inspired mermaid town beneath
Rumi's pool, inside Pearl Castle). Names, customs, places and minor characters
here are proposals for owner review, not book canon. Reserved choices are
marked **(Q*n*)** and listed in the [README](README.md#5-owner-questions).
**Use:** one source for the book's art and text *and* for any later game
chapter, so both show the same place. Codex turns these descriptions into
reference boards; Claude made no image.

## 1. The premise in one breath

Beneath Rumi's pool, below a round crystal skylight that is the underside of
the pool, lies **Pearl Hollow**: a town of mermaids and sea creatures built in a
deep basin. Once a year, on **Lantern Night**, every home gets a sleeping
glow-jelly lantern. At dusk the town gathers in a circle: one voice calls
"Here I am!" and everyone answers "Here you are!" Each answer wakes a lantern,
and the lit lanterns rise through the skylight into Rumi's pool. This year the
answers are vanishing.

## 2. World rules

| Rule | Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Everything is underwater.** | No water surfaces inside the town. Hair, ribbons and cloth float a little. Bubbles rise. Bath "pools" are basins of warm vent water that shimmer like heat haze, each a different tint. Drinks come in lidded shell cups with straws. | Removes the current draft's mixed physics (W-V7) and matches the game's swim movement. |
| **Light falls from the skylight.** | By day the pool's turning rainbow shines down through the skylight. At dusk it dims; at night the town lives on shell lamps and soft bioluminescence. | Gives the book a natural colour script (§8) and ties the town to the castle's existing pool magic. |
| **Sound travels far underwater.** | A small clear sound — a whistle, a whisper in the Echo Dome — carries a long way. A huge BOOM smears every sound into one roar and drowns the small ones. | The voice theme becomes a rule of the world instead of a speech. |
| **Glow-jellies wake to answers.** | The lanterns are sleepy moon-jellies in clam shells. They light only when someone *answers* a call; noise alone, however loud, does not wake them. | One small piece of magic with a visible test, which the game can show as feedback. |
| **Currents are roads.** | The bubble tram rides a current over the Pearl Bridge. Currents are drawn as faint ribbons with direction. A strong pulse can spin water into a whirlpool. | Supplies the first action set piece and a swim mechanic. |
| **The magic budget is small.** | The only magic is the pool's existing light and the glow-jellies. Everything else is physical: currents, props, machines, people. No voice-stealing, no spells, no weapons. | Keeps canon intact and keeps the plot causal. |
| **No words in the world.** | Shop and room signs are pictograms (a bun, a needle, a lantern, the three wavy bath lines). Only balloons and captions carry words. | `DL-READ-04`; lets the same art serve the game. |
| **Customs.** | Answer when someone calls. Every home gets a lantern. "There is always one more lantern, for someone we forgot." | The last saying is planted early and pays off at the climax. |

The imps are land creatures from the castle's Opera. Underwater they walk on
the seabed in weighted boots and never swim — a running visual joke, kept
consistent.

### 4.2 Proposed minor character

**Grandma Gobuk** (*gobuk*, "turtle") keeps the Bubble Bathhouse. She is a
very old sea turtle in round spectacles, with a sheep-towel hat and an orange
float she uses as a pillow. Everyone in town calls her Grandma, as Korean
children call any grandmotherly elder *halmeoni*; she is **not** Duri's
relative by default, because a new family relationship is a reserved decision
(Q2). She was the first caller of Lantern Night long ago, and she whistles
when she surfaces from a long dive — a nod to the haenyeo divers' whistle,
which says "I'm still here". Her sayings: "Robes on, towels up, worries off!",
"In here, even a whisper sings." and "There is always one more lantern, for
someone we forgot."

## 5. The Bubble Bathhouse

The owner said the bathhouse "is not truly developed". Here it becomes a
place with its own architecture, keeper, rituals and rooms, and it does six
jobs in the plot. It adapts the Korean family spa (*jjimjilbang*): matching
uniforms, sheep-head towels (*yangmeori*), baked eggs with a sweet rice drink
(*sikhye*), a heated-floor lounge where families rest and snack together.
Real bathhouses separate bathing areas and guests bathe unclothed; this
children's version is a family spa lounge where everyone wears matching robes
throughout. Underwater it also borrows a real reef habit: sea turtles visit
cleaning stations where surgeonfish and cleaner shrimp nibble algae off their
shells.

### 5.1 Rooms, in the order the friends pass through them

| Room | What it looks like | Ritual or event | Plot job | Game job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Entrance hall** | Shell cubbies, a robe counter run by a hermit-crab attendant, the three-wavy-lines pictogram | Swap into matching striped mint-and-peach robes; fold a towel into sheep ears | Arrival ritual; costume change for the act | Dress-up |
| **Cleaning Station** | A warm flat rock among sponge corals; cleaner shrimp and yellow tang fish at work | Duri lies down and gets scrubbed; it tickles; Roshan helps with a soft sponge | **Clue**: under the algae, glitter paint and a top-hat print | Scrub (Day One cleaning verb) |
| **Echo Dome** | A round shell chamber where every sound rings in widening circles | Grandma's whistle lessons | **Test**: Roshan's tiny "la" rings beautifully — until a BOOM outside sucks the echo away. Proof that the BOOM swallows small voices | Echo / call-and-response |
| **Warm Terraces** | Three stepped basins: rose (warm), mint (cool), gold (sparkly minerals); vent bubbles rising | Soak and talk | **Repair**: the trio own their parts; Rumi renews her promise; Grandma teaches the whistle | Rest point |
| **The Lounge** | A long heated stone floor, a long shell couch, low tables the length of the room | Everyone lies on the warm floor in robes and sheep towels; the feast | **Feast and interruption**: Roshan starts her line; the imps roll the Boom Shell through; BOOM; dark-out | Serve / feast; natural session end |

### 5.2 The feast (owner-requested, "much larger and more extravagant")

A table as long as the lounge, low enough to lie beside: kelp rolls, fish-shaped
pastries, rice-cake skewers glazed in honey, a fruit mountain with star fruit
on top, flower cookies, brown baked eggs from the vent stones, sweet rice
drink in lidded shells with straws. Gags: Nari taps an egg on her own sheep
towel ("Tok!"); Duri tries to eat the fruit mountain from the bottom and
starts a grape avalanche. Keep the food pictures specific and warm-toned
against the mint room.

## 6. Lantern Night

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Invitations** | Duri delivers one clam-shell lantern to every home. Inside sleeps a glow-jelly. |
| **The circle** | At dusk the town forms a ring on Moon Plaza, a little like the moonlit circle song *ganggangsullae*, where a lead singer calls and the circle answers. |
| **The call** | Caller: "Here I am!" Circle: "Here you are!" Caller: "Near or far," Circle: "big or small," All: "We make room for us all!" |
| **What answering does** | Each answer wakes a glow-jelly; the lit lanterns rise through the skylight into Rumi's pool and float there till morning. |
| **The last lantern** | There is always one spare, pearly lantern with no name, "for someone we forgot". |
| **The first line** | Whoever calls first stands in the centre of the circle. Roshan wants that spot. |

## 7. The show

The trio — call them **the Tidebells** — are Pearl Hollow's singing group.
Borrow the wider pop-concert vocabulary, not one film's:

- a practice room with a mirror wall, where formations are rehearsed;
- **lightsticks**: glow-jellies on sticks that the crowd holds up (dark until
  answers wake them);
- the crowd's chant answers the singers, just like the town's call;
- the **centre** spot of the formation, which the trio finally give to Roshan;
- a quick costume change into stage outfits;
- the **ending close-up**, the final pose a pop-music show lingers on —
  Roshan's lantern-lit face on the finale spread.

**The Boom Shell** is the imps' prop: a giant spiral conch on a wheeled cart,
fed by a ribbon cable, painted with glitter and stamped all over (cable
included) with the imps' little top-hat logo — the logo Roshan later finds
smeared on Duri's shell. It makes everything louder by
swallowing every sound into its spiral and blasting it out as one BOOM. Draw
the swallowed sounds literally: speech balloons, cheers and notes curl into
its mouth. When it finally falls quiet it coughs them all back out as floating
bubbles of words. Keep it comic: round, pastel and googly-spiralled, never a
mouth with teeth (`DL-AGE-08`). After the climax the imps point it upward,
and it becomes the **Lantern Horn** that carries soft voices and lanterns up
to the skylight.
