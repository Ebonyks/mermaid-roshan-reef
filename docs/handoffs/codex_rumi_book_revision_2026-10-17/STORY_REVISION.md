## 2. The new spine

**Logline.** On Lantern Night, when every voice in the hidden town beneath
Rumi's pool wakes a lantern, Detective Roshan must find out why the town's
answers keep vanishing — and discover that her own small, clear voice is what
everyone needs to hear.

**Central question (pages 7–29):** Why do the answers vanish, and can the
lanterns still rise tonight? It is asked by a visible failure (the dark
lanterns on page 7), investigated with fair clues (pages 9, 13, 15, 16),
reversed at the midpoint (the dark-out on page 19) and answered by Roshan's own
action (pages 25–29).

**Personal want:** to sing the first line. It is promised (page 2), talked
over (6), tried and swallowed (19), *given up* to help someone else (25) and
finally granted (29). Her choice on page 25 — the microphone or the waving
hand — is the book's moral moment, shown rather than said.

**Theme in action:** a big noise drowns small voices; a small clear voice
carries when friends make room. Every scene dramatises one side of that rule:
the BOOM swallowing answers, Roshan's "STOP!", the Echo Dome, the whistle, the
silent plaza, the imps' soft answer. Nobody states the moral; the closest line
is Roshan's plan on page 21: "Then we'll make an answer so clear, nothing can
swallow it."

**Why it now fills 32 pages:** five threads braid and pay off — A (Roshan's
voice), B (the mystery), C (Duri's lanterns), D (the imps), E (the trio's
promise). The plan gives every story page at least one thread beat, and every
chapter changes place, light and danger.

## 7. Dialogue rules

- One idea per balloon. In the plan, 128 of 130 balloons have 12 words or
  fewer; the longest is Grandma's whistle line (18 words).
- Show the lesson, never state it. Replace lines such as "I am speaking.
  Please listen." with an action ("STOP!"), and "We hear you. We're here. We
  won't leave you." with "I hear you. I'm here."
- Every character sounds different. Roshan: short, curious, brave. Rumi: warm
  and excited. Hana: big exclamations. Nari: funny, full of sound words. Duri:
  breathless courier. Grandma Gobuk: sayings. Glitter Imp: showbiz capitals.
  Top-Hat Imp: grand, then small.
- Keep the running jokes: sheep towels, the detective sheep, Duri spinning on
  his shell, the egg "tok!", the grape avalanche, the imps' weighted boots.
- Say each new name aloud the first time the character speaks or is spoken to.
