### B.2 Primary route: regenerate the weak frames as LTX keyframes ("slow-motion keyframe retake")

This follows the owner's preferred direction and the protocol's
[retake structure](../../../design/animation/ANIMATION_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md#iterative-repair-with-aseprite-and-temporal-retakes)
(Aseprite → keyframes → bounded temporal retake → Aseprite).

The idea: the smear comes from speed, so regenerate each weak span **four times slower**. Each
generated frame then moves at most 3.8 cell px per frame, the speed range where g1's hands stayed
crisp. Then keep every fourth frame. Both ends are locked to g1's own clean frames, so the new frames
join the old ones.

| Window | g1 anchors (clean) | Original time of generated index k (0 to 40) | Keep | Replaces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rise | frame 4 at k = 0, frame 14 at k = 40 | t = 4 + k/4 | k = 4, 8, …, 36 | g1 frames 5 to 13 |
| Lower | frame 24 at k = 0, frame 34 at k = 40 | t = 24 + k/4 | k = 4, 8, …, 36 | g1 frames 25 to 33 |

For each window:
1. **Pose.** Evaluate the g1 pose at the fractional times t. Interpolate every channel of
   `data/rig_pose_run7.json` (unwrapped arm angles, bone deltas, root offset, shoulder offset) linearly
   between integer frames. The result at k = 0 and k = 40 must equal frames 4 and 14 (or 24 and 34)
   exactly. Write it to the new packet; do not change the pilot's files.
2. **Guides.** Render the three lanes with `render_guides_acting.py --occlude` (shift 40), eyes open.
   Check that no generated step moves the guide fingertip more than 4 cell px.
3. **Job.** Use the g1 recipe in A.3 unchanged, except:
   - **Opening and end images.** The opening image (`frame_idx` 0) is g1 native frame 4 (or 24), and
     the end lock (`frame_idx` 40) is g1 native frame 14 (or 34).
   - **Motion sentence.** Replace it with one slow continuous movement and no frame numbers. Rise
     draft: "She slowly lifts her left hand out at her side from beside her hip to beside her head over
     the whole clip, the open hand always out beside her hair, never in front of her hair, face or
     body." Lower: the same, reversed.
   - **Rest of the prompt.** Keep the acting, eyes-open, finger and arm-length sentences.
   - **Candidates.** Make two per window, with seeds 20261004 and 20261005.
4. **Self-audit** (Part C) on all 41 generated frames. Then, for the kept frames, check:
   - registration against the g1 anchors;
   - W2 relative to g1 frame 0;
   - hand length: open 62.4 to 68.9 canvas px wrist crease to longest fingertip, relaxed rest hand
     about 51.9 px. `hand_state` per frame is in the data.
5. **Pick and splice.** Pick one candidate per window, as a whole window, and splice its kept frames
   into the Aseprite master (B.6) as whole frames. Do not mix the two candidates inside a window unless
   Codex's frame review of every affected boundary passes.

Run the rise window first as the pilot. Expand to the lower window only after the rise window passes
the self-audit and Codex's review.

**If a window still fails after its first round**, change the input before the second round. Choose
one of these:
- **Corrected interior keyframes.** Pick one or two of the worst frames in that window. Repair the
  hand inside each flattened frame with ImageGen (mask from the data, refined on the frame; references
  below). Add each as an extra `LTXVAddGuide` image at its stretched index (for g1 frame t, k = 4 ×
  (t − anchor)). This is the protocol's "clean entry/exit poses, one corrected intermediate pose" form.
- **A smaller window or a slower stretch.** For example 6× over a 6-frame span, with the same anchor
  rule.

Do not run a third round on a window without a diagnosis recorded in the job card.

`LTXVAddGuide` accepted single-image guides at `frame_idx` 0 and 40 in every pilot take from a2 on. Interior
indices are expected to work as appended keyframe guides; confirm on the first job and record it.
A latent-masked retake from g1's saved `refined_latent` is not recommended first: the
[2026-10-04 retake study](../../../assets_src/cinematics/ltx_retake_repair_20261004/README.md)
produced ghosted arms with that structure.

### B.3 Fallback: ImageGen repair inside the frame

Use this only for frames still weak after B.2, or for the interior keyframes above. It is a local
repair under `DL-MOT-14`, painted into the one flattened frame (W1), never a pasted hand layer at
export or runtime.

- **Mask.** The hand, wrist and any ghost trail or speck only. Start from
  `starting_mask_box_canvas_px` and refine on the frame. Pixels outside the mask must be unchanged
  after export (verify).
- **References, in this order:**
  1. the frame itself;
  2. the nearest clean g1 hand with the same `hand_state` (open: frames 14 to 24; rest: frames 0 to 4 or
     34 to 40);
  3. the approved hand drawings: K2 open hand, row 0 column 2, and K0 rest hand, row 0 column 0, of
     `roshan_gesture_a.png`.
- **Size.** Draw the hand at the frame's own forearm direction, at the target length above, with the
  same contour weight and skin colours.
- **Order.** Repair consecutive frames outward from the clean anchor, giving the previous repaired
  frame as a reference, so the hand does not flicker. Play the span at 24 fps and frame-step it before
  moving on.

## Part C: the after-completion self-audit

Run it automatically after every completed take, retake and assembly. Its result decides the next
repair round. A pass is one machine lane only. It never grants acceptance.

### C.1 Checks

| ID | Check | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Size: `measure_wave.py` against frame 0 | Figure ≤ 1.0%, head ≤ 2.0% |
| M2 | Hand speed risk: guide fingertip speed, centred, per frame | Frames over 6 cell px per frame are **at risk** and must pass V1 explicitly |
| M3 | Hand length on annotated joints (Aseprite, per W3) | Open 62.4 to 68.9 canvas px; rest about 51.9 |
| M4 | Arm: upper arm + forearm where the elbow is visible | Within ±5% of 160.2 canvas px |
| M5 | Eyes: `eye_openness.py` | Open on every frame except the declared blink frames; closed or half only there |
| M6 | Detached specks of 3 px or more | None |
| M7 | Temporal steps: consecutive-frame silhouette IoU, and mean absolute difference outside the waving arm | No pair above 2× the clip median (catches splice seams and pops) |
| M8 | Registration at splices: crown, neck and waist anchors | Within 2 canvas px of the path through the neighbouring frames |
| M9 | Colour after grade: opaque-interior mean per frame | Within 3 levels of the clip median; boundary steps ≤ 3 levels |
| M10 | End cells 0 and 40 | Byte-equal to K0 |
| M11 | Outfits: every outfit on every frame | 41/41; area spread ≤ 4%; drift ≤ 1 px; no garment pixel over face or hands (after Part D) |
| V1 | Codex frame review: every frame at native size and every hand at 2× | See the checklist below |

**V1 checklist.**
- **Hand.** An open hand shows five distinct fingers; a resting hand matches K0's relaxed hand.
- **Contour and colour.** Continuous contour and skin colours; no grey or brown smear, ghost trail or
  speckled forearm.
- **Identity.** Face, eyes, tiara, hair and tail match the approved style.
- **Whole figure.** The whole body moves as one, and repaired areas do not look pasted.

### C.2 Calibrate on g1 before trusting it

[`data/g1_frames.json`](data/g1_frames.json) labels every g1 frame:
- weak: 5 to 12 and 25 to 33;
- marginal: 13;
- good: the rest.

Claude tried simple hand-box measures, none of which separated weak from good frames:
- skin share;
- grey share;
- Laplacian edge density;
- high-frequency energy.

The guide speed rule (M2) matches the labels exactly. It predicts risk, not the result of a new
take. So V1 decides hands unless Codex builds a reference-based machine hand check that reproduces
the labels, flagging all 17 weak frames and none of the 23 good ones. Two ways to build one:
- compare against the approved K2 or K0 hand rotated to the frame's forearm;
- compare against the hand of the nearest clean frame.

Machine flags may add frames to repair; they never clear a frame V1 flags.

### C.3 The loop

1. **Audit** the completed take or assembly and list the frames that fail.
2. **Window** the failures into spans with a clean, passing anchor frame on each side. Prefer windows
   that align with latent blocks: frame 0 alone, then 1 to 8, 9 to 16, 17 to 24, 25 to 32 and 33 to 40.
3. **Regenerate** each span by B.2: two candidates, one round.
4. **Splice** the best passing candidate into the master and export.
5. **Audit again.**

Stop when:
- everything passes; then report for owner review;
- or the cap is reached;
- or a window fails two rounds. Then diagnose and change the input (B.2 options or B.3); do not
  repeat the same input.

Automate the loop on the PC. After the runner writes a job's receipt, it (or a small watcher) runs
the self-audit. Within the caps in Part E, it queues the next round's retake jobs. Rounds that need a
changed input, ImageGen or owner art approval wait for Codex.

Write `self_audit.json` per round:
- frame verdicts with reasons;
- the windows and the jobs submitted;
- cumulative usage.

### D.2 Requirements

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| O1 | **Style.** Paint in the approved Roshan style: flat value bands, navy or purple contour at the source sprite's own contour width at runtime size, soft aqua or lavender shadows. No gloss, gold-foil lines or detail finer than about 2 px in a 256 px cell. Author at 2× runtime and reduce once; check legibility at 1×. |
| O2 | **Views.** Draw each outfit in each view it appears in: front, three-quarter, side, back, swim-front and swim-back. Back views show the back of the garment. Mirror a view only when the garment is symmetric. Never stretch one view into another. |
| O3 | **Fit by anchors.** Per cell, annotate neck centre, both shoulders, waist centre and sides, and the bodice outline in Aseprite (slices or JSON). Place the garment with one similarity transform per cell (uniform scale, rotation, translation) solved from the anchors. Non-uniform stretch ≤ 3%. One garment scale per view family across all cells, so clothes never change size. Rotation follows the torso, which clips need. |
| O4 | **Occlusion.** Whatever the source draws in front (hands, arms, hair) stays in front, using a per-cell front mask verified in Aseprite. The current skin-colour rule alone is not enough. |
| O5 | **Accessories.** Bows and other accessories are painted, not procedural, and sized from each character's own head height (top of head to chin) by one fraction for every character. Start at 0.4; the owner picks on the board. Keep the current anchor locations unless the owner changes them. |
| O6 | **Garden and disguise.** Both are real garments, not recolours. The disguise keeps the garden motif but has a silhouette that reads differently from the everyday garden outfit at thumbnail size (the disguise design uses silhouette and motif, never hue alone). ### D.3 Method

1. **Pilot: the party dress on three views.** Use front (K0), three-quarter (`roshan_directional` 1)
   and back (`roshan_directional` 4).
   - **ImageGen edit.** For each view, edit the approved cell (upscaled 4×) with a mask covering only
     the bodice and peplum area plus a frill margin.
   - **Design references.** `assets_src/fashion_designer/party_garment_v1/native.png` and the dress
     candidate in `assets_src/fashion_designer/party_dress_v1/`.
   - **Style reference.** The cell itself.
   - **Garment layer.** Extract it as the difference between dressed and original inside the mask,
     cleaned in Aseprite.
   - **Owner board.** Codex builds a board of original, dressed and garment alone for each view.
2. **After owner approval,** extend to the remaining views, then ribbon, garden and disguise, then the
   friends' accessories.
   - **Placement.** Place by anchors on every cell and review every cell.
   - **Cells that fail the fit.** Repaint only those, with ImageGen inside that cell.
3. **Builder.** Replace the single-garment resize in `tools/build_fashion_outfits.gd` with per-view
   garment art, anchors, the similarity transform and the authored front masks.
   - **Clip mode.** Keep `--fit` and `--out`.
   - **Default outputs.** These change: a runtime asset change, so it needs the full probe suite,
     Mobile captures, provenance and license rows.
4. **Clips.** Re-bake the outfits on the repaired g1 and rerun M11.
