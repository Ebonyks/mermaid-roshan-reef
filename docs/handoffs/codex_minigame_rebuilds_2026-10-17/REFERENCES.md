# References: published game designs and child-development evidence

- **Status:** `SUPPORTING_CURRENT` evidence for the three rebuild specifications.
  It grants no acceptance and cannot redefine a design rule.
- **Method:** four research passes on 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-18 fetched public
  pages: developer and publisher pages, store listings, Nintendo pages,
  well-sourced encyclopedia articles, reviews, and the research papers
  themselves or their abstracts. Claude spot-checked the two findings the
  designs depend on most (Woodruff Carr et al. 2014; Bobin-Bègue et al. 2014)
  against the papers. No game was installed or played, no asset was copied,
  and nothing here claims a game's child suitability.
- **Marks:** **UNVERIFIED** means the claim was seen only in a search snippet,
  or no fetched page stated it. No fetched source gave a numeric hit window
  for any commercial game, so every timing value in the specifications comes
  from the child-development evidence (section 5) and must be tuned on the
  phone with the child.
- **Earlier research:** Codex's 2026-10-17 Pop Star note (uncommitted) also
  cites Cytus II, Deemo, Hatsune Miku: Colorful Stage, Taiko no Tatsujin:
  Rhythm Festival, A Dance of Fire and Ice, Rhythm Doctor and Just Dance Now.
  Those findings are consistent with section 2.

## 1. How each design uses the evidence

| Design decision | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Pop Star keeps the echo but times it: call bar, then answer bar | Patapon, PaRappa the Rapper, Space Channel 5; Simon is the untimed counter-example |
| Rhythm rewards; it never blocks an engaged child | Woodruff Carr et al. 2014: 13 of 35 children aged 3–4 did not synchronize with a live drummer at either rate |
| Beat around 500 ms; never ask for a slow steady pulse | McAuley et al. 2006; Bobin-Bègue et al. 2014; van Noorden 2013 |
| Windows lean late | Bobin-Bègue et al. 2014 (anticipating the beat is rarely found in young children) |
| Phrases of 2–3 notes or steps | Gathercole et al. 2004 (block and digit recall at age 4); Pokémon Diamond/Pearl dance round uses 3 steps |
| Falling ribbons onto the real drum | Taiko no Tatsujin, Donkey Konga, Project DIVA (cue lands on its own outline), Cytus (still target, moving line) |
| The band repeats a section, then waits | Rock Band 2 No Fail, Rhythm Heaven's skip after three tries, Sayonara Wild Hearts' skip, Patapon's clean beat after a slip |
| A drum always sounds | Wii Music (the player supplies timing, the game the notes); Simon's triad-tuned buttons |
| Ballet: every input is a dance move; the dancer lands on the beat, the child is not judged | Phillips-Silver et al. 2024 and Eerola et al. 2006 (under-5s rarely lock whole-body movement to the beat); Diva Ballerina and Ballerina Toddler Fun Game as counter-examples |
| Ballet: show then copy, cue on the dancer | Space Channel 5, Just Dance Kids (the leader's hand glows), Dance Dance Revolution Beginner (a dancer steps the routine) |
| Ballet: held pose and capped spin | Just Dance Gold Moves, Just Dance Kids Freeze bonus, Elite Beat Agents spinners |
| Racer: three lanes chosen by a tap; the kart drives itself | Subway Surfers, Excitebike, Toca Cars, Mario Kart 8 Deluxe and Mario Kart Tour assists; Flatters et al. 2014 on tracking at 4–5 |
| Racer: no long holds; no mashing for speed | Blaze and the Monster Machines: Axle City Racers; Excitebike overheating; Go Go Thomas and Pokémon Dash as counter-examples |
| Tap is the core gesture; big targets; register at touch-down | Vatavu et al. 2015; Nacher et al. 2015; Sesame Workshop 2012; Nielsen Norman Group 2018 |
| Voice plus an on-screen hand demonstration; re-prompt after 6–8 s | Hiniker et al. 2015; Sesame Workshop 2012 |

## 5. Child development and touch research

| Finding | Numbers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Young children's own tapping tempo is fast and narrow | 4–5-year-olds: spontaneous interval 312 ms; accurate only near a 337 ms target; slow rates rarely matched at ages 4–7 | McAuley et al. 2006, [PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~rbelew/courses/cogs260_s10/readings/mcauley_etAl_2006.pdf) |
| Children keep near 500 ms even when asked to slow down | 3- and 5-year-olds (20 each): median intervals about 508 and 463 ms; the 0.4 s target was handled well; beyond 1.0 s fewer than half of taps were reinforced; anticipating the beat is "rarely found in young children" (from the literature the paper cites) | Bobin-Bègue, Droit-Volet & Provasi 2014, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4256987) (spot-checked) |
| A third of 3–4-year-olds do not lock to a beat even with a person | 35 children (mean 4.37 years) drumming with an experimenter at 2.5 Hz and 1.67 Hz: 22 synchronized at both rates, 13 at neither | Woodruff Carr et al. 2014, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4210020) (spot-checked) |
| A live partner helps | Children aged 2½–4½ drummed more accurately with a person than with a drum machine or speaker | Kirschner & Tomasello 2009, [abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.jecp.2008.07.005&resultType=core&format=json) |
| Spontaneous tempo across labs | About 400–500 ms at ages 2–7 in other labs; method differences explain McAuley's faster value | Provasi, Anderson & Barbu-Roth 2014 review, [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01048/full) |
| Dancing to music under 5 | Movement is sometimes regular but only occasionally matches the beat | Phillips-Silver et al. 2024, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231307/) (citing Eerola et al. 2006) |
| Sequence memory at 4 | Block recall 15.4/15.1 and digit recall 19.8/19.5 (boys/girls); by the test's scoring rule (Claude's inference), block span is reliable at 2 and about half the time at 3 | Gathercole et al. 2004, [PDF](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/ben.ambridge/papers/Gathercole,%20Pickering,%20Ambridge%20&%20Waring%20(2004).pdf) |
| Gestures at 3–6 | Tap 98.7%, one-finger drag 92.0%, double tap 82.8% completion; a tap lasted 267 ms on a phone; accept long taps and treat several fingers as one touch | Vatavu, Cramariuc & Schipor 2015, [PDF](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf) |
| Gestures at 2–3 | Tap 94%, drag 91%, one-finger rotation 88%, long press 50%, double tap 63% | Nacher et al. 2015, [PDF](https://riunet.upv.es/bitstreams/1f44e11b-abf5-459c-b725-52acd986bbac/download) |
| Teaching a gesture | Spoken instructions and an on-screen hand worked from about 3½; pulsing alone failed at every age | Hiniker et al. 2015, [PDF](http://faculty.washington.edu/alexisr/TouchscreenPrompts.pdf) |
| Preschool practice | Register input on touch-down; give every touch a sound; idle hint after 6–8 s in games; large, separated hotspots | Sesame Workshop 2012, [PDF](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/SesameWorkshop-2012.pdf) |
| Targets and quick reactions | Targets at least 2×2 cm for ages 3–5; avoid quick reactions under 5; one large button to drive a car worked | Nielsen Norman Group 2018, [article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/) |
| Tracking a moving target | Following a moving dot improved sharply from ages 4–5 to 6–7; a guide line gave 4–5-year-olds no benefit | Flatters et al. 2014, [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0088692) |
| Stray touches | Holdover touches about 4% on phones; make hit areas larger than the drawn target | Anthony et al. 2019, [preprint](https://init.cise.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/378/2019/03/anthony-et-al-IJHCS2019-MTAGIC-final-preprint.pdf) |

Gaps: no study reports 4-year-olds' tap-to-beat asynchrony in milliseconds,
copying accuracy by phrase length at age 4, or a comparison of continuous
steering with lane choice at 4–5. Those are device and child tests, not
literature facts.
