# Grok handoff retrospective — 2026-08-30

## Result

The Day One pool-restoration storyboard is the strongest direction package in
the project. Its advantage is not prompt length. It makes story causality,
room topology, character identity, and rejection conditions explicit before a
generation starts. The opening-flight trial demonstrates the opposite: one
large request made Grok invent edits, wildlife, geography, and missing action.

The production rule derived from the evidence is:

> Give Grok one approved opening image, one camera setup, one dominant causal
> action, exact character locks, a visible end state, and no more than three
> additional role-labeled visual authorities. Let the project—not Grok—own
> writing, shot order, continuity, review, and edit decisions.

The operational formula is in
`design/GROK_MASTER_HANDOFF_FORMULA_2026-08-30.md`. Machine enforcement is in
`tools/audit_imagine_handoff.py`; V2 shot-card fields are defined in
`design/templates/IMAGINE_SHOT_CARD_V2.md`.

## What consistently worked

1. **A one-sentence story promise.** “Pull the plug, restore both water
   sources, join their clean fronts, reveal Rumi, hug” is harder to derail than
   a list of attractive moments.
2. **Causality in visible order.** Trigger, physical change, and readable
   settle are named for every shot. The best pool sequence also forbids the
   purification payoff from appearing early.
3. **Environment topology plus forbidden topology.** “One giant shared pool”
   was more effective than general style prose. “No local basin” caught the
   most repeated pool error.
4. **Physical camera addresses.** North/east/south/west wall ownership created
   unseen but connected room material. Lens words alone did not.
5. **Identity, pose, and relationship treated as separate domains.** Rumi's
   full-body identity, motion poses, and Roshan relationship sample each answer
   a different question. Combining them into “make Rumi” invites averaging.
6. **Exact anatomy and contact negatives.** One tail, no legs, exact arm count,
   correct hand ownership, visible nozzle overlap, and four-arm hug contact are
   stronger than “keep characters consistent.”
7. **Accepted endpoint inheritance.** Continuing from the exact accepted prior
   end prevents teleportation and state reversal.
8. **Fresh regeneration with retained rejects.** The pool sequence did not
   patch local-basin, premature-flow, single-source, or human-leg failures. It
   regenerated the complete frame and preserved the failed attempt as evidence.
9. **Independent status claims.** Archive completeness, generation readiness,
   and delivery acceptance describe different facts. The bathroom and Stuffie
   packets are useful precisely because they do not call an archive executable.

## What consistently failed

- One request containing multiple shots, locations, edits, and dependent
  actions. Grok filled uncertainty with montage and scenic filler.
- Large undifferentiated upload sets. Semantically related fauna, alternate
  rooms, boards, or dirty/clean variants leaked into the generated story.
- Style paragraphs without an approved style/grade image. Text did not prevent
  glossy modern rendering.
- Character names without discriminative identity traits and anatomy locks.
  Roshan became generic; Rumi was substituted; legs appeared; scale drifted.
- Perspective labels without a room compass and wall inventory. “Different
  angle” repeatedly meant a crop of the same north wall.
- Close-ups with no continuity anchor. They became catalog/product images.
- Numeric scores without knockout rules. A visually attractive frame can still
  have the wrong pool, wrong actor, wrong flow direction, or wrong limb count.
- Storyboards or HUD captures used as pixel authority. They carry incorrect
  or incomplete pixels into later work and violate the current handoff rule.
- Self-certification by the generator. Smooth video is not evidence of stable
  identity, full-frame generation, or correct continuity.

## Character-consistency conclusion

Character consistency must be both an archive-level contract and a shot-level
executable lock. The archive records the canonical image hash, approval status,
identity traits, anatomy, forbidden drift, relative scale, and relationship
rules. Each shot then names the exact cast, binds each visible character to an
identity image, declares screen role/start/end state, and repeats at least two
short discriminative lock phrases in the prompt. A character who is absent is
also explicit: absence is a continuity state, not empty prompt space.

This is now enforced by the V2 audit. A shot cannot be `GENERATION_READY` when
its character binding differs from the archive authority, the exact cast and
locks disagree, required identity phrases are absent, or a continuing shot
does not use the accepted previous endpoint.
