## Editorial judgment

Implement this draft. Its strongest improvement is causal: Roshan begins with a mess that feels too big, tries small jobs, helps friends, then asks those friends to help with a job she cannot do alone. The ending now names a lesson the preceding events demonstrate. The stuck door and promise repair the earlier impression that Roshan hears someone in need and simply leaves. Rumi's offer and Roshan's later request give the helpers a reason to return.

The dust bunnies' treatment also improves. Their needs explain their behavior, while the page21 apology and promise describe a useful change in behavior. Grand Puff receives reassurance and practical help. His rainbow is a reveal, rather than the reason he deserves kindness. The last invitation provides a warm return to safety and rest.

The familiar activities support an adult reading with a preschooler: facing a mess, bath play, helping a friend, rough play, asking for help and resting together. That is an editorial judgment about accessible situations, not proof that every child will identify with them or understand the moral.

## Where the handoff overstates its case

1. **Relief is not the same as forgiveness.** Baby Eagle's happy chirp shows that he feels better. It does not establish that he has forgiven the bunnies, nor should an apology require an immediate happy response. Keep the chirp, apology and promise; describe the resolution as relief and a commitment to gentler play.
2. **Empathy must still acknowledge impact.** "They just wanted to play" is understandable motivation, but "just" can sound like an excuse when someone is stuck. The gentle intervention and "We'll be gentle!" are essential. The story should not suggest that good intentions make rough play harmless.
3. **An illness is not wrongdoing.** Grand Puff says sorry about the dust, not about having a cold. Read his apology as concern for the mess; his care and welcome are already offered before it. The text must not be interpreted as requiring sick children to apologize or earn help. The magical washing/transformation belongs to this fictional story; it is not a lesson about treating illness.
4. **The door payoff is a magical rule introduced late.** The existing sparkle trails support page23 visually, and the promise has a clear payoff. Nevertheless, "clean rooms open the door" remains a proposed story fact. Do not present this draft as an established game rule or an owner-accepted new canon fact.
5. **The stronger arc costs breathing room.** Twenty-five captions expand. Pages5,11,17,23–26 and30–32 deserve particular reading and layout attention. Short individual sentences alone do not prove a good read-aloud; speaker changes and page turns matter too. Preserve pause breaks where space permits and fit the exact words without shrinking the type.
6. **The ending repeats its lesson.** Page30 states friendship, page31 states everyone's need for help, and page32 shows belonging. This is coherent reinforcement, but a real reading may find the explanation heavy after the transformation. "Even the dust bunnies" explicitly includes the title characters while also marking them as outsiders. Retain the proposed words for this review; ask the owner to listen for whether that emphasis is welcome.
7. **Not every problem is small or immediately resolved.** Rumi is trapped for several beats, Baby Eagle is pinned, and Grand Puff is unwell behind a stuck door. The kind resolution lowers threat; the handoff's blanket claim of "no danger" is stronger than the depicted situation supports. Likewise, no clinical age suitability or speech-language approval follows from this editorial pass.
