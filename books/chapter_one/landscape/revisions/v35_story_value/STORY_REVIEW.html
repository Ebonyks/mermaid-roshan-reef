Mermaid Roshan · editorial development review · V35 proposal

What does Roshan carry out of the castle?

Fantasy, visual humour and family warmth already give this book value. The next draft can give its heroine a clearer learning journey: notice a need, help gently, accept help, and make room for a friend.

Proposal only. The current V34 manuscript, pages and PDFs remain unchanged. One owner question is pending: kindness first, small-step confidence first, or equal weight? The cover candidates below are new review layouts; no whole new narrative scene was drawn.

The stronger emotional arc

At first the castle looks like too much. Daddy helps Roshan choose a beginning. She learns that cleaning can make play comfortable and let someone move freely. When play hurts a friend, she notices that friend’s point of view. At the last door, she hears a need instead of treating a dusty stranger as an enemy. This time she asks her friends for help. The final reward is feeling better and being included.

This is an editorial proposal using the existing scenes. It preserves the finished kindness plot and the rainbow surprise. It does not add a new quest, a failure episode, a new medical diagnosis or a sermon after each room.

What the existing draft leaves on the table

A helpful heroine with little development

On 4 Roshan immediately offers to clean; on 5 Daddy gives a useful strategy. She seldom needs that support in a visible choice.

Give her one honest question about beginning, then show her choose a small first task. Confidence can grow without a new failure, a crying pose or a dangerous setback.

A chain of errands needs a shared purpose

Bath, pool, playroom and paints currently read as completed jobs. The care beneath those jobs is often left implicit.

Connect cleaning to comfort, freedom, gentle play and a shared place to rest. Keep practical responsibility without making tidiness the measure of a good person.

Intent is explained; impact is underdeveloped

On 18 the bunnies want to play while Baby Eagle is pinned. On 20 they say Sorry and We were just playing, but there is no clear next behaviour.

Give Eagle words for his boundary and the bunnies a small concrete repair. Their existing exact lines are prior owner wording: the proposed second-bubble change is a decision, not silently implemented.

Rumi can become a participant

Rumi is helped on 10–16 and joins the group on 26, but her choice to help is not voiced.

Bridge the hug to later teamwork. Rumi helps because she chooses to join in; gratitude does not create a debt or an obligation to repay Roshan.

The finale can show receiving help

A quiet request on 23 leads to care on 25–26. Roshan currently appears to know exactly what to do.

Let Roshan ask her friends to help with the big job. Helping others and accepting help can belong to the same child.

Appearance carries much of the payoff

The rainbow transformation is wonderful fantasy, followed by We helped your rainbow shine on 31.

Keep the exact reveal. Put the emotional reward in comfort, thanks and company. Show care before the change; nobody earns belonging by becoming clean or pretty.

Physical cause and magical cause can be clearer

A cup blocks the fountain; pool magic makes rainbow water. Both events use Whoosh, with little explanation.

State the physical blockage plainly. Keep rainbow water explicitly magical; no science claim about real water changing colour when rubbish is removed.

A rear cover discloses the answer

The current rear shows the entire cooperative wash: Grand Puff is a friend who is helped, not an unresolved mystery.

Use an early threshold scene and a beginning-focused question. Omit washing, rainbow Puff, the transformation, the sleeping finale and the solution. The front remains a separate later cover-review decision.

What the guidance supports

ASHA: Communication Milestones, 4 to 5 Years

Clear story connections, location words, categories, prediction, conversation and child-led retelling are useful shared-reading activities.

Our proposed application: Use a few concrete language opportunities already shown in the art. This is an editorial application, not a tested intervention or SLP sign-off.


AAP: Books to Teach Kids About Feelings and Resilience

Shared reading and conversation about characters’ feelings can support discussion of emotions and other people’s perspectives.

Our proposed application: Let visible feelings lead to an understandable choice and action. A value is dramatized through the story; no clinical benefit is promised.


Harvard Center on the Developing Child: Activities for 3 to 5 Years

Pretend play, planning, sorting and revisiting stories provide opportunities to practise organizing ideas and actions.

Our proposed application: Offer a short optional retelling or gentle-play activity after reading. Reading this book alone is not evidence of improved executive function.


These sources inform the editorial design. A finished book still needs real read-aloud observation and owner/child acceptance; the review does not establish professional SLP approval.

Decision before rewriting

The recommended emphasis is kindness through attentive action, with the small-jobs refrain as a tool for acting on that kindness. The pending owner response will determine how much the opening and closing lean into confidence versus perspective-taking.
