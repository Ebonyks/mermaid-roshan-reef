# Dust Bunnies book: story and language critique (V44)

Status: `SUPPORTING_CURRENT` critique behind the V45 work order in
[README.md](README.md). Written by Claude on 2026-10-31; Claude made no
images. Book: "Mermaid Roshan and the Dust Bunnies", V44 at commit
`49a199846c61a39a3735e162214706b3687f12d3`.

V44's sentences are short, musical and the right length for ages 3–5, but
the story doesn't yet add up to one lesson. Roshan never feels anything a
child would recognize, the sniffle behind the door vanishes for 17 pages, her
friends come back for no reason, and the ending offers three different
takeaways. The owner's moral is already in the pictures: the dust bunnies
cause problems but just need help, and friends together can do big jobs. The
next draft, V45, makes the words carry it, changing 25 of 32 pages and no art.

## The moral

In a child's words: **everyone needs help sometimes, even a dust bunny who
makes a mess, and friends can do big jobs together.** The book says both
halves out loud near the end, on pages 30 and 31, and every earlier page
builds toward them.

The owner's words (2026-10-31): "Empathetic representation of the dust
bunnies, who cause problems, but just need help, is a critical element, and
that through the power of friendship, larger jobs can be accomplished."

Roshan's arc, one line per stage:

1. **Beginning (1–6):** the castle is a big mess, and she doesn't know where
   to start.
2. **Middle (7–22):** one little job at a time, she cleans and helps, and sees
   what each dust bunny really needs.
3. **Turn (23–25):** the biggest job is too big for her alone, so she asks for
   help.
4. **Climax (26–29):** the friends she helped come back, and together they
   make Grand Puff well.
5. **End (30–32):** the lesson is said, Grand Puff says sorry and is forgiven,
   and everyone rests together.

## What already works

The book's voice is right for ages 3–5; keep all of these.

- Short, clear sentences: 392 words, about 12 a page and 5 a sentence.
- Sound words and refrains a child can join: "Splash, splash!", "Scoop,
  scoop!", "Scrub, scrub!" three times, "Chirp, chirp!", "POP!" and
  "AH-CHOO!"
- Everyday places and chores inside a magic castle: bath, sink, pool,
  playroom and art supplies.
- Three rescues that grow in size: a mermaid, a baby bird, then a giant dust
  bunny.
- No villain: every problem is solved with care.
- Two page-turn surprises (23 to 24 and 27 to 28) and a cozy ending in the
  bubbles.
- The rear cover promises a beginning without giving away the end.

## What isn't working yet

Eight problems, most important first. The first six are story problems; the
last two are about cause and wording.

1. **Roshan doesn't feel anything, or change.** Only Grand Puff's feelings are
   named, three times. Roshan is cheerful and capable on every page, so a
   child never hears "this is too big", "I'm worried" or "I can't do this
   alone".
2. **Page 5 walks away from a need.** She hears sniffling, says "Hello?" and
   goes off to clean bathrooms, in a book about helping. The mystery isn't
   mentioned again until page 23. Claude recommended this order in the V34
   handoff; Codex objected at the time, and Codex was right.
3. **The dust bunnies aren't joined up.** The title is "and the Dust
   Bunnies", but the words never say Grand Puff is a dust bunny, and the
   bunnies' trouble is never shown as a need.
4. **The friends come back for no reason.** Rumi and Baby Eagle disappear
   after their rescues and reappear at the wash. Roshan never asks for help,
   so "together" has no turning point.
5. **The Baby Eagle quarrel ends on an excuse.** "Sorry!" next to "We were
   just playing!" explains but promises nothing, and Baby Eagle never
   answers, so no forgiveness is shown.
6. **The ending scatters, and praises looks.** Pages 30–32 offer three
   takeaways: small jobs, "We helped your rainbow shine!" and room for
   everyone. The middle one rewards Grand Puff's new colors rather than the
   friendship.
7. **Causes are missing.** Why the big door stays shut, what the cup was
   blocking, where the rainbow came from, and where the bath bunny goes when
   the plug is pulled.
8. **Word-level slips.** An idiom a 4-year-old won't follow ("He felt like
   himself again"), double-meaning or vague phrases ("Hold on", "clean and
   better", "Little colors… beneath them"), "rainbow-colored" straight after
   "rainbow", "Whoosh!" on back-to-back pages, "a brush" for what is drawn as
   a feather duster, and Rumi named before she is introduced.

## Resolutions and lessons

Every problem in V45 is small, solved kindly and quickly, with no danger or
blame, which suits ages 3–5.

| Problem | How it's solved | What a child learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The castle is a big mess (4) | Daddy: one little job at a time; Roshan starts with the sink (6, 8) | Big jobs get done in small steps |
| Someone sniffles behind a stuck door (5) | She promises to come back, and does (23) | Keep your promises |
| The bath bunny splashes gunk everywhere (7) | She cleans the bath, so it has a good place to play (10) | Trouble can come from a need |
| Rumi is stuck and asks for help (11) | Roshan scoops the gunk and pulls and pulls the cup free (12–16) | Help when asked; keep trying |
| Two bunnies squash Baby Eagle (19) | Gentle brushing; "Sorry!" and "We'll be gentle!"; a happy chirp (20–21) | Play gently; say sorry and mean it; forgive |
| Grand Puff is dusty, sneezy and unwell (24) | Roshan isn't mad, asks her friends for help, and they wash him together (25–29) | Be kind to whoever caused the trouble; ask for help |
| Grand Puff says sorry for the dust (31) | "That's okay. You just needed help." | Forgive; everyone needs help sometimes |
| Who belongs? (32) | "Room for everyone, even the dust bunnies" | Everyone is welcome |

## What every child will recognize

The castle is magic, but each turn of the story is something a preschooler
has lived.

| Pages | Moment | The child's own experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | "It's SO messy! Where do we start?" | Tidy-up time when the room feels too big |
| 5 | A noise behind a closed door | Hearing a strange sound and wondering who it is |
| 7–10 | Splashing, the plug, a clean bath | Bath time |
| 11–17 | A friend stuck, then a big thank-you hug | Helping a friend and being thanked |
| 15 | "Roshan pulled and pulled..." | Trying hard with something stuck |
| 19–21 | Rough play, sorry, playing gently | Playing too rough, saying sorry, being forgiven |
| 22 | Putting the paints in their places | Putting art things away |
| 24 | Sneezing and not feeling well | Having a cold |
| 25 | "Can you help me?" | Asking for help with something too big |
| 31–32 | "That's okay." Everyone cuddled up | Being forgiven; snuggling at bedtime |
