Reviewed 30 September 2026. Edition: **v27_correct_door**, source commit **f857a65e95d5d7846af19f27046735a74cba4ec4**. This is an editorial and visual review, not a revised edition. No book art, manuscript, font, layout code or PDF has been changed.

**The book has a strong emotional spine: Roshan makes a place for everyone by helping them. A focused continuity, action and page-turn pass is the most useful next step.** The bath payoff, Rumi's hug, messy art-table composition, group scrub and sleeping ensemble are worth protecting. The current weaknesses are concentrated enough to fix without losing those gains.

7. **Make page turns do narrative work.** In a conventional dummy with S1 on the right, S24/S25 and S28/S29 share spreads: both reveals are visible alongside their setups. Validate physical pagination before moving pages.

## What the first book teaches this edition

The reference's design language is a rhythm, not merely a dotted border. It uses quieter pale aqua stationery, small contextual lower objects, varied sizes of genuinely isolated figures and scenes that sometimes occupy the whole page. The small recurring actions reward another reading without competing with the main event.

The current book has **22 full-art and 10 cutout story pages**. In the original's 32 interior pages, the visual count is **13 full-art and 19 framed/cutout pages**. These are descriptive counts, not a target ratio. The current uninterrupted full-art run from S11 through S18 particularly reduces contrast between water cleanup, Rumi's thanks and the next rescue.

Consider converting S13's connected fountain action and S15's hug into genuine silhouette compositions, if complete source contours support them. This would give 20 full-art and 12 cutout story pages before any pagination experiment. Do not use irregular scenic blobs as a substitute. Keep the rainbow, emergence, rescue, POP and nap large.

Useful original-book precedents include original PDF4's scale contrast, PDF13's alternating figure/text arrangement, PDF16's multiple cooking actions, PDF21's action/result composition and PDF28's sleeping Eagle at the bottom. Borrow the compositional logic, not a rigid grid or unrelated legacy characters. Original PDF11 identifies Lamb-a by name.

Keep the blue base, but make its wash and dotted perimeter quieter: the current saturated cloudy blue, dark perimeter and outlined banks sometimes feel like a game interface. Judge this at print size before applying a global adjustment. **Quiet space is purposeful; empty space becomes a problem when the narrated action is absent.** Do not fill every gap with another prop, bunny or bubble.

Never claim a precise hidden-10% occlusion or exact alpha extraction for generated flattened pixels when that was not measured. Retain the established decorative size/zone rules and no-duplicate-prop rule. **S19's speaking bunnies are narrative actors**, covered by its existing scoped exception; compose them with Eagle rather than shrinking them to decorative stamps.

## Story, emotional logic and pacing

The book's sequence is coherent: arrive, start small, restore the water, free Rumi, rescue Eagle, make art, help Puff, rest together. Rumi and Eagle later helping Puff gives the kindness a satisfying return. Keep that connection visually clear.

The weak middle transitions are manageable. Move from Eagle's apology/gentle play into the art room with a short invitation or visual directional cue. S21/S22/S23 can then read as sort, clean, create. On S22 the picture still contains paint residue while the text declares the table clean; align the verb tense with the actual stage.

Do not let cleaning imply that someone becomes worthy of friendship only after looking pretty. Roshan offers care while Puff is dusty and unwell. His comfort, inclusion and return to himself should carry at least as much weight as his rainbow color. The ending already offers this; preserve its warmth without adding a moralizing paragraph.

## Language for reading together now and participation later

The current story has approximately **368 words including the five apology words**, excluding covers. It is already short. Cutting it further by default would remove useful emotion and cause/effect. Prefer precise verbs, clear speakers and concrete referents over a lower word-count score.

Keep the productive repetitions: “I'll help,” “scrub, scrub,” “one little job at a time” and “little by little.” Repetition can invite Roshan to join in naturally; it need not appear mechanically on every page. “Brushes here. Paints there.” is particularly useful. Avoid the redundant gentle-play line across S19/S20, the label “playful pest” where “dust bunny” is clearer, and ambiguous “your” when the addressee is missing.

Keep a few supported richer words, such as fountain, sparkly and sprang, alongside familiar verbs. Names can remain part of shared reading. The book need not become artificially flat or all one-syllable words. Pictures should support understanding, not train a child to guess printed words from illustrations.

The editorial direction is consistent with the Institute of Education Sciences' advice on shared reading, rereading and conversation for ages 3–5: [5 Steps to Use with Young Children During Shared Book Reading Time](https://ies.ed.gov/learn/blog/5-steps-use-young-children-during-shared-book-reading-time). Later independent reading also needs sound–letter knowledge, decoding practice and connected text; this picture book is not a controlled phonics reader or a certified reading level. See [Foundational Skills to Support Reading for Understanding in Kindergarten Through 3rd Grade](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21/Published).

Any grown-up reading suggestions belong outside the story. Do not add comprehension quizzes, “find the hidden character” instructions or a printed answer key. Let the story and the quiet visual recurrence remain rewarding on their own.

## Typography and production

Sniglet is embedded and the story text is nominally 18 points. Keep it comfortably large. The extracted page-layer text boxes fall within the 7 × 5-inch trim; that mechanical check does not establish optical readability, bleed safety or gutter clearance.

Most full-art captions use navy letters with a white outline. On busy walls or art-room details that halo can look heavy while still competing with the image. Try moving the caption into naturally quiet space, then a bounded local value adjustment, then a slightly lighter outline. Do not solve this by reducing font size, covering the whole page with an opaque strip or adding new scenery.

The PDF is about 189.4 MB and uses lossless Flate image compression. The remaining softness should not be attributed to destructive JPEG compression in this PDF. Many full-page native sources are about 1484 × 1060, yielding about 212 ppi at 7 × 5 inches. That calls for actual-size print evaluation, not an automatic failure or a fictional “300 dpi” pass from upscaling.

The clearest exceptions are the stretched ceiling strips on S27–30: 116.0, 116.9, 136.7 and 137.7 effective vertical ppi, respectively. The retained lower scenes are about 239 ppi. Preserve those scenes and fix only the weak extension geometry/native detail.

This is a review PDF, not a printer-ready cover/interior package. It has no explicitly authored TrimBox/BleedBox beyond the media size. Choose the actual printer/binding specification before adding bleed, safe margins, spine, cover wrap and any publication metadata. Do not guess a universal spine width or claim a print standard is satisfied. Preserve the lossless master; a clearly labeled smaller viewing copy can be separate.
