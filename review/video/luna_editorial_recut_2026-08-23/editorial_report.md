departure, relationship, destination, discovery, play, reunion, arrival. The
final arrangement is:

## Why this improves the current project

- The visibly ghosted/double-exposure doorway section around the old
  28–30.5-second area is gone.
- The story is 39.125 seconds instead of roughly 42 seconds, without losing any
  narrative beat.
- Castle and forest now run as one continuous scenic passage rather than two
  mechanically separated shots.
- Repeated cabin and otter coverage was reduced to the strongest setup, action,
  and resolution angles.
- Three transitions are reserved for changes in place or chapter. The remaining
  cuts stay direct, which protects attention and action clarity.
- Sound cues begin slightly before selected picture changes, functioning as
  gentle J-cuts/L-cuts: the island swell leads the first dissolve, exterior
  movement bridges the reunion, and bridge water anticipates the last arrival.
- Downloaded clip audio is excluded. The cut uses a dedicated A1 mix made from
  the accepted music and purpose-authored thematic stems, not repurposed game
  effects.

## Further improvements worth considering

The present cut is the strongest version available from these clips. The only
material next step is performance audio: an owner-approved family line in the
two cabin spaces. That should be recorded and placed without changing the
protected voice originals. If a child viewing shows attention loss, the first
trim should be 0.5–1 second from the second cabin beat—not the otter discovery
or castle arrival.

Avoid adding more dissolves, speed ramps, zooms, or decorative overlays. The
storybook images already carry the spectacle; extra effects would make the
sequence feel like a trailer instead of a welcoming opening.
