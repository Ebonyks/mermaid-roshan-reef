**The governing rule this pass establishes:** a job does not earn its place by producing an object. It earns its place by **meeting a need a guest would otherwise feel**. ## 4. GAPS, REDUNDANCIES, AND THE FIXES

**GAP A — the shared moment has no maker, and that is the point.**
Needs 1–15 are all covered. The sixteenth need — *"the moment we all stop and do the same thing: the candles, the wish, the song"* — is owned by **no job**. It is assembled from the three staff (Phantom's light, Maestro's downbeat, Dragon closing the door so everyone is inside) plus the pop star's microphone. Do not assign it to a job. Make it explicit instead, because **it is exactly what the Ember King steals.** The villain does not take a single thing a child made — he takes the one thing nobody could make alone. Wire it through the shipped five-candle rhyme: the Phantom's five lanterns *are* the five candles. This turns a structural gap into the chapter's strongest beat at zero cost.

**GAP B — "being wanted when nobody ever invited you" cannot be made at all.**
It is not a prop; it is a sentence, and only Roshan can say it. **Plant it in the racer act.** On the delivery run there is one door Harper and Fiona skip: dark, off the road. *"Who lives there?" — "Nobody invites them."* Roshan has one invitation left in her hand at the end of the act. It sits on the party table beside the trophy for the rest of the chapter, and at the climax it is what she hands the Imp Captain. Dialogue only, no new art, and it converts the chapter's thesis from a climax surprise into a five-act promise.

**REDUNDANCY 1 — boxer sash vs detective tiara.** Both were "a thing Roshan wears." Fixed above: tiara = identity, sash = the game prize that passes and ends on the Imp Captain.

**REDUNDANCY 2 — farmer picnic vs chef cake.** Both were "food." Fixed above: chef owns the *one shared ritual food*; farmer owns *everybody gets fed, including the animals*, with Pudding as a guest in a party hat.

**REDUNDANCY 3 — ballerina music box vs Maestro's band.** Fixed above: the box is a dancing *game* that stops when the dancing stops; the Maestro conducts the *song*.

**REDUNDANCY 4 — painter's banner vs astronaut's rocket** (both "makes it look special"). Fixed by timing: the painter owns *the moment you walk in*; the astronaut owns *the moment it ends*.

**THE PARTY MAP — give every piece a named place in the Main Hall.** This is what makes the thirteen read as one party rather than thirteen souvenirs, and it satisfies the review's "build the party venue as a place, not a cutscene."
door → Dragon · lanterns/candles → Phantom · walls → the framed sunrise · head of table → the cake · long table → the picnic, Pudding in a hat · every chair → a party bag · one chair → the mended starfish · corner → the sleepy corner under the stars · stage end → microphone, music box, magician's hat · outside → the rocket · on Roshan → the tiara · going round the room → the sash · in her hand → the last invitation.

# 1. PASTRY CHEF — the cake
`ACTS[0]` · floor 1 · steal at phase 4 (`CAKE CHASE`) · finale from phase 5 · helper **Kareem** (`shop` → `bm_george`, routes today)

### THE PARTY ROLE
**"The one who builds the middle of the table."**

A step past "makes the cake": the cake is **the only object at the whole party that every guest looks at at the same second.** Thirteen pieces get made and twelve of them are used by somebody, one at a time — you wear the tiara, you dance to the box, you take a bag. The cake is the one thing that is *used by everybody simultaneously*, and only once, and only when somebody says now. The pastry chef doesn't make a dessert. She makes **the place the whole room points at**, and — literally — **the flat surface the five candles stand on.** Everything the chapter's climax does (candles, wish, song, the Ember King's puff) happens on top of a surface the child's own finger made in the `TOP` phase.

### THE GUEST WHO NEEDS IT — **Evie**
Canon's line is already shipped in the party beat: *"Lamba wants to sit next to the cake!"* Her specific problem, made personal: **Evie is the guest who is convinced she has already spoiled the birthday.** She brings a present that arrives broken (Act 5), and a four-year-old who thinks she has ruined something spends the party against the wall. She needs a *best seat* — and at a party there is exactly one best seat and it is next to the cake. No cake, no best seat; no best seat, nowhere for Evie to put the one thing she cares about and prove she belongs at the front.

Secondary, and free: **Kareem**. He sells cakes for a living and has never once been at a table where someone sang at one of his.

### THE STAKE — one image
Fourteen guests standing in a ring around a long empty table, and Roshan holding five candles in her fist because there is nothing to stand them in. Nobody knows when to sing, so nobody sings.

Floor-1 posture is **playing** — the shipped scuffle is literally *"Mischief imps grabbed the spoons!"*: they take **tools, never food.** They want their hands on the making. So why does the Captain take the finished cake at `CAKE CHASE`?

Because **a cake is evidence.** It is the one object that proves a party is actually happening and is not just a rumour, and it is the only object that comes with a rule about *who gets the first slice*. Taking it is not taking food — it is taking the question **"whose party is this?"** and carrying it off to see what happens. This is the first moment in the whole chapter the Captain touches a *finished* piece, and he does it out of curiosity, not malice. He is checking whether a real party exists. The shipped steal line already says it: *"Is that for the PARTY? Let me hold it! Are we invited?"* — *hold*, not keep.

# 2. CANDY MAKER — the party bags
`ACTS[3]` · floor 1 · steal at phase 4 (`CANDY CHASE`) · helper **Sparkle** (chirps only, no TTS)

### THE PARTY ROLE
**"The one who counts everybody."**

A step past "nobody goes home empty-handed": **the bag is the only piece of the party that leaves the party.** Every other piece stays in the main hall — the cake gets eaten there, the box plays there, the rocket goes up over it. The candy maker owns **tomorrow**: the proof, the next morning, that last night happened and that you were in it.

And the deeper structural fact, which is what makes this job quietly enormous: **the bags are the only piece that has to be made in a count — one per guest.** Every other piece is one-of-one. That means the party bags are **the guest list rendered as objects.** Fourteen bags and fifteen guests is not a shortage; it is one child who was not thought of. The candy maker is doing the same arithmetic the imps are on the wrong end of, which is why this act sits one act before the Curtain Dragon.

### THE GUEST WHO NEEDS IT — **Sparkle**
Canon's reveal, and it is the best one on floor 1: **she keeps eating the bags because nobody had made her one yet.** The personal problem, stated the way a four-year-old feels it: **Sparkle cannot ask.** She can't talk, she can't count, and she can't read a list, so she has no way to find out whether she is on it. A baby taking what she couldn't request. A bag with her name on it is the only sentence in the room that says *we knew you were coming before you got here.*

### THE STAKE — one image
The door closes behind the last guest and Sparkle is still at the picked-over table, eating the tablecloth, because nothing on it was ever hers.

### WHY THE IMPS TARGET IT
Floor 1, and the escalation ladder gives this act the verb **eat** — the only act on the floor where what they take is genuinely gone. Shipped scuffle: *"We are SO hungry — just one each?"* Shipped steal: the Captain rolls away **the whole cart**, not one bag.

The reason is exact and it is the chapter's thesis in miniature: **a bag is the only party piece there could be a spare of.** One cake, one crown, one trophy, one music box — every other piece is single and obviously not theirs. A cart of bags is the only object in the castle where *"one for everybody"* might accidentally have included them. **They steal the piece with the best odds of having their name on it.** They aren't stealing a party; they're checking whether one each exists. That is floor-1 posture — playing and hungry — and it is why the act's answer is a shrug: it's candy, you make more.

## 2. THE COUNT MOTIF

### FIVE is already taken, and it must stay taken

Five is the most-shipped number in the game and it already means *light*:

| Where | Shipped |
|---|---|
| `opera_career_world_2d.gd` phase 0 of **all thirteen careers** | `"combat": {"count": 5}` |
| `ACTS[1]` detective | `"clues": 5` |
| `ACTS[9]` Shadow Phantom | `"lanterns": 5` |
| `PHASES.nursery[2]` | `"goal": 5.0`, "under **five** falling babies" |
| `PHASES.painter[3]` | "Tap **five** happy splatters!" |
| `ember_fortress.gd:33` | `const LANTERNS := 5` |
| Chapter 2 climax | five birthday candles |

**Ruling: five is the LIGHT register and nothing else. The guest count must not be five.** If five also meant guests, "he took five" would read to a four-year-old as *he took five friends* — the one sentence this chapter must never accidentally say.

### THIRTEEN is the TABLE register

Thirteen is already the shipped table number: thirteen careers, thirteen `GOAL_PROPS` (`opera_career_world_2d.gd:173-187`), thirteen shelf slots in `PARTY_ORDER = [0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8,10,11,12,15,13]`, and the bible's own `"PARTY TABLE   %d / 13"`. It is also — and this is the pass's strongest finding — exactly the number of **distinct named beneficiaries** the party-function taxonomy assigns: Evie, Sparkle, Flower Friend, Huluu, Kareem, the imps, Chuck, the Curtain Dragon, Wacky, Faron, Harper & Fiona, Mewsha, Evie's starfish. Thirteen jobs, thirteen guests served, thirteen places at the table. It is not a coincidence and it should be stated as canon.

### The operator between them: **"and one more"**

The child never counts to thirteen. She counts the leftover. So the motif is not a number, it is an **arithmetic gesture repeated thirteen times**:

> **Every act makes enough — and then one more that nobody uses.**

Thirteen bags and one more. Thirteen plates and one more. Thirteen invitations and one left in her hand (taxonomy Gap B). Thirteen chairs and one empty. Nobody explains it for the whole chapter. At the climax, thirteen leftovers is exactly enough for a crew of imps — `{"count": 8, "captain": true}` = nine, plus stragglers — and the shipped party line **"There's a bag for every one of you"** lands as arithmetic the child has been watching accumulate since act one.

**Free implementation:** the bible's shelf spec is a **7 × 2 grid** (`PartyPiece0..12`, "row 0 → slots 0..6, row 1 → slots 7..12"). That is **fourteen cells for thirteen pieces.** The fourteenth cell already exists in the layout and holds nothing. Put the leftover invitation there, drawn dim from act one, lighting only when Roshan hands it to the Captain. Zero new geometry, zero new art, and the empty chair is on screen for the whole chapter.

## 4. THE ORDER-INDEPENDENCE RULE

**Rooms are freely visitable (§10), so all 13! orderings must be safe. One rule, three clauses, all with shipped precedent.**

> **THE INTERLOCK RULE**
> **(a) Additive only.** A link may only *add* — a visible object, a shortened dwell, a better line, an extra verse. It may never remove, gate, block, or make an act harder. Every act must be completable and comprehensible as act one.
> **(b) Read at the top of the beat that uses it, never at build time.**
> **(c) Every callback ships in two variants — `linked` and `neutral` — chosen by one predicate. The neutral variant is never an apology and never names the missing act; it is the line the act would have had anyway.**

**Clause (a) is already how the shipped code behaves:** `lens_dwell_need = LENS_DWELL * 0.64` only helps; `pipe_queue_depth = 4` only helps; `kart_cfg["vehicles"]` only upgrades; `KITCHEN_RECIPES` shows Pearl Cake always and *adds* Carrot Cake. The guard shape is always the same bare positive test with an implicit no-op else:

```gdscript
if int(m.opera_pantry.get("carrots", 0)) > 0:
```

**Clause (b) is a defect the project has already paid for twice** — `opera_act.gd:2036-2041` ("reading the pantry in `_build_lens()` always read it one beat too early and the lanterns never did anything") and `:2466-2472` ("that mistake cost the pit crew's wheels and the stagehands' lanterns"). In the 2D world this means: the read happens inside `_show_phase()` for the phase that uses it, **not** in `setup()` / `_build_world()`.

### How it reads off the two signals

**Pantry for the WORLD; bitmask for the WORDS.**

- **`m.opera_pantry`** — for world changes that want a *count* or that were produced mid-act. Written only when an act actually reaches its gift beat.
- **`m.opera_stars & (1 << PRODUCER_INDEX)`** — for dialogue. This is the authoritative "she finished that act" test (set once, at `opera_house.gd:658`), it needs no write, no new key and no migration, and the codebase already reads it this way in `_floor_unlocked` (`:547-555`), `_floor_shows_starred` (`:557-563`), `OperaLobby2D._update_guide` (`:345-353`) and the bible's `_refresh_party_table`.

### Where the predicate lives

`OperaStory.fire(m, trigger, config, ...)` already receives `m`, so `lines_for()` gains the same parameter and reads **only** `m.opera_stars` (already read-only in `_story_replay()`) and `m.opera_pantry` (already read in `try_chapter_open`). **The satellite rule (`CHAPTER2_BIBLE_SYSTEMS` §1.4) is preserved exactly — no new surface on `m`.**

Encode links as a **diff on the neutral array**, so nothing can go missing:

```gdscript
STEAL: [
    {"who": "Roshan", "hold": 2.6, "vo": "st_chef_steal_1",
     "text": "He's taking your birthday cake to the STAGE! After him!"},
],
STEAL + "@LINK": {
    "needs": 7,          # act index 7 = boxer; tested against m.opera_stars
    "swap": 0,           # replace element 0 — survives the replay slice
    "line": {"who": "Roshan", "hold": 2.8, "vo": "st_chef_steal_1b",
             "text": "He's wearing my sash… fine. He can KEEP it — if he wins."},
},
```

`lines_for()` copies the neutral array, and if `needs` is starred, overwrites `swap`. A missing `@LINK` key means no link. **A missing producer is not a special case — it is the default path.** Nothing else in the codebase has to know a link exists.

`cfg["act_index"] = i` (bible §2.1, `opera_house.gd:625-626`) is the enabler; `config` is `duplicate()`d and nothing iterates it, so adding keys is inert (bible §7.5). `probe_opera_2d.gd` never sets `act_index`, so it defaults to `-1` and always exercises the full neutral path — which is the right regression target.

## 5. THE PARTY TABLE AS THE PROOF

The bible's shelf (`CHAPTER2_BIBLE_SYSTEMS` §4) fills **left-to-right in earn order** via `PARTY_ORDER = [0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8,10,11,12,15,13]`. That is correct as a *counter* — it is the child's progress bar — but it is the wrong order for showing that the pieces belong to each other.

**Ruling: keep `PARTY_ORDER` for the fill; re-sort into the PARTY MAP at the climax.** One `for` loop of position tweens, ~2 seconds, zero new art. Pieces that depend on each other end up **touching**, and the child sees thirteen souvenirs become one room.

### The map — what sits next to what, and why

| Place on the shelf | Pieces | The interlock it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Head of the table** | `goal_chef` with `goal_painter` directly behind/above it | **L5.** The painting is the cake's backdrop. The five candles stand on the cake — this is the exact frame the Ember King attacks. |
| **Beside the cake** | `goal_racer` + the **14th cell**, the leftover invitation | **L1/L2/Gap B.** "Everybody came" sits next to "the one who was never asked." |
| **Down the long table** | `goal_farmer` then `goal_candymaker`, adjacent | **L1 + L2 + L3.** Both were counted off the same guest list, so they read as one row. Pudding is in a party hat, a guest, never food. |
| **One chair** | `goal_doctor` with `goal_magician` (the hat, Lamba inside) beside it | **L7.** The mend earned the trick. The mended starfish has its own chair with its own party hat — the guest who would have been left in a drawer. |
| **The quiet corner** | `goal_nursery`, with `goal_astronaut` placed at the **far outside edge** | **L8.** The loudest thing was physically moved away from the quietest. Distance on the shelf *is* the link. |
| **Stage end** | `goal_popstar` and `goal_ballerina`, adjacent | **L9.** The mic carries the tune the music box taught. |

### The two exceptions that carry the chapter

1. **`goal_detective` and `goal_boxer` leave the shelf.** Eleven pieces are furniture; the tiara is *who this is for* and the sash is *the invitation made physical*. Two tweens, and the shelf stops being a checklist.
2. **The nursery slot does not dim.** The bible's crash spec tweens every slot to 35% (`CHAPTER2_BIBLE_ARC` §5). Per taxonomy decision 7, exempt `goal_nursery`: **one `modulate` exception on one slot.** The last image of Chapter 2 is a dark table with a cake that has no candles — and one corner still glowing, because the least legible job in the whole chapter turns out to own the only light the Ember King could not take.

That single exception is worth more than any new asset in the chapter: it pays off the rank-12 job, it hands the shy Phantom's light motif to the nursery, and it is the frame the child carries into Chapter 3.
